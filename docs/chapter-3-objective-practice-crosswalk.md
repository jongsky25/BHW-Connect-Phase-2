# Chapter III objective–practice–indicator crosswalk

**Planning draft • 29 September 2026 • GitHub `main` 8b0e91a.** This is curriculum design, not approved clinical teaching material or a published course. No live system was queried or changed. Companion: [Chapter III incorporation plan](chapter-3-incorporation-plan.md).

## How to read this map

`R` = one-based PDF page in the supplied *BHW REFERENCE MANUAL.pdf*; `F` = one-based PDF page in *FACILITATOR GUIDE-Oct11 (1).pdf*. IDs below are **proposed lesson boundaries**, not existing repository IDs or headings in the 2022 sources. Each row specifies an observable outcome, the practice that would make it visible, and an indicator to put in the private facilitator rubric. Clinical facts, thresholds, schedules, service entitlements, and local routes are deliberately omitted where they need current review. “Select/flag/refer” means applying an approved, locally supplied protocol, not independent diagnosis.

**Planning count: 65 short lessons in 12 units.** This count is provisional until the concept inventory and BHW/facilitator task review are accepted. The course owner's planning total is **392 facilitated hours**: 8 for primary care/navigation and HEPO introduction (3.1–3.2); 144 for the seven priority areas (3.3–3.9); 72 for monitoring (3.10); 96 for records (3.11); 72 for BHS equipment/supplies (3.12). Source F45 prints 384 because it lists monitoring as 64, while F101's detailed monitoring allocation is 72. The hours describe a source training program, not 392 hours of phone reading or automatic certification. F50 splits the priority-area 144 hours as 40/32/20/12/12/16/12. F101 splits monitoring 72 as 8 teaching + 16 return demonstration + 48 community practice. F103 splits records 96 as 24 orientation + 72 field demonstration. F106 splits equipment 72 across five activities. These allocations need a feasible facilitator/field timetable and a formal source-discrepancy disposition before a public training-hours claim.

## Complete design example A — 3.1.3 Referral and handover

**Role and outcome.** A resident asks whether a service is available at the BHS. The local directory is incomplete. Given the current verified directory, referral form and a fictional resident case, the BHW identifies what needs confirmation, contacts or refers to the responsible provider, explains the next step to the resident, and documents a private follow-up without promising a service. Source basis R63–65, F45–46. This is a design example, not approved lesson text.

**Proposed seven-section learning sequence:** (1) resident request and what is known; (2) BHS versus PCF/RHU roles; (3) life-stage need; (4) check the local directory and mark unknowns; (5) ask permission and contact the appropriate provider; (6) concise factual handover; (7) confirm who will follow up and when. Read and Slides should teach the same decisions with separately authored wording. Checks after sections 3 and 6 should require a decision and explain every option; the final section should ask the learner to reconstruct the referral plan.

**Practice kit:** fictional resident card, service-directory card with one deliberately unknown entry, referral/handover form, BHW card, midwife card, observer checklist, and a private answer key. Roles rotate. The resident has a need that may exceed BHS capacity; the BHW must not infer availability from the manual's general service list. A second round changes the directory entry to test adaptation.

**Observation anchors:**

| Critical action | Kaya na | Kailangan ng practice | Hindi pa |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identify need and urgency | Correctly states need and checks urgency using approved local criteria. | Identifies the need but omits a relevant clarification. | Chooses a destination before assessing the need or misses a supplied urgent cue. |
| Confirm service and scope | Uses verified directory, marks unknown, and contacts the responsible provider. | Uses the right provider but does not explicitly confirm one uncertain detail. | Promises an unverified service or offers care beyond role. |
| Explain and hand over | Gains consent, gives resident a concrete next step, and passes concise factual details privately. | Gives a workable next step but misses owner/time or one handover fact. | Shares private details openly or leaves the resident without a clear route. |
| Close the loop | Records follow-up owner/time and checks resident understanding. | Records a follow-up but does not confirm understanding. | No follow-up plan. |

**Feedback and transfer:** Facilitator gives one strength, one correction and a repeat attempt. A one-page job aid contains “need → urgency → verify service → ask permission → hand over → follow up,” plus blank local-directory fields. Workplace transfer is a supervised directory check and one de-identified referral review with the midwife. No real resident details go into the lesson package.

**Review dependencies:** Current service directory, local emergency/referral protocol, who may access the form, and a locally approved handover template. The role-play cannot be a certificate of independent clinical triage.

## Complete design example B — 3.10.1 BP measurement and handover

**Role and outcome.** Given a checked training BP device, a volunteer or training partner, a current approved measurement checklist, a blank synthetic record and a supervisor, the BHW prepares the person, takes a reading with the approved method, records it with units/time/device, communicates it accurately, and follows the current escalation pathway. Source basis R120–122 and F101–102. The 2022 manual's numeric categories and step timings are **review items**, not fixed instructions for this sample.

**Proposed seven-section learning sequence:** (1) why the reading is requested and scope; (2) equipment/size/condition check; (3) person preparation and positioning under approved protocol; (4) measurement and repeat/confirmation rules from approved protocol; (5) accurate record with units and context; (6) communicate the result and apply escalation decision; (7) clean/store equipment and hand over. Place a decision check after preparation and another after recording. A final case uses a supplied protocol chart rather than requiring memorized thresholds.

**Practice kit:** approved and deliberately faulty training devices, different cuffs where available, current measurement checklist, timer, synthetic observation form, two case cards (routine and escalation), observer sheet and private answer key. Facilitate one demonstration, individual return demonstrations and feedback/retry. The F101 8/16/48-hour training pattern means the phone lesson prepares for, but cannot replace, coached practice and community observation.

**Observation anchors:**

| Critical action | Kaya na | Kailangan ng practice | Hindi pa |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prepare and check | Confirms identity/consent, correct approved equipment and required preparation. | Performs main checks but misses a noncritical setup detail. | Uses unsuitable/faulty equipment or omits a critical preparation step. |
| Measure | Follows every critical action on the approved protocol and obtains a readable result. | Needs one prompt or repeat for a noncritical action. | Cannot complete the approved sequence safely or reliably. |
| Record | Documents actual value, units, time, method/device and any required repeat. | Result is accurate but one contextual field is missing. | Alters, guesses or omits the result or units. |
| Communicate and refer | Gives the value without diagnosis and takes the correct protocol-based next step. | Escalation choice is right but handover lacks one relevant fact. | Reassures or treats without basis, or misses a supplied escalation trigger. |

**Feedback and transfer:** Repeat the full procedure after feedback, then compare observer ratings for the critical steps. Job aid is the approved device-specific checklist and record example, not a copied 2022 threshold table. Workplace transfer is supervised measurement and record review; the supervisor signs the observed result separately from app lesson completion.

**Review dependencies:** Qualified clinical reviewer must settle current BP technique, rest/repeat rules, device maintenance, age and pregnancy scope, threshold/urgent referral rules, and acceptable device types. The actual assessment checklist is versioned to that protocol. The app must not score unsupervised digital completion as measured BP competence.

## Traceability and unresolved decisions

- Each proposed lesson ID must map to one or more versioned source concepts; the `coverage` record must map back from concept to lesson, check, practice, observer criterion, and any exclusion. The rows above are the first editorial crosswalk, not final concept-level IDs.
- Review the image-only/source tables before content authoring: R73 feeding guide, R79–80 cancer charts, R97 vaccine schedule, R108 family-planning chart, R121 BP categories, R125–130 growth/BMI figures, R142–143 equipment list, and F45/F50/F101/F103/F106 hour tables. Many carry dates, percentages or cut-offs and should be reauthored from approved current sources.
- F106's final 24-hour row says “maintaining the aneroid sphygmomanometer” but describes medicine stock cards and medicine-room organization. This map treats those activities as medicine supply management pending formal correction.
- Determine local program relevance for typhoid, malaria, leprosy, filariasis and schistosomiasis as F78 recommends, while retaining a crosswalk disposition for every manual topic.
- Determine whether the proposed 65 boundaries are too many for usable navigation through a BHW/facilitator walkthrough. Merge only when the same observable task can still be taught, practiced and assessed clearly.
- Follow repository content format, private-guide separation, bilingual parity, and draft isolation from Chapter II. No Chapter III package, database row, production URL, or live learner data is required for this planning crosswalk.
