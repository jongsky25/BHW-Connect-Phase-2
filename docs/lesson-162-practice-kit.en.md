# 1.6.2 · Gibs / Liza · Fictional practice kit

## BHW role card

You are Gibs, a fictional male BHW. The purpose, language and comfortable place are already agreed. Start with “What happened?” Ask one relevant neutral clarification, allow an answer or correction, and confirm the corrected summary. Do not see the resident’s private card before the round.
Round 1: Liza says “I have nobody with me.”
Round 2: Liza says “The afternoon did not work.”
Round 3: A neighbor says “She does not want to go”; Liza has not given her account. Ask Liza directly without adopting the neighbor’s claim.
Offer to check current service hours only if Liza agrees. Service hours, childcare, transport and visit dates are unconfirmed. Never invent them.

## Liza role card

You are fictional Liza, 29, yellow blouse and blue skirt. Do not volunteer all facts at once. Correct assumptions politely.
Round 1: “I have nobody with me” means someone to watch your child, not a travel companion. Your sister was away that day. Correct “always” to “only when my sister is away.”
Round 2: “The afternoon did not work” means that particular afternoon because you were watching your child. It does not mean every afternoon. You do not remember the exact date; keep it unknown.
Round 3: The neighbor’s claim is not your explanation. You did not know the current service hours and want to check them first. You have not decided a visit date.
In each round, confirm the corrected summary only when it matches your account. Agree to checking hours first; no appointment or childcare is promised.

## Observer role card

Record exact words, not motives or a grade. Rotate roles A/B/C through BHW/Liza/observer so everyone practices once. Each round: 1 minute cards, 4 conversation, 2 feedback, 3 retry.
Round 1 observe meaning; round 2 time/uncertainty; round 3 source distinction.
Feedback: “You asked __. Liza corrected __. Your next summary said __. Try __.” Repeat the part needing support. This is peer feedback; only directly observed performance can inform a facilitator assessment.

## One-page question aid

1. Invite: “What happened?” Allow the story.
2. Clarify one detail: “What do you mean by __?” or “Was that only on that day?” Do not ask three questions at once. Yes/no can confirm a detail.
3. Summarize as a question: “Have I understood correctly: __?”
4. Accept correction: “Thank you. So __. Is that right?”
5. Confirm a useful next step: “Would you like us to check the current service hours?” Keep unconfirmed dates and services unknown.
Avoid “You just do not want to?”, always, invented motives, promises of childcare/transport/appointments and unnecessary private detail.
Alone: say both roles aloud, then check the sequence. This is rehearsal; arrange later direct observation. At the next authorized visit, clarify one ambiguous answer before recording it. Follow local policy; urgent help is not delayed by a routine interview.
