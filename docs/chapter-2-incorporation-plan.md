# BHW Connect Phase 2: Chapter 2 incorporation plan

Prepared 25 September 2026. Planning deliverable; no application, repository, database, or publication changes have been made.

## 1. Recommended approach

Build **Chapter 2: Ang BHW Bilang First Responder** using the same Chapter → Subchapter → short Lesson experience established in Chapter 1. Use the Reference Manual for curriculum scope, the Facilitator Guide for competencies and practical training, and the Day 1 Part 2 presentation as a teaching-resource and coverage cross-check.

The proposed structure is **seven subchapters and 55 short lessons**, mapped to the guide's **four common competencies**. Seven subchapters and 55 lessons are instructional design choices, not printed manual headings or additional TESDA units. Final lesson boundaries can change during storyboarding without losing source coverage.

The new deck is not a complete Chapter 2 curriculum by itself. In particular, community mobilization is largely a heading in the deck, and several first-aid topics are named without being taught in detail. Restore their coverage from the manuals.

### How to apply the proposed capacity-building standard

The repository still labels this standard **proposed**. This plan uses its instructional criteria as the Chapter 2 design target in response to the request to carry standards forward. It does not claim every rule is already adopted across the app or automatically enforced.

Use the following numerical targets as explicit review checks: Filipino Read sections up to 80 words; at least 90% of sentences up to 20 words and none over 30; Slides up to four lines of 70 characters and 280 characters total; checks every two to three sections, with a final scenario decision and at least three plausible options; a facilitator run sheet up to 150 words and full lesson guide up to 800 words. Preserve necessary clinical qualifications by splitting sections instead of deleting them.

Design observations with specific Kaya na / Kailangan practice / Hindi pa descriptions, activity setting, group size, sampling, feedback and another attempt. Align guides and recorded indicators through one canonical definition. Design parallel pre/post forms with objective tags and predominantly application questions; evaluate distractors and answer-position balance. Confirm appropriate item counts, timing and cut-score method when the assessment blueprint is finalized.

The proposed standard also calls for task validation with BHWs and supervisors, independent accuracy/assessment review, BHW read-aloud testing, pilot item analysis and rater calibration, and later transfer evaluation. Put these in the implementation review schedule; do not invent completed reviews or signatures. Automatic linting, claim registries, offline caching, timed recall, and new certification rules need explicit implementation and testing. Do not silently retrofit Chapter I certification.

Keep self-study time, facilitated practice time, field practice, and the source training allocation separate. The rule that a facilitated timetable should reconcile with its approved allocation does not mean digital reading minutes alone must equal that allocation.

### Proposed lesson boundaries

**2.1 — Four lessons**

1. Identify the concern, prepare a response and recognize when support is needed.
2. Listen, clarify and respond calmly in an appropriate setting.
3. Agree on a next step while respecting the resident's decision.
4. Report relevant facts privately and review one's response.

**2.2 — Four lessons**

1. Apply effective, safe and people-centered service to a barangay case.
2. Protect privacy and provide respectful, inclusive service.
3. Apply timeliness, equity, integration and efficient use of resources.
4. Use 7S, review a work area and plan a specific improvement.

**2.3 — Seven lessons**

1. Identify how infection can spread in a household or BHS scene.
2. Choose controls at the source, environment, work-practice and protective-equipment levels.
3. Demonstrate hand hygiene using the reviewed protocol.
4. Demonstrate cough etiquette and explain it to a resident.
5. Identify food, water and sanitation actions that help prevent diarrhea.
6. Locate BHS IPC policies and distinguish BHW support from staff procedures requiring training.
7. Use a checklist to report a gap, propose a feasible improvement and follow it up.

**2.4 — Eighteen lessons**

1. Scene safety, help activation, initial assessment and referral pathway.
2. Childhood danger-sign recognition and communication.
3. Cough and breathing concerns in children.
4. Diarrhea, dehydration concerns and reviewed ORS education.
5. Convulsion recognition, response boundaries and escalation.
6. Shock, fainting and loss of consciousness: recognizing urgency and seeking help.
7. CPR/BLS orientation and supervised skills-practice pathway.
8. Drowning: safe response and referral boundaries.
9. Poisoning: information gathering and appropriate emergency contact.
10. Wounds and bleeding.
11. Suspected fractures and dislocations.
12. Insect bites, stings and signs requiring escalation.
13. Snakebite response and referral.
14. Burns and escalation.
15. Choking and supervised practice using the appropriate training equipment.
16. Chest pain and emergency referral.
17. First-aid kit readiness, contents, storage and replenishment checks.
18. Integrated case: communicate the incident and hand over to the health team.

These are authoring scopes, not approved treatment algorithms. First-aid scripts, age distinctions, measurements, medicines and escalation rules require current primary-source and qualified clinical review before learners receive them.

**2.5 — Twelve lessons**

One introduction on scope, reliable identification and consultation; ten individually identifiable plant lessons; one integrated practice lesson. The ten plants are akapulko, ampalaya, bawang, bayabas, lagundi, niyog-niyogan, sambong, tsaang gubat, ulasimang bato/pansit-pansitan and yerba buena.

Use the same short card structure for each: verified identity and plant part, reviewed purpose, limitations, preparation only where verified, relevant restrictions, and consultation/referral message. Keep age-specific preparation tables readable in both modes. Do not use an AI-generated plant image as botanical identification evidence. Do not design practice that requires learners to ingest a preparation.

**2.6 — Four lessons**

1. Identify the community issue and people affected.
2. Invite relevant stakeholders and address barriers to participation.
3. Build trust, facilitate a participatory meeting and form or work with a core group.
4. Agree on responsibilities, resources, follow-up and a way to review results.

**2.7 — Six lessons**

1. Identify local hazards, vulnerable households and available capacities.
2. Explain locally verified evacuation arrangements, warning channels and contacts.
3. Prepare and explain a go-bag/e-balde using reviewed local guidance.
4. Explain the BHW/BHERT role and coordinate with the local response team.
5. Match needs to medical/public health, nutrition, WASH and mental-health/psychosocial services.
6. Complete a disaster tabletop and plan continuity, referral, follow-up and support for responders.

## 4. Training hours and competency mapping

F32 lists four common competencies totaling **42 hours**: difficult/challenging behavior 10; quality patient/client service 10; IPC 10; basic first aid 12. These are the supplied guide's allocations, not a claim of independently verified current TESDA requirements.

F38 divides the 12-hour block into **7 hours emergency techniques + 1 hour first-aid kit + 2 hours medicinal plants + 2 hours community organizing and disaster risk reduction together**. Map 2.4 to the first eight hours, 2.5 to two hours, and 2.6–2.7 to the shared two-hour allocation. Do not allocate an additional 12 hours to each of these subchapters or count shared activities twice.

F35–36 divides IPC into infection 1 hour; transmission 2; control levels 1; handwashing 2; cough etiquette 2; diarrhea prevention 2. Preserve this source map while making room for policy/checklist work in the actual session design.

The breadth of the source does not prove these hours are sufficient for every learner's practical proficiency. The training design must specify rotations, trainer availability, field practice, additional/remedial sessions and the applicable approved skills-training requirements. Do not promise first-aid/BLS qualification through short digital lessons.

Produce a day-by-day training grid with sessions, breaks, recap, assessment, practice and assigned field tasks after reconciling current requirements. Estimate digital time from final text/narration. Preserve all three time fields separately in the course data and facilitator plan.

## 6. Assessment, transfer and privacy

Map each objective to a named practice and specific visible actions. Example for 2.1: in a resident-refusal role-play, the BHW asks about the concern, summarizes it accurately, gives a respectful explanation, agrees or records an appropriate next step, and shares only necessary facts with the appropriate team member. Define the ratings against these actions and prompting required, not against confidence or attendance.

Use existing rating labels, but create task-specific descriptors. Keep online completion, attendance, activity participation, observed competence and formal assessment distinct. Record the observer, time, setting and evidence actually observed; support re-observation without overwriting earlier history.

Draft a Chapter 2 pre/post-test blueprint tied to published lessons and the four competency groups. Keep formative checks ungraded. Build a separate practical-observation blueprint. Do not change Chapter I questions or certification thresholds merely to add Chapter 2. Decide and implement the Chapter 2 delivery-course/assessment relationship explicitly before activation; course completion must not imply a whole-manual or BLS certificate.

Every subchapter includes a compact job aid, one workplace application task, three to five recall scenarios, and a supervisor observation prompt. Proposed follow-up intervals are +2 days, +2 weeks and +2 months, with workplace confirmation in 2–4 weeks. These are content/scheduling specifications; no reminders are created by this plan. Use safe workplace tasks such as checking a contact directory, reviewing a kit or preparing a meeting, rather than requiring an actual emergency or treatment encounter.

Use fictional patient data in learning material and practice forms. Do not put identifiable resident case details into ordinary free-text training notes. Keep private rubrics and answer keys protected in web, export and offline variants.
