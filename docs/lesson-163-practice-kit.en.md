# 1.6.3 · Gibs · Fictional practice kit

## Role card 1 · Jo · family planning

Jo, 24: “I heard something frightening. I do not want someone choosing for me.” You need not share private details. First teach-back: “Will you choose a method for me?” After repair, explain the first step and what is unconfirmed in your own words.

BHW/Gibs: acknowledge the concern and voluntary choice. Offer to ask the health team about a qualified contact and private conversation arrangements. Name/hours are unknown on the card. No method selection, dose, appointment promise or forced disclosure. Ask for an explanation in the person’s own words; repair and recheck.

Observer: record a respectful phrase and a confusing phrase. The repair is “Find out the contact first; I will not choose your method.” End with Jo’s own explanation, not a nod. Give practice feedback only.

## Role card 2 · Ben · distress

Ben, 37: “I have felt low for weeks. I want someone to talk to, but I do not know who.” You may decline details. The fictional card contains no immediate-danger cue. First teach-back: “Do I have an appointment now?” On recheck, explain the actual step; availability remains unknown.

BHW/Gibs: listen without diagnosing. “If you want, I will ask the health team about a qualified contact.” Name/hours are unknown. Asking is not an appointment. Use an own-words question, repair and recheck. No treatment or secrecy promise. If real immediate danger arises during the session, seek help at once under verified local procedures; do not rely on this fictional card or an ordinary interview.

Observer: record the concern, uncertainty, repair and own-words reply. Silence or posture is not a diagnosis. Practice feedback only.

## Role card 3 · Ramon · smoking

Ramon, 45: “I want to cut down, but it is hard when I am stressed. I do not want a lecture.” You may decline discussion. First teach-back: “Will you give me medicine?” On recheck, explain the first step and who should discuss medication.

BHW/Gibs: ask permission and hear the goal/barrier. Offer to ask the health team about a qualified contact and support actually available. Contact/availability are unknown. “I will not give or choose medicine.” Ask for the step, repair misunderstanding and ask again. No dose, promised outcome or invented service.

Observer: identify the respectful phrase, actual misconception and repair. End with “Find out the contact; medication is a discussion with the provider,” in the person’s own words. Practice feedback only.

## One-page task aid · explain → ask → repair → recheck

1. Ask permission and hear the concern: “What worries you?”
2. Acknowledge without blame: “Your concern matters.”
3. One plain authorized step: identify a qualified contact. State what is unconfirmed.
4. “To check that I explained clearly, how would you describe the first step in your own words?”
5. If the meaning differs: “My explanation was unclear. First we will…” Use different plain words.
6. “What will we do first?” Hear the person’s own answer and confirm respectfully.

A nod alone is not teach-back. No diagnosis, treatment, dose, appointment guarantee or forced disclosure. Before an actual visit, verify the local contact, authorized message, privacy and emergency procedures. In immediate danger, do not delay help for a routine interview/report.
