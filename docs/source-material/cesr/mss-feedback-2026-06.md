## Objectives

1. To monitor the City/Municipal Epidemiology and Surveillance Units (C/MESU) of Palawan and
   Marinduque with respect to:
   - Availability of CESR resources and tools
   - Capacity strengthening
   - Signal detection and screening
   - Event verification and reporting
   - Timeliness of signal detection and notification, and event detection, notification and response
   - Data quality
   - Challenges encountered

2. To monitor select Barangay Health Stations (BHS) of Palawan and Marinduque and Barangay Health
   Centers (BHC) of Quezon City with respect to:
   - Availability of CESR resources and tools
   - Signal detection, confirmation, recording, and reporting

- Accomplishment of the ESR/CESR Supportive Supervision Tool and the Barangay Health Station
  Supervisory Checklist
- Health Event Reviews
- Collect baseline data of case-based surveillance (Measles, Acute Flaccid Paralysis, Watery
  Diarrhea) and event-based surveillance
- M&E data baselining

Key M&SS items tracked per municipality: *ESR/CESR policy or guidance documents; Signal
Logbook/Database; CESR IEC; Roster of KIs.*

## Event review 1 — Confirmed rabies in dog, Barangay Malibago, Torrijos, Marinduque

**Description.** On 20 March 2026, 3:00–4:00 PM, a dog with unprovoked aggressiveness and aimless
running bit 4 individuals. The **Barangay Kagawad on Health, acting as a key informant, reported the
signal to the BHW.** Type of ownership: stray dog. With the assistance of the Provincial Veterinary
Office, the dog tested positive on Rabies Antigen and RADDL DFAT rabies confirmatory testing.

| Age / sex | Anatomical site of bite |
|---|---|
| 8-year-old / female | Buttocks area |
| 6-year-old / male | Left side of the trunk area |
| 9-year-old / male | Multiple bites in the eyes, legs and shoulders |
| 12-year-old / male | Trunk with scratches on the arms |

**Key findings.**
- 4 human bite victims in the same barangay.
- Cases were immediately identified and brought to the Animal Bite Treatment Clinic of the RHU Main
  in Torrijos. Two of the victims are Category III — deep punctured wounds in highly innervated
  areas.
- **There was no supply of Equine Rabies Immunoglobulin (ERIG) in Torrijos RHU.** Cases were
  referred to Boac ABTC for ERIG but were not able to go to Boac due to the distance. Anti-rabies
  vaccine was completed at Torrijos RHU with no ERIG.

**Actions taken.**
- Immediate identification and medical management of animal bite victims
- Immediate report to Provincial Veterinary Office (PVET)
- Profiling of dogs and cats in Barangay Malibago conducted by the barangay
- Ring vaccination conducted by PVET
- Community assembly on rabies prevention in both humans and animals
- Presentation of rabies response to the Local Health Board
- Field investigation, contact tracing, and records review initiated immediately after notification
- Immunization registries reviewed to identify missed children

|---|---|---|---|

| Notification | 1 day | 1 day | Yes |
| Response | 7 days | 3 days | Yes |

- **Date of emergence — 20 March 2026.** A dog with unprovoked aggressiveness and aimless running
  bit 4 individuals around 3:00–4:00 PM.
- **Date of detection — 20 March 2026.** Signal reported by Barangay Kagawad on Health to the BHW.
  Recorded to the CESR logbook and the midwife, and subsequently reported to Torrijos MESU.
- **Date of notification — 20 March 2026.** Signal received by Torrijos MESU at 6:00 PM.
- **Date of early response completion — 23 March 2026.** Deployment of the rapid response team,

## Event review 2 — Clustering of influenza-like illness in a national high school, Sta. Cruz, Marinduque

**Description.** The school principal of a public school reported to a BHW of Landy BHS an
increasing number of students and teachers with fever, cough and colds. The signal was reported by
the BHW to MESU Sta. Cruz.

- Onset of illness: 12 October 2025, reported as a group of Grade 7 students.
- **128 cases** total. Most female (76, 59%), male (52, 41%). Age range 11–54 years (median 15). No
  cases hospitalized.
- 124 (97%) students, 4 (3%) teaching personnel. Grade 7 (43, 34%) had the highest number, followed
  by Grade 11 (27, 21%) and Grade 12 (25, 20%).

**Key findings.**
- One of the key informants of the BHW of Landy BHS is the principal and school staff of the
  national high school.
- Case identification challenges: mild cases may go unnoticed and unreported; non-specific symptoms
  (fever, cough, colds) overlap with other common illnesses; students attend school despite illness
  due to academic pressure or lack of awareness.
- Absence of specimen collection kits (e.g. swabs, viral transport media) according to MESU.

**Actions taken.**
- Contact tracing and active case finding
- School and household visits
- Suspension of face-to-face classes and mandatory wearing of face masks through the advisory and
  letter from the Municipal Government of Sta. Cruz
- School disinfection
- Flu prevention IEC through social media posting and radio announcement highlighting flu
  transmission, common signs and symptoms, respiratory etiquette, hand hygiene and staying home when
  sick

| Stage | Target | Actual | Met? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Detection | 7 days | **8 days** | **No** |
| Notification | 1 day | 1 day | Yes |
| Response | 7 days | 3 days | Yes |

- **Date of emergence — 12 October 2025.** Reported cluster of cough and colds of Grade 7 students.
- **Date of detection — 20 October 2025.** School principal reported the signal to the BHW. BHW
  recorded the signal in the CESR logbook and reported it to the midwife. Verification conducted by
  both midwife and MESU.
- **Date of notification — 20 October 2025.**
- **Date of early response completion — 23 October 2025.** Deployment of rapid response team, risk

# CESR M&E indicators

## Proposed CESR M&E metrics

| Category | Metric | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| **Coverage** | Participation / reporting rate | % of barangays trained in CESR that are actively reporting; % of trained CHWs actively reporting per barangay |
| **Data quality** | Completeness / accuracy | % of reports with all fields completed (completeness); % of reports with correct details post-validation (accuracy) |
| **Effectiveness** | Confirmation rate (signal quality) | % of reported signals that lead to verified health events. N: number of verified health events. D: number of reported signals |
| **Effectiveness** | Detection capacity (sensitivity) | % of true events / total actual events. N: number of verified events through CESR. D: total number of health events in community |
| **Timeliness** | Detect / Notify / Respond | Detect: time from onset to detection. Notify: time from detection to reporting. Respond: time from reporting to response. Measured as mean/median time and/or as % of total signals / confirmed health events |
| **Outcome** | Outbreak detection | Number and % of health events first detected through CESR |
| **Outcome** | 7-1-7 | % of confirmed health events detected through CESR falling within 7-1-7 timelines |
| **Outcome** | Outbreak response | % of verified health events with completed response |
| **Impact** | Lives saved | Number of lives saved through CESR (measured through number of deaths/cases prevented) |

# Key findings

| Core surveillance activity | Findings |
|---|---|
| **Detection** | Animal and agricultural sector are not fully integrated as key informants. Signal detection and reporting requires further integration into standard Barangay Health Station workflows (**BHWs demonstrate gaps in recalling signal definitions**). Key informants are in a different notebook and have a different template. |
| **Registration** | Same format but different approaches were used in signal recording in the CESR logbook. |
| **Reporting** | As a form of reporting, **pictures of the logbook with recorded signals were sent via Messenger** in <1 day upon verification of the midwife. |
| **Confirmation** | Signals that are health events were immediately verified by MESU (1–2 days). **Currently there is no active surveillance conducted by MESU to check for BHS with no signal reports** as part of their monitoring and supervision — except Torrijos MESU, which conducts monitoring visits to BHS. |
| **Data management** | Signals are not consolidated at the MESU level, **except for Quezon City which uses a digital tool from BHS to CESU.** |
| **Analysis** | Signals are consolidated at the MESU level, but **these are pictures and have no database.** Marinduque PESU created a Google Sheet where MESU encode signals but this is not regularly done (**8 entries**). |
| **Feedback** | No feedback is done except on signals that were health events. Signal reporting is not translated to a surveillance report and distributed to stakeholders. |

# Recommendations

| Aspect | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Implementation protocol** | Training for midwives. KI orientation. |
| **Reporting** | Reporting template per BHS to MESU **with zero reporting (every Friday)**. Tag BHS with **3 consecutive weeks of no reports as "silent" BHS**. MESU shall conduct visits to BHS tagged as "silent" or with 3 consecutive zero reports. |
| **Analysis** | MESU will consolidate signals and encode in the CESR Database, and have an **automated dashboard with simple data visualization that can generate a surveillance report.** |
| **Data management** | Landscape signal logbook format. |
| **Feedback** | CESR Signal Surveillance Report distributed to disease surveillance stakeholders. **Signal Health Alert and Report (SHARE)** — loop of e-mail to MHO, Mayor, Councilor on Health, HRMO, Planning, MDRRMO, DILG. Duration: weekly or monthly. |
| **M&E indicators** | Coverage: number & % of barangays actively recording signals; number & % of trained CHWs actively recording signals; detection capacity. |

Deliverables shown: a proposed CESR logbook, a MESU/PESU signal dashboard, and a feedback email
report.

# Recommendation: CESR Signal Application

> **This is the explicit ask for a digital product.**

| Dimension | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Application access and installation** | PESU, MESU, Midwives, BHW |
| **Device app can be installed on** | Smartphone, Tablet, Computer |
| **Variables** | Same as the CESR logbook |
| **Online / Offline** | Online version |
| **Reporting** | Real-time reporting |
| **Period of analysis** | Weekly / Monthly |
| **Other features** | Database is downloadable (Excel file) |

**Three named components:**

1. **Enter Signal Data (Form)** — allows electronic entry and submission of a signal that will be
   stored in an online spreadsheet.
2. **Downloadable File** — file is downloadable in MS Excel format to the level of the BHS, MESU,
   PESU and EB.
3. **Dashboard** — provides summary and visualization of data. Includes reported signals that were
   true health events, and insights on disease trends to assist decision-making.

**Stated benefits:**
- Organized data collection
- Early detection and reporting of signals
- Automated analysis per Municipality/City
- Trigger immediate response
- Privacy and confidentiality

> We recommend institutionalizing regular CESR reports and establishing a Signal Health Alert and
> Report, or SHARE mechanism, that can provide periodic updates to local government health officers
> and program managers, and other stakeholders.

# Conclusions

- CESR brings One Health and surveillance principles close to the frontlines.
- **Institutionalizing CESR in both local surveillance processes and health staff workflows is
  essential to quality implementation.**
- Continuous and structured monitoring and supportive supervision activities are critical and
  necessary to quality CESR implementation.
- Conducting health event reviews provides significant insight into response quality and its
  contributing factors.
- In detection, a key observation was that some important sectors — particularly agriculture and
  fisheries — are not yet fully integrated into the CESR network despite their critical role in One
  Health surveillance.
- **Barangay Health Workers demonstrated varying levels of recall regarding signal definitions.**
- For registration, signal documentation practices were inconsistent. BHS utilized the CESR logbook
  differently and kept the key informant list in a separate notebook.
- One encouraging finding is that once signals are identified and verified, reporting is generally
  timely, often within one day.
- **However, we observed the absence of a formal mechanism for identifying silent barangays or
  health stations. Currently, municipalities are unable to distinguish whether the absence of reports
  reflects the absence of signals or a failure to report.**
- For confirmation, most health-event signals were verified by MESUs within one to two days,
  demonstrating responsiveness at the local level.
- Challenges remain regarding specimen collection. For respiratory events, particularly ILI clusters,
  municipalities reported limited availability of specimen collection kits and transport materials.
