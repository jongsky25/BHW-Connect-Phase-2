## Slide 4 — Identifying Signals

How to discover signals in the community?

Who might recognize when many people are sick at the same time?

*Speaker notes:*
"In the previous session, you've learned what the signals are. Signals are clues or patterns that may indicate a health concern. These could be unusual illnesses, clusters of similar symptoms, or sudden increases in severe illness in animals.

Then, how to discover signals in the community?"

(Ask) "Who might recognize when many people are sick at the same time?" (Solicit a few responses.)

"In your barangay, who are the people most likely to notice when health issues arise?" (Encourage participants to provide specific names or roles.)

"Who might recognize when many people are sick at the same time? Let's think about some examples. For example, a teacher or school headmaster would notice if an unusual number of children were absent from school because of illness or who all developed a similar illness at the same time. A local pastor or Imam might recognize when an unusual number of their congregants are sick at the same time."

*Instructor Tips:*
- Guide participants to consider individuals like teachers, religious leaders, community elders, and shopkeepers who interact with many people daily (sample answers see the next slide).

## Slide 7 — They are KEY INFORMANTS!

- Well-connected community members
- Would likely notice significant illness trends
- No special medical knowledge needed
- Just need clear guidance on what to report and whom to report it
- Serve as community monitors

*Speaker notes:*

## Slide 8 — A good Key Informant is ..

- Willing to participate and serve the community
- Someone with a good sense of the life and events happening around them or within their social group
- Having a broad social network, knowing lots of people in the community
- NOT necessary to have a formal education or even be able to read and write
- having the ability to contact the BHW when the need arises

*Speaker notes:*
"A good key informant has the qualities of: willing to participate and serve the community; having a good sense of the life and events happening around them or within their social group; having a broad social network, knowing lots of people in the community. A good key informant does NOT necessarily have a formal education or even be able to read and write—what's important is that he or she has the ability to contact the BHW when the need arises."

## Slide 9 — Key Informants for Urban Areas

Social connections may be more important than geographical location.

- Neighbors may not recognize when something unusual is happening in their neighborhood.

*Speaker notes:*
"Identifying key informants in urban areas requires different considerations. In cities, social connections are often more important than geographical location. For example, a city resident may be more connected to someone they attend church with than to a neighbor in the same building. Neighbors may not notice unusual events in their area."

Key informants include:

- Barangay Animal Health Worker
- Barangay Council for animal and environment
- Farmers
- Livestock Inspectors
- Wildlife preservation officers

Can immediately report events to the next appropriate level (i.e. MAO, MVO, MENRO)

*Speaker notes:*
"In some situations, individuals working in animal or environmental health are the first to detect signals that could pose risks to human health."

Key informants in this category include Barangay Council for animal and environment, Farmers, Livestock Inspectors, Forest Workers, Wildlife preservation officers and Protected Area Superintendent (PASu). These individuals are often on the front lines and may notice unusual animal deaths, signs of diseases, or environmental changes early.

## Slide 13 — Role of the BHW with Key Informants

Engagement with identified Key Informants (KIs) for CESR:

1. Engage the Key Informants
2. Orient the Key Informants
3. Communicate and maintain relationship with the Key Informants

*Speaker notes:*
**1. Engaging Key Informants**
To enhance community-based surveillance, it is important to identify and actively engage key informants (KIs) within your area. These individuals can play a vital role in detecting and reporting unusual health-related events or signals.

**2. Orienting Key Informants**
Orientation of key informants helps them better understand their role and responsibilities in CESR.

**3. Maintaining Communication and Motivation**
Sustained engagement of key informants is crucial for the long-term success of CESR.

## Slide 14 — Key Informants Engagement

- Identify and engage well-connected community members, including informal sources.
- Orient them on signals and reporting, with clear examples and instructions.
- Maintain regular communication and periodically reinforce guidance.
- Keep a Key Informant directory/roster at the Barangay Health Center for reference.

*Speaker notes:*
"Now let's look at how you can work with key informants. Key informants are your trusted partners in identifying signals within the community. To effectively engage key informants, follow these steps:

Identify well-connected individuals in your area. Think outside the box—this might include local gossip sources or community hubs.
Familiarize them with the signal list. Make sure they understand what to look for.
Give them clear guidance on reporting signals. Explain how, when, and to whom they should report.
Maintain regular communication. Check in occasionally to remind them of the signal list and how to report.
Keep a directory at the Barangay Health Station. Record their name, position, and contact information so others can reach them if needed."

*Instructor Tips:*
- Invite participants to share their experiences or good practices in engaging with community members. What are the tools or skills that would help conduct the steps mentioned above?

## Slide 15 — Orienting Key Informants (meetings and key messages)

- Meetings can be informal but should remain focused
- Decide on key messages before the meeting
  - Highlight the importance of their role
  - Explain what signals are
  - Clarify reporting channels
  - Emphasize the need for early reporting

*Speaker notes:*
"Training or orienting key informants doesn't have to be formal. You can meet casually, but your messages must be clear and focused.

Your conversation with them should be focused. You will decide on your key messages before the meeting, for example:
Emphasize the value and importance of their role and explain the signals to watch for.
Teach them how to notify the BHW or Barangay Health Station. Stress the importance of early notification, even if the cause is uncertain."

*Instructor Tips:*
- Stress the importance of delivering key messages like early notification of unusual events and clear instructions on how to report (phone call, text messages, walk-in, etc).

## Slide 16 — Orienting Key Informants (materials and support)

- BHW should give the KIs pamphlets or posters for reference and guide including contact information for reporting
- BHW may seek support of midwives and public health nurses to assist in conducting orientations

*Speaker notes:*
"The BHW should give the Key Informants materials like pamphlets or posters from the local government for reference, and include the specific information needed for reporting such as a phone number to call. The BHW may want to ask the nurse/midwife for support in approaching and orienting the Key Informants, especially if the BHW him or herself is new to the job."

*Instructor Tips:*
- Encourage BHWs to seek help from midwives or public health nurses when conducting orientations if needed.

## Slide 17 — Communicating with Key Informants

How to maintain or increase key informant engagement:

- Give timely feedback on reported signals.
- Maintain consistent communication and keep them updated on new or priority signals.
- Acknowledge their efforts through involvement in activities and expressions of appreciation.

*Speaker notes:*
"Once you've recruited key informants, how do you maintain or increase their engagement? Here are some tips:
Provide timely feedback when they report a 'signal'
Ensure consistent communication to reinforce their involvement
Regularly update them on 'new' or 'priority' signals
Recognize their contributions by involving them in community health activities
Express appreciation for their role and dedication to improving community health"

*Instructor Tips:*
- Highlight the importance of feedback and regular communication. When people see that their opinions are valued and acted upon, it builds trust and strengthens relationships. Feedback also makes community members feel involved and invested in the process, fostering a sense of ownership.

## Slide 35 — Exercise: Approaching Potential Key Informants

Instructions (3 Ws):

1. **Who?** Select one person from the key informants list.
2. **Why?** Explain why they are a strong KI candidate.
3. **What?**
   - Familiarize them with signal list
   - Give clear guidance on reporting
   - Maintain regular communication
4. **How?** Demonstrate the approach. Participants to act as BHW and Key Informant.

*Speaker notes:*
"Ok, jumping off from the key informant list output, let us try to simulate an initial approach with one of the key informants from your list. So let us use the 3 Ws: Who, Why, and What? \<read each question\>
Finally, how would you do this? Let us try to demonstrate. The participants will perform the role of the BHW and the KI."

*Instructor Tips:*
- This exercise encourages participants to apply the criteria for identifying key informants, prepare the necessary information to introduce the concept and the role, and think about how to assess the potential informant's suitability and willingness during the crucial first contact. Instructor can invite one participant per group to share.
