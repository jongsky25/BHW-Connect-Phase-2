**Activity title.** Community Event-based Surveillance and Response Refresher Training for
Supervisors of the Barangay Health Workers in Palawan and Marinduque

**Date and time.** July 19–25, 2026 | 8:00 AM to 5:00 PM

This training is the direct follow-through on the recommendation that recurs across the May–June
2026 M&SS findings: *"Conduct refresher training on CESR signal definitions, detection, recording,
and reporting procedures"* and *"Conduct targeted training and refresher activities for midwives,
BHWs, and key informants."* The M&SS round found that **BHWs demonstrated gaps in recalling CESR
signal definitions**; this is the remediation, aimed one level up — at the midwives and nurses who
supervise them.

## Activity highlights

- A refresher training on Community Event-Based Surveillance and Response (CESR) was conducted for
  Barangay Health Worker (BHW) supervisors in the **CESR pilot provinces of Palawan and Marinduque**
  from July 20 to 24, 2026.
- The primary objective was to **strengthen the capacity of Barangay Health Station (BHS)
  Supervisors, particularly midwives and nurses**, to more effectively support the implementation of
  CESR. This was achieved by discussing their key roles in **mentoring, monitoring, management, and
  mobilization of BHWs**, while increasing awareness of the interconnectedness of human, animal, and
  environmental health through a **One Health** perspective.
- The activity also aimed to contribute to the timely detection and response to public health
  threats and the achievement of the **7-1-7 targets**. The training adopted a **participatory and
  competency-based learning approach**, combining lectures, guided discussions, hands-on exercises,
  simulations, and knowledge assessments.
- A total of **390 participants** were trained across **8 batches** — 6 in Palawan and 2 in
  Marinduque. *(See the discrepancy note below: the batch table sums to 347.)*

The activity commenced with participant registration, opening ceremonies, and administration of a
**pre-test** to assess participants' knowledge on CESR.

- The background and implementation phases of CESR were presented first, followed by a comprehensive
  discussion of the modules. The rationale for conducting the refresher training was explained,
  **highlighting the results and feedback gathered during the Phase III Monitoring and Supportive
  Supervision visits** conducted in selected BHS in Palawan and Marinduque. These findings
  underscored the need to strengthen the capacity of BHW supervisors and reinforce their critical
  role in CESR implementation, particularly in case detection, notification, reporting, and response.

### Training modules

Co-developed by DOH EB and EpiC GHS:

- **Module 1: CESR Signal Refresh**
- **Module 2: CESR Steps Refresh**
- **Module 3: CESR BHW Supervision**

EpiC GHS staff served as the primary resource person for **Modules 1 and 2**, providing refresher
discussions on CESR signals, surveillance principles, and the CESR process, **with emphasis on
Detect, Confirm, Record, and Report**.

**Module 3** was jointly facilitated by representatives from the PDOHO and the Provincial, City, and
Municipal Epidemiology and Surveillance Units (P/C/MESUs), highlighting the critical roles and
responsibilities of BHW Supervisors in mentoring, supporting, and supervising BHWs and Key
Informants.

Representatives from RESU MIMAROPA and MESU Disease Surveillance Officers (DSOs) served as
facilitators and provided technical assistance throughout, guiding participants during practical
exercises, group discussions, simulations, and other learning activities.

### Participant feedback

- These activities enabled participants to review and apply surveillance concepts while
  strengthening practical skills in community event detection, verification, documentation,
  reporting, and coordination.
- Participants expressed appreciation for the practical and interactive nature of the training. The
  **scenario-based exercises and One Health coordination activities were identified as the most
  valuable sessions** because they allowed participants to apply theoretical concepts in realistic
  situations. Participants likewise highlighted the importance of:
  - Regular refresher trainings to sustain competencies;
  - Continuous mentoring and supervision support from surveillance units;
  - Additional opportunities to practice signal verification and risk assessment;
  - **Strengthened feedback mechanisms between BHWs, BHS Supervisors, and MESUs/CESUs.**

> That last ask is the same broken feedback loop the M&SS round found from the other direction —
> supervisors asking for it, having been told by monitoring that it isn't happening.

### Outcomes

By the end of the training, participants:

- Demonstrated an enhanced understanding of public health surveillance, the Public Health Approach,
  and the role of CESR in strengthening early warning and response systems at the community level.
  They were equipped with knowledge and skills necessary to effectively support, mentor, and
  supervise BHWs and Key Informants in the detection, confirmation, recording, and reporting of CESR
  signals.
- Can identify and assess priority CESR signals involving human, animal, and environmental health
  events; conduct signal checking and decision-making processes; and facilitate timely reporting and
  response to potential public health threats. They also enhanced their communication and supportive
  supervision skills for engaging with BHWs, Key Informants, local stakeholders, and surveillance
  units.
- Through practical exercises and One Health coordination simulations, gained hands-on experience in
  CESR processes and are prepared to implement actionable strategies that strengthen signal
  detection, confirmation, recording, reporting, feedback mechanisms, and community participation
  within their respective areas of responsibility.

Overall, the activity reinforced the critical role of BHW Supervisors in CESR and contributed to the
broader goal of strengthening local early warning and response systems, thus improving public health
preparedness and health security in the region.

### One Health and 7-1-7 sensitization

> **This activity also served as a sensitization on the principles of the One Health approach and
> the 7-1-7 surveillance framework targets for detection, notification, and early action responses.**

- The **four hypothetical scenarios** worked on during Session 1 Exercise 1 all started as signals
  affecting animal and/or environmental health, before finally affecting human health and
  necessitating multisectoral response.
- The last portion of Session 1 was dedicated to discussing the One Health components in CESR and
  why adopting the One Health approach is beneficial to disease surveillance as a whole.
- **Municipal Health Officers from Culion, Coron, and Dumaran openly asked and discussed**
  considerations for introducing and institutionalizing the One Health approach in their respective
  Local Government Units.
- The **three hypothetical scenarios** worked on during Session 3 Activity 3 prompted participants to
  consider approaches to mentoring and advising BHWs on timely detection and notification, thereby
  contributing to the 7-1-7 targets.
