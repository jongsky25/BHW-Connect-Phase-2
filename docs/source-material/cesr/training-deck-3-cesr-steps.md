## Slide 4 — Steps in CESR at Barangay Level

1. **Detect**
2. **Confirm**
3. **Record**
4. **Report**

*Speaker notes:*
"Here are the four steps in CESR at barangay level. Once Key Informants are identified by the BHW, together they can now watch out for the signals in the community as discussed earlier.

First is to detect signals, then, second, to confirm if the signal or rumor is true. Third, record all detected signals in a logbook or logsheet in the Barangay Health Stations or Barangay Health Centers. Lastly, report all signals to DSO and ESU for DSOs to further verify the signal and encode in the DOH system."

## Slide 6 — Step 1. DETECT

Signals are your first clue that something is threatening the health of the community. Their design is aimed at detecting high-priority diseases that may cause outbreaks.

Examples include:

- Multiple people with the same illness who are associated with one another, like two (2) people at the same workplace who develop bad lung disease
- People who get sick after being around sick animals (including rabid dogs or dying chickens)
- Individuals with symptoms of a high-priority disease like measles or cholera

*Speaker notes:*
"Signals are your first clue that something is threatening the health of the community. There are many signals in the community, but we spend most effort to look for those high priority diseases or situations that may cause outbreaks."

(Ask) "Do you remember the signals we discussed previously?" (Ask one or two participants to respond.)

## Slide 7 — Step 1. DETECT (key informants)

The most important way for the BHW to detect a signal happens when a Key Informant tells them about something unusual regarding the health of human, animal or environment in their social group or community.

*Speaker notes:*
"The most important way for the BHW to detect a signal happens when a Key Informant tells you about something unusual in their social group or community. Such reports need to be taken seriously and confirmed which will be discussed in the next step."

*Instructor Tips:*
- You may emphasize the importance of identifying the right KI and consistently and constantly engaging them as signals from the community may potentially come from them.

## Slide 8 — Step 1. DETECT (rumors)

BHWs should also consider rumors of unusual illnesses or clusters of illness heard from other sources as potential signals.

This might include the BHW's own social group, church, or even conversations with strangers while shopping or in a salon.

*Speaker notes:*
"In addition, you should also consider rumors of unusual illnesses or clusters of illness heard from other sources as potential signals. This might include the BHW's own social group, church, or even conversations with strangers while shopping or in a salon.

(Ask) What are rumors you have heard about recently? What did you do after hearing about it?"

*Instructor Tips:*
- Emphasize that potential signals may come from anyone other than the key informants like your neighbour, or acquaintances.

## Slide 9 — Steps in CESR: Confirm (section divider)

1. Detect
2. **Confirm**

*Speaker notes:*
"The second step is 'Confirm'. Once you receive a signal or 'chismis' from the Key Informant or any community member, you need to confirm."

## Slide 10 — Step 2: CONFIRM

Once a signal is reported to the BHW, it must be confirmed for accuracy. The BHW shall conduct confirmation of information from the key informants.

*Speaker notes:*
"Once a signal is reported to the BHW, it must be confirmed for accuracy. For example, if a local leader reports three unexpected deaths in their area, the BHW needs more details before reporting it further. Sometimes, the initial report from the Key Informant might be incomplete or incorrect, hence the BHW needs to gather all necessary details to evaluate the accuracy and severity of the signal."

*Instructor Tips:*
- Discuss the importance of confirmation: Confirmation or verification is crucial because it ensures the accuracy and reliability of information. This prevents the spread of misinformation, which can lead to confusion, panic, and inappropriate actions. In the context of surveillance, it helps in making informed decisions and taking effective measures.

## Slide 13 — Step 2: CONFIRM (what information to look for)

Important information that BHW should always look for are:

- Age of cases
- Complete location of signal
- How cases are connected
- When the sickness started
- Was there an exposure to sick wild or domestic animals or something unusual in the environment
- Possible reasons for deaths

*Speaker notes:*
Important information that BHW should always look for are:
- Ages of each of the involved individuals.
- Where they lived – a complete address or description of how to find their houses.
- How are they connected – that is, did they live in the same house? Were they related? Did they work together? Attend the same church? Did they all attend the same event, such as a wedding or other large gathering?
- When they got sick and when they died. These dates are important and should be recorded even if there isn't complete certainty (along with a note of why there is uncertainty).
- Was there an exposure to a sick wild or domestic animal, or something unusual in the environment, like a chemical spill.
- Possible reasons for their deaths.

*Instructor Tips:*
- You may elaborate each item and relate it to the answers of the group.

## Slide 17 — Step 3: RECORD

- Every signal reported must be recorded on a log sheet kept at the Barangay Health Station (BHS)
- It's important to record ALL signals, even if it turns out that the original reported signal is incorrect

*Speaker notes:*
"Every signal should be recorded in a logsheet. The logsheet, or logbook, should always be kept at the Barangay Health Station. This is to ensure safekeeping and to prevent the loss of the logbook. Additionally, all signals, even if it turns out that the originally reported signals are incorrect, should be recorded in the logbook."

*Instructor Tips:*
- Explain the importance of recording all signals: it will help understand what's happening in the community and some situations might change afterward (some false signals might turn out to be true once further information is available).
- The instructor can ask around how they usually keep and organize their files/data in consideration of data security.

## Slide 18 — Step 3: RECORD (who records)

- If BHW is near the Barangay Health Station (BHS), he/she will immediately write in the logsheet the reported signal
- If BHW is far from BHS, he/she will text or call the received information to BHS Staff (Ex. BHW Leader) to log the received information in the log book

*Speaker notes:*
"If BHW is near the Barangay Health Station, he or she will immediately write in the logsheet the reported signal. If BHW is far from BHS, he/she will text or call the received information to BHW staff (e.g. BHW leader or a designated CESR focal person in charge) to log the information. Thus whoever is near the BHS or is in the BHS, is in-charge of logging and recording signals that are reported to the BHW. It is important that this is done to prevent data loss.

In the next slide, we will show you the key information needed in the logsheet."

*Instructor Tips:*
- The instructor can explain that any trained personnel can log entries in the logsheet as long as they are filled out properly.
- Some BHWs cannot read or write, so it is okay to delegate someone in the group who can record the signals.

## Slide 21 — Step 3. Record the Signal in the Logbook (definitions 2 of 3)

| Definition of variables | |
| --- | --- |
| Date of signal occurrence | Date of signal occurrence could be (choose the earliest): 1. Date of symptom onset of index case (first detected case); 2. Date of symptom onset of the earliest case; 3. Date of first case identified in a facility or community; 4. Date of laboratory result released of index or earliest case |
| Location of signal occurrence | Location where the signal occurred. As near as possible, record an address or other kind of location so that the place can be found again in case an investigation is needed. |

*Speaker notes:*
"The date of signal occurrence could be the date of symptom onset of the first detected case, the date of symptom onset of the earliest case, the date of identification of the case in a facility or community, OR the date of laboratory results release of the first detected case or earliest case, if a confirmatory test is conducted. Choose the earliest date to report and record if more than one date is identified.

For the location where the signal occurred, record the location as near as possible. This includes the address, purok number, certain landmarks, and other pertinent information that can pinpoint the location of the signal so that the place can be found again in case an investigation is needed.

Numbers of any suspect involved cases, hospitalized, and deaths should be tallied, for example, Cases: 7, Hospitalized: 4, Deaths: 3. Please note that the total number of cases would include those who were hospitalized and those who died. If none died or none was hospitalized, the encoder should indicate zero."

## Slide 23 — Screen the Signal

Screening involves checking if the reported information is true and meets the criteria for being a signal.

**Signals for Detection in the Community**

1. CLUSTERS OF SERIOUS ILLNESS OR DEATH
2. CLUSTERS OF SEVERE ILLNESS IN ANIMALS
3. SEVERE ILLNESS IN AN INDIVIDUAL WHO HAS BEEN EXPOSED TO A SICK ANIMAL
4. INDIVIDUALS WITH CERTAIN KINDS OF ILLNESS
   - Children with rash, fever, and either cough, cold or red eyes
   - A child under the age of 15 years experiencing sudden weakness in arms or legs
   - Individuals over 2 years old with severe watery diarrhea leading to dehydration

*Speaker notes:*
"Let's look at the Screening again. Screening involves checking if the reported information is true and meets the criteria of being one of these four signals. For example, if the information received is about signs and symptoms that suggest a measles case, it meets the criteria and should be reported to the supervisors. Screening can be done by the BHW or the encoder—double-checking the information received with the list of signals will help verify it."

*Instructor Tips:*
- Remind the participants the information should still be recorded even though it does not meet the criteria or definition. Explain that it will help understand what's happening in the community and some situations might change afterward.
- Another purpose is to assess the sensitivity and specificity of the surveillance system and to determine if the signal list needs updating. However, this might be too technical for the audience.

## Slide 30 — Step 4: REPORT

Once the accuracy of the signal has been confirmed and screened, it shall be reported immediately to Rural Health Midwife / Public Health Nurse and/or Disease Surveillance Officer.

*Speaker notes:*
"Once the accuracy of the signal has been confirmed and screened, it shall be reported immediately to immediate supervisors such as the Midwife, Nurse and/or Disease Surveillance Officer (DSO) at the MESU or CESU."

*Instructor Tips:*
- Emphasize that if signals need immediate attention or response while the midwife and/or nurse are not available or not responding, the signal should be reported to the DSO directly.

## Slide 31 — Step 4: REPORT (method and timeliness)

- The method of reporting is thru text/online message, phone call, or face to face.
- Reporting should happen as soon as signal is detected (within 24 hours from detection)

*Speaker notes:*
"Any method that is currently utilized in the community to notify the immediate supervisor or the DSO is okay, either through text, online message, or phone call. Reporting should happen as soon as a signal is detected, confirmed, and screened, no more than 24 hours."

*Instructor Tips:*
- Discuss the communication channels and protocol for reporting in the jurisdiction.
- Emphasize the importance of timely reporting—encourage the reporting of any available information promptly, even if the signal hasn't been fully investigated yet.

## Slide 34 — Step 4: REPORT (supervisor's role)

- Rural Health Midwife or Public Health Nurse will check if the signal fit the criteria to watch in the community.
- If it does, it must be reported to the Disease Surveillance Officer immediately.

*Speaker notes:*
"Rural Health Midwife or the Public Health Nurse, as BHS supervisors, once receiving the report, should check if the signal matches the official list in the Event-Based Surveillance and Response (ESR) interim guidelines. If it does, it should be reported to the MESU/CESU immediately. If not, the midwife or nurse should give feedback to BHW. The BHW should check back later (e.g. after one week) to see if the situation changed."

*Instructor Tips:*
- The feedback should be given regardless of whether it is reported to DSO/MESU or not.

## Slide 35 — Step 4: REPORT — information flow diagram

Reporting chain (signals flow upward, feedback flows back down at each level):

- **Key Informants** → Signals / Feedback →
- **Barangay Health Workers** → Signals / Feedback →
- **Nurses / Midwives** → Signals / Feedback →
- **Disease Surveillance Officers (DSO)** — **MESU / CESU**

Parallel reporting from the BHW to nurse or midwife and DSO is encouraged for immediate notification and response.

*Speaker notes:*
"This diagram shows the information flow from the community to the local level. Parallel reporting from BHW to nurse and midwife and DSO is encouraged for immediate notification and in instances where nurse/midwife are too busy with other programs.

Besides reporting, same as nurses and midwives providing feedback to you (BHWs), you (BHWs) are expected to give feedback to Key Informants or other sources of signals."

## Slide 37 — Steps in CESR at Barangay Level (full recap)

**DETECT**
- Learn the list
- Identify, recruit, and train Key Informants

**CONFIRM**
- Make sure the details reported are accurate

**RECORD**
- Name, and contact number of Key Informant
- Date, time, and location of signal
- Description of signal – what happened?
- Number of cases, deaths, and hospitalizations

**REPORT**
- Determine if signal information meets the signal definition
- Report those that do immediately (<24 hr) to the Nurse/Midwife/DSO using the method decided upon previously
- Include whether the signal involves exposures to animals or environmental contamination

*Speaker notes:*
"Let's review the steps in CESR at the Barangay Level. First is to detect the signals. This involves identifying key informants in the community who have extensive connections and may notice unusual events that could potentially lead to an outbreak. Once these key informants are identified, teach them the signals they need to watch out for and tell them how to contact or communicate with you if they have any signal to report to.

The second step is to confirm the signals. After receiving the report, make sure the details are accurate. Visit the area and talk to those affected to verify the details. Collect the information including: date when the unusual events (signals) began, location, number of people affected and identify whether there are any severe cases such as hospital admissions or deaths.

The third step is Record. Name, contact information of key informants, date time, location of the signal, description of the signal—what happened? Number of people affected and identify whether there are any severe cases such as hospital admissions or deaths. Record all signals in the logbook completely and accurately.

The fourth step is Report. Determine if signal information meets the signal definition. Report those that do immediately or within 24 hr to the Nurse/Midwife/DSO using the method agreed upon previously. Include essential information to help the supervisor decide on an urgent response based on event timing, case numbers, and severity. Specify if the signal involves animal exposures or environmental contamination."

*Instructor Tips:*
The instructor can:
- Discuss the importance of accurate and complete record-keeping
- Revisit the information that should be included in the signal logsheet
- Emphasize the timely reporting to the BHS Supervisor (Nurse or Midwife) and/or DSO.
