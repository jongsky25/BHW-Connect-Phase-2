## Slide 20 — STEPS IN CONDUCTING INTERVIEWS FOR DETECTION AND CONFIRMATION (section divider)

*Speaker notes:*
"Let's look at the steps in conducting interviews for detection and confirmation."

*Instructor Tips:*
- Organize a short dialogue exercise for an introduction of the strategies below where participants practice introducing themselves and building rapport.
- Provide constructive feedback to participants, focusing on their use of these strategies.

## Slide 21 — The nine steps in conducting interviews

1. Establish Rapport
2. Introduce Yourself
3. Highlight Confidentiality
4. Solicit Consent
5. Conduct the Interview
6. Discuss the Next Steps
7. Provide Health Education
8. Ask for Other Concerns
9. Provide Feedback

*Speaker notes:*
In conducting interviews for detection and confirmation, it is important to follow a structured process: begin by establishing rapport to build trust, introduce yourself clearly, and highlight confidentiality to ensure openness. Solicit consent before proceeding, then conduct the interview in a respectful and systematic manner. Afterward, discuss the next steps, provide relevant health education, and ask about any other concerns the interviewee may have. Finally, give constructive feedback to reinforce understanding and cooperation.

*Instructor Tips:*
- Create a short scenario to go through the steps in conducting interviews.

## Slide 22 — Step 1: Establish Rapport

Build a trusting and respectful connection that makes the other person feel comfortable to share information.

> "Magandang araw po. Kumusta po kayo?"

*Speaker notes:*
Establishing rapport is the first and most important step in conducting an effective interview. It involves building a trusting and respectful connection with the interviewee so they feel comfortable, valued, and safe in sharing information. A warm greeting, showing genuine interest, and using open body language can help create an atmosphere of trust that encourages honest and open communication.

*Instructor Tips:*
- Remind participants that good rapport often determines the accuracy and completeness of the information shared.
- Other possible dialogue: "Magandang araw po. Kumusta po kayo? Naparito po ako hindi para manghimasok, kundi para makipag-usap at kumustahin ang kalagayan ng inyong anak."

## Slide 23 — Step 2: Introduce Yourself

Introducing yourself and your companions means clearly stating your name, role, and purpose to set a respectful and professional tone.

> "Ako po si [Name], BHW dito sa barangay."

*Speaker notes:*
Clearly state your name, role, and purpose to set a professional and respectful tone from the start.

*Instructor Tips:*
- Remind them that many people may not know their official role as BHWs; introduction avoids misunderstanding.
- Other possible dialogue: "Ako po si [Name], Barangay Health Worker na naka-assign dito. Ipinadala po ako ni Kapitan para makipag-ugnayan sa inyo."

## Slide 24 — Step 3: Highlight Confidentiality

Assure the person that all information shared will be kept private and used only for the purpose of the investigation.

> "Ligtas at kumpidensyal po ang pag-uusap natin"

*Speaker notes:*
Assuring confidentiality makes interviewees more open, as they know information will be kept private and only used for health purposes.

*Instructor Tips:*
- Share how confidentiality builds credibility. Give an example of what not to do (e.g., gossiping about cases).
- Other possible dialogue: "Lahat po ng ating pag-uusapan ay mananatiling kumpidensyal. Hindi po ito ilalabas sa iba nang walang pahintulot ninyo."

## Slide 25 — Step 4: Solicit Consent

Ask for the person's permission to proceed with the interview to ensure they are willing and comfortable to participate.

> "Pwede po ba akong magtanong tungkol sa kanya/inyong kalagayan?"

*Speaker notes:*
Always ask permission before starting. Consent ensures respect for the person's rights and comfort in sharing information.

*Instructor Tips:*
- Stress cultural sensitivity—people appreciate being asked instead of ordered.
- Other possible dialogue: "Kung okay po sa inyo, maaari ko po bang alamin ang ilang detalye tungkol sa kalagayan ng inyong anak?"

## Slide 26 — Step 5: Conduct the Interview

Conduct the interview by asking questions in a respectful and organized manner to gather accurate information.

> "Ano po ang mga unang sintomas na napansin ninyo?"

*Speaker notes:*

Ask respectful and organized questions about signal details, timeline, spread, and actions taken. This helps gather accurate and complete information.

Other Possible dialogue:
- "Kailan po nagsimula ang mga sintomas?"

## Slide 27 — What are the questions to ask?

Three question areas:

1. **Signal Details**
2. **Timeline and Spread** (Kailan at sino ang naapektuhan?)
3. **Actions and Response** (Ano ang ginawa pagkatapos?)

## Slide 28 — Question set 1: Signal Details

- Ano po ang iyong pangalan, contact number, at trabaho/tungkulin?
- Kailan at paano po ninyo nalaman ang pangyayari/sakit na ito?
- Maaari niyo pong ilarawan ang pangyayari o sintomas na napansin?
- Ilan ang naapektuhan (nagkasakit, na-ospital, namatay)?
- Saan po mismo nangyari ang pangyayari (lokasyon)?

## Slide 29 — Question set 2: Timeline and Spread

- Kailan po unang nagsimula ang sintomas o pangyayari?
- May iba pa po bang tao na nakaranas ng kaparehong sintomas o pangyayari?
- Mayroon pa po ba kayong ibang napansing kakaibang pangyayari bago o pagkatapos nito?
- "Nagkaroon po ba kayo ng kamakailang pakikisalamuha o paghawak sa mga hayop, maging alagang hayop o mga alagang pangkabuhayan (livestock)?"

## Slide 30 — Question set 3: Actions and Response

- Ano po ang mga unang hakbang na ginawa ninyo o ng komunidad (hal. pumunta sa health center, naglinis, tumawag ng tulong)?
- May natanggap po ba kayong tulong o aksyon mula sa iba (opisyal, kagawad, BHW, ospital)?
- May nais pa po ba kayong idagdag na impormasyon na makakatulong sa aming pag-unawa sa pangyayaring ito?

## Slide 31 — Step 6: Discuss the Next Steps

Discussing the next steps means explaining what will happen after the interview so the person knows what to expect.

> "Ang lahat pong impormasyon na aking nakalap ay aking itatala sa logbook at irereport sa BHS. Nais ko rin pong hilingin na panatilihin ninyo ang bukas na linya ng komunikasyon upang madali namin kayong matawagan para sa mga updates at feedback."

*Speaker notes:*
Explain what will happen after the interview so the interviewee knows what to expect and feels assured that their input has value.

*Instructor Tips:*
- Avoid making promises that cannot be delivered. Stick to what is realistic.
- Other possible dialogue: "Base po sa sinabi ninyo, napansin natin na nanghihina ang paa ng bata at matagal na po itong nararamdaman. Para mas matulungan siya, ang susunod na hakbang ay maipatingin natin agad sa health center o ospital. Ako po ang mag-uulat nito sa aming nurse at midwife para matulungan kayong makapunta doon. Maaari rin po akong sumama o mag-coordinate sa Barangay Captain para sa transportasyon."

## Slide 32 — Step 7: Provide Health Education

Provide health education by sharing accurate and practical information to help people understand and protect their health.

*Speaker notes:*
Use the interview as an opportunity to share accurate and practical health information that empowers individuals and communities.

*Instructor Tips:*
- Encourage using simple language, visuals, or analogies familiar to the local community.
- Other possible dialogue: "Habang inaayos po natin ang pagpapatingin sa health center, maaari po muna nating alagaan ang bata sa bahay. Siguraduhin pong may sapat siyang pahinga, bigyan ng masustansiyang pagkain at malinis na tubig. Iwasan din po ang paggamit ng anumang gamot o herbal na hindi sigurado ang epekto, para po hindi lumala ang kondisyon. Ligtas po siyang matutulungan kapag nasuri agad ng health worker."

## Slide 33 — Step 8: Check for Other Concerns

Give the person a chance to share questions or issues that were not discussed.

> "Mayroon pa po ba kayong ibang alalahanin tungkol sa kalusugan o sa tulong na maaring maibigay ng health team?"

*Speaker notes:*
Give interviewees a chance to raise issues not yet covered, ensuring no important information is missed.

*Instructor Tips:*
- Pause and show openness—sometimes the most important details come at the end.

## Slide 34 — Step 9: Provide Feedback

Regardless if the signal being confirmed, provide feedback virtually or in person.

> "Magandang Umaga po, tungkol po sa iniulat nyong karamdaman, kasalukuyan na po itong iniimbestigahan ng ating BHS team"

*Speaker notes:*
Always provide feedback—whether virtually or in person—so the interviewee feels acknowledged and involved in the process.

*Instructor Tips:*
- Even a short 'thank you' or quick update builds trust and motivates future cooperation.

## Slide 36 — The ten considerations

1. Follow protocols
2. Be respectful
3. Be sensitive
4. Create a safe space
5. Ask open-ended questions
6. Be neutral
7. Active listening
8. Clarifying & Paraphrasing
9. Summarizing
10. Conciseness

*Speaker notes:*
Effective interviews require following protocols, showing respect, being sensitive, creating a safe space, asking open-ended questions, staying neutral, practicing active listening, clarifying/paraphrasing, summarizing, and being concise.

*Instructor Tips:*
- Break down each point with examples. Ask participants to reflect: which skill do they find most challenging?

## Slide 37 — Consideration 1: Follow protocols

Secure the required endorsement letter from offices before conducting interviews or site visits.

*Speaker notes:*
Following protocols means ensuring that all official requirements are met before starting an interview or conducting a site visit. This includes securing the proper endorsement letters from offices or authorities, which helps establish legitimacy, build trust with the community, and avoid misunderstandings. Adhering to protocols also demonstrates professionalism and accountability as a BHW.

*Instructor Tips:*
- Share that endorsement letters give authority to collect information and prevent resistance from community members.
- Remind participants to always double-check documents before going to the field.
- Ask: What offices or leaders do you usually seek endorsement from in your barangay?

## Slide 38 — Consideration 2: Be respectful

- **Follow protocols** — Secure the required endorsement letter from offices before conducting interviews or site visits.
- **Be respectful** — Practice mindful speaking by being careful of your tone of voice, keeping eye contact and good body posture, and giving suggestions respectfully.

*Speaker notes:*
Being respectful in interviews means speaking mindfully with a calm tone, using good eye contact and posture, and giving suggestions positively without judgment to build trust and openness.

*Instructor Tips:*
- Model how different tones of voice can change the meaning of the same phrase.
- Cultural sensitivity: Remind participants that respect may look different depending on local customs (e.g., gestures, manner of addressing elders).

## Slide 39 — Consideration 3: Be sensitive

Be mindful of non-verbal cues, show empathy, and offer positive reassurance.

*Speaker notes:*
Being sensitive means noticing non-verbal cues (such as body language, facial expressions, and tone), showing empathy, and giving positive reassurance to make people feel understood and supported.

*Instructor Tips:*
- Encourage participants to use phrases like "I understand how you feel" or "Salamat po sa pagbabahagi."
- Stress that sensitivity is not only about listening but also about responding with care and compassion.

## Slide 40 — Consideration 4: Create a safe space

- **Be sensitive** — Be mindful of non-verbal cues, show empathy, and offer positive reassurance.
- **Create a safe space** — Ensure that the interview area will allow confidentiality and will be conducive to sensitive topics.

*Instructor Tips:*
- Remind participants to avoid public or crowded areas where others might overhear.
- Share examples of appropriate venues (quiet corner in barangay hall, health post room).

## Slide 41 — Consideration 5: Ask open-ended questions

Ask questions that will enable probing.

*Speaker notes:*
Asking open-ended questions allows interviewees to share detailed information in their own words, encourages deeper discussion, and helps uncover important details that closed questions might miss.

*Instructor Tips:*
- Give simple examples: "Ano po ang nangyari?" vs. "May sakit ba siya?"
- Encourage participants to start questions with who, what, when, where, why, and how.

## Slide 42 — Let's Try! Open-ended or closed-ended?

Determine whether the question asked is open-ended or closed-ended.

- "Ano po ang napansin ninyong mga sintomas bago siya nagkasakit?"
- "May lagnat po ba siya?"
- "Kailan po nagsimula ang sintomas—kahapon ba o ngayong araw lang?"

*Instructor Tips:*
- Emphasize that both types of questions are useful. Closed-ended questions help confirm specifics (dates, numbers), while open-ended ones allow people to explain fully and reveal information you might not think to ask.
- "May Lagnat po ba siya" → "Paano po ba nagsimula yung lagnat nya?"
- "Ano po ang napansin ninyong mga sintomas bago siya nagkasakit?" or "Paano niyo po napansin na may kakaiba sa kanya bago siya nagkasakit?"
- "Kailan po nagsimula ang sintomas—kahapon ba o ngayong araw lang?" → "Pwede niyo po bang ikuwento kung paano nagsimula ang mga sintomas?"

## Slide 43 — Consideration 6: Be Neutral

- **Ask open-ended questions** — Ask questions that will enable probing.
- **Be Neutral** — Be aware of personal biases, avoid being judgmental, and try to understand different viewpoints.

*Speaker notes:*
Asking open-ended questions allows interviewees to share detailed information in their own words, encourages deeper discussion, and helps uncover important details that closed questions might miss.

*Instructor Tips:*
- Give simple examples: "Ano po ang nangyari?" vs. "May sakit ba siya?"
- Encourage participants to start questions with who, what, when, where, why, and how.

## Slide 44 — Consideration 7: Active Listening

Show the interviewee that you are interested in the conversation.

*Speaker notes:*
Active listening means giving full attention to the interviewee, showing genuine interest through eye contact, nodding, and verbal cues, and avoiding distractions. It helps the person feel heard and encourages them to share more openly.

*Instructor Tips:*
- Demonstrate simple cues like nodding, saying "I see" or "Sige po, tuloy niyo po".
- Remind participants to avoid interrupting and to let silences give space for answers.

## Slide 45 — Consideration 8: Clarifying & Paraphrasing

- **Active Listening** — Show the interviewee that you are interested in the conversation.
- **Clarifying & Paraphrasing** — Clarify and confirm understanding of the conversation.

*Speaker notes:*
Clarifying and paraphrasing means restating what the interviewee said in your own words to confirm understanding, avoid misinterpretation, and show that you are actively listening.

*Instructor Tips:*
Demonstrate with an example:
- Interviewee: "Matagal na siyang nilalagnat, parang mga tatlong araw na."
- BHW: "So, ibig niyo pong sabihin, nagsimula ang lagnat mga tatlong araw na ang nakalipas?"

## Slide 46 — Consideration 9: Summarizing

Summarize key points from the interview and confirm the accuracy of the information gathered.

*Speaker notes:*
Summarizing means restating the key points from the interview to confirm accuracy, ensure nothing important is missed, and close the discussion with shared understanding.

*Instructor Tips:*
- Show an example: "So, to summarize: dalawang tao ang nagkasakit noong Lunes, parehong may lagnat at ubo, at dinala sila sa health center kahapon. Tama po ba?"
- Remind participants that summarizing should be brief but complete.

## Slide 47 — Consideration 10: Conciseness

- **Summarizing** — Summarize key points from the interview and confirm the accuracy of the information gathered.
- **Conciseness** — The interview should be simple, clear, direct, and quick.

*Speaker notes:*
Conciseness means keeping the interview simple, clear, and direct—avoiding unnecessary words or questions while still gathering essential information quickly and respectfully.

*Instructor Tips:*
- Stress that concise does not mean rushing; it means being focused.
- Share tip: stick to questions relevant to signal verification, avoid "chitchat" that may cause confusion.

## Slide 54 — SCENARIO A

You are the Barangay Health Worker (BHW) assigned in a rural area. One morning, the Barangay Captain informs you that a caretaker in a goat farm has been sick for several days.

When you arrive at the farm, you see the caretaker lying down in a small hut near the goat pens. He has had fever, cough, and body weakness for the past three days. Some co-workers are present but appear hesitant to talk about the situation.

As you introduce yourself, the farm owner arrives. While explaining the caretaker's condition, the owner also mentions that several goats have recently fallen ill, showing loss of appetite and weakness. The owner expresses concern but admits they have not yet sought veterinary or medical help.

You are not a member of this farm community, so you will need to approach the caretaker, co-workers, and farm owner carefully.

## Slide 55 — SCENARIO B

You are the Barangay Health Worker (BHW) assigned in Barangay Pagkakaisa. One afternoon, while picking up your own child at the elementary school, you overhear a conversation between a Grade 1 teacher and a parent.

The parent explains that her 6-year-old son will remain absent from school for another week because he is still suffering from watery diarrhea and signs of dehydration. The teacher expresses concern but doesn't know what advice to give.

Sensing the seriousness of the situation, you decide to approach the teacher after their conversation. You introduce yourself and politely ask for the parent's address, explaining that you would like to visit the family at home to check on the child's condition and provide support.

The next step is for you, as the BHW, to conduct a home visit with the parent and child.

## Slide 56 — SCENARIO A: What to demonstrate

**Apply the learnings from the Steps in Conducting Interviews for Detection and Confirmation**
- Establish rapport and introduce yourself.
- Highlight confidentiality and solicit consent.
- Conduct the interview with the caretaker, co-workers, and farm owner.
- Ask open-ended questions to gather details about the illness (both human and animal).
- Actively listen, clarify, paraphrase, and summarize key points.
- Provide feedback, discuss next steps, and offer health education.

**Observe the Considerations in Conducting Interviews**
- Follow protocols and maintain neutrality.
- Be respectful, sensitive, and create a safe space for sharing information.
- Remain concise while ensuring all essential details are gathered.

**Close the Interview Properly**
- Ask if there are other concerns.
- Reassure the caretaker and farm owner that appropriate actions will be coordinated.
- Thank them for their time and cooperation.

## Slide 57 — SCENARIO B: What to demonstrate

**Apply the Steps in Conducting Interviews for Detection and Confirmation**
- At the school: Establish rapport with the teacher, introduce yourself, and explain your role. Request the parent's address respectfully, ensuring confidentiality.
- At the home visit: Introduce yourself again to the parent, highlight confidentiality, and solicit consent before asking about the child's condition.
- Use open-ended questions (e.g., "Can you describe when the diarrhea started and how it has changed over the past few days?").
- Practice active listening, clarifying, paraphrasing, and summarizing to confirm key details (frequency of diarrhea, hydration status, home remedies used, warning signs).
- Discuss next steps: importance of referral to a health facility, giving Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS), ensuring access to clean drinking water.
- Provide health education on handwashing, food safety, and safe water practices.

**Observe the Considerations in Conducting Interviews**
- Be respectful and sensitive to the parent's possible worries (e.g., stigma, financial concerns).
- Follow protocols, remain neutral, and create a safe space for discussion.
- Use clear, simple, and concise language.

**Close the Interview Properly**
- Ask if the parent has other concerns about her child's health.
- Reassure her that the barangay health team can support them.
- Thank the parent for her cooperation and assure follow-up.
