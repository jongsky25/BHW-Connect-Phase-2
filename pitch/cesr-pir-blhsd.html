Three things to say, in this order:

One — only the relevant questions open. The animal-exposure branch never appears, so it can never be filled in wrongly. Fewer questions, better data.

Two — the read-back. Accuracy is confirmed at the point of entry, by the person who was actually there, not at consolidation weeks later by someone who was not.

Three — the timestamp. The 7-1-7 clock starts itself. Nobody has to remember to start it.

Then the line that matters most: the logbook stops existing. There is no paper to fill, no photograph to send, no MESU re-encoding at the other end — because describing the signal is what records and reports it. That is the same claim the demo on slide 23 makes; say it the same way in both places.

Insight without exposure

Maximise the insight. Never the exposure.

  • 01Consent at first login, in Filipino and English. The privacy notice on every screen.
  • 02The BHW consents for herself. Community members never touch the app — so their data is de-identified at capture: counts, ages, purok. Not names.
  • 03Above the barangay, only aggregates travel.
  • 04Chat and analytics purge at 24 months. The audit trail is kept for five years.
  • 05Data-subject export and anonymise on request. NPC 72-hour breach playbook, rehearsed against a written runbook.

Insight comes from patterns across barangays — never from any one household.

Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173); BHW Connect Phase 2 data-privacy and consent design.

The honest version, if pressed: the surveillance value lives in the aggregate, so the aggregate is what we keep. We do not need names to see an outbreak.
