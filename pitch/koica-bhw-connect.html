01 The Frontline
~200,000Barangay Health Workers

The backbone of primary care.

In every barangay, volunteer health workers are the first — and often the only — point of care. They weigh newborns, track pregnancies, chase immunizations, and notice outbreaks before anyone else.

They carry the system on paper and memory.

02 The Gap

The frontline is working blind — and so are we.

Paper that disappears

Records live in notebooks. Data is delayed, duplicated, or lost before it ever informs a decision.

No real-time picture

By the time community health data reaches decision-makers, the moment to act on it has already passed.

Workers left unsupported

Two hundred thousand volunteers hold up primary care with little training, tooling, or recognition.

Systems that don't connect

The barangay level and the national level can't see each other. Insight never travels.

03 The Cost

A blind spot has a price.

  • — Outbreaks are noticed late, not early.
  • — Maternal and child risks go unseen until they become emergencies.
  • — Decisions are made on data that is weeks — or months — old.
  • — The effort of 200,000 people never becomes national insight.

The gap isn't a lack of care. It's a lack of connection.

04 Our Stance
Technology is not the end-all.

A better app doesn't fix a health system — people do. BHW Connect earns its place only by making the human frontline stronger: faster to answer, easier to train, impossible to overlook. The tool serves the worker, never the other way around.
