01 The Frontline
~300,000Barangay Health Workers

The backbone of primary care.

In every barangay, volunteer health workers are the first — and often the only — point of care. They weigh newborns, track pregnancies, screen for hypertension and diabetes, and notice outbreaks before anyone else.

They carry the system on paper and memory.

02 What DOH Has Already Delivered
1 yearPhase 1 · Delivered

A national BHW registry, after three decades without one.

BHW Connect Phase 1 — conceptualized and delivered by BLHSD — profiled close to 300,000 Barangay Health Workers nationwide within about a year, organizationally located from national down to barangay level.

Proven delivery

This is not a bureau proposing its first platform. It has already taken a national digital health system from concept to nationwide adoption.

The users already exist

Onboarding for Phase 2 is activation of known, registered users — not recruitment from an empty database, which is where most digital-health platforms fail.

A proven adoption pathway

The same CHD, LGU and BHW mobilization channels that delivered nationwide profiling in a year deliver Phase 2 onboarding.

03 Six Months From Now

The training ends. Then the system forgets.

  • — The instructional videos are final. PhilPEN guidance moves; the videos don't.
  • — The trained representative cascades to their peers. Nobody measures what survives the retelling.
  • — The return-demonstration checklists sit in a filing cabinet in Iloilo. Nobody can answer which BHWs are competent?
  • — At a doorstep in month nine, a BHW has a question and no one to ask.
  • — The contractor's five months end. The capacity leaves with them.

None of this is a flaw in the training. It is what happens to every training that ends.

04 What HHP+ Has Already Built

The clinical work is done — and it is good.

Facility capacity built

Physicians, nurses and NCD coordinators trained under PhilPEN 2025 and PPSA, integrating diabetes and hypertension care into primary care across Western Visayas.

Tools that work

Clinical job aids, NCD flipcharts and monitoring tools developed, field-tested and distributed — not drafts, but instruments already in use.

A defined BHW competency

Region VI CHD already treats community screening — including CBG monitoring with return demonstration — as a Barangay Health Worker skill.

The gap was never the clinical content. It is what carries it — and what keeps carrying it after month five.

Summarized from “Strengthening Barangay Health Worker Capacity under HHP+”, BLHSD alignment meeting, 31 July 2026.

06 What a Training Programme Cannot Do

Six gaps that survive a perfectly executed APW.6 open

G1Open

Frozen content

Video modules and printed job aids are final at month five. When PhilPEN guidance changes, every distributed copy is quietly wrong and there is no mechanism to correct it.

“Review and update of instructional video modules” — a one-time act inside a five-month contract.

G2Open

Cascade decay

Representatives are trained, then cascade to peers. What each BHW actually receives depends on who retold it and how well — and nothing measures the difference.

Module 6: “cascade standardized practices to their peers within the LGU.”

G3Open

Thin coverage

All Iloilo districts and a single Guimaras session, delivered by one contractor in five months. Most of the region's BHWs are reached second-hand or not at all.

Coverage: “Iloilo (all districts) and Guimaras (one session).”

G4Open

No route to national

Output 5 delivers scale-up recommendations. A recommendation is not a mechanism. Nothing carries this package to the other ~290,000 BHWs.

Output 5.2: “scale-up and adaptation recommendations.”

G5Open

One competency, once

Seven domains on NCD screening, and nothing left standing to hold the next programme. Immunization, nutrition, MNCH each start again from zero.

Scope limit: “not a new national training system.”

G6Open

Paper competence

The return-demonstration checklist is a paper form, hand-scored and filed locally. There is no queryable record of which BHWs are competent, and no re-certification trigger.

29 criteria, 87-point scale, evaluator signature — on paper.

07 The Cost

A blind spot has a price.

  • — Screening is missed, referral is delayed, follow-up is inconsistent.
  • — Decisions are made on data that is weeks — or months — old.
  • — The effort of 300,000 people never becomes national insight.
  • — Every programme that follows pays the training cost again.

The gap isn't a lack of care. It's a lack of connection.

08 Our Stance
Technology is not the end-all.

A better app doesn't fix a health system — people do. BHW Connect earns its place only by making the human frontline stronger: faster to answer, easier to train, impossible to overlook. The tool serves the worker, never the other way around.

09 What BHW Connect Is

Three things a training event cannot be.

Support at the doorstep

A bilingual Knowledge Base and Chat Guide that answer a BHW's question in Filipino, English or Taglish — at the moment the question arises, not at the moment the training was held.

Training that doesn't decay

Every BHW takes the same standardized modules directly — no cascade, no retelling. Assessment is per person, against the same rubric, and the content updates for everyone at once.

A record that can be queried

Competency is written to the national registry. DOH can ask “which BHWs in this municipality are certified for CBG screening?” and get an answer — at any level of the hierarchy.

All three run on the same platform, on a workforce that is already registered in it.

12 Why WHO

Your normative work, given a delivery system.

HEARTS and PhilPEN

HHP+ operationalizes HEARTS through PhilPEN 2025. Community screening is the entry point to both — and the one link with no standardized, maintained competency pathway behind it.

The CHW guideline, delivered

WHO's guidance on optimizing community health worker programmes calls for competency-based training, certification, supportive supervision and workforce data. This platform is the mechanism for all four.

Digital health, done properly

A government-owned digital public good on open standards — country-led, institutionally housed, and built to outlast the engagement that funds it.

WHO has already paid for the clinical content. This is what makes it permanent, current and national.

13 The Ask

What we're asking WHO to build with us.

APW financing

Fund Phase 2 build-out and Year-1 rollout as an Agreement for Performance of Work — the same instrument already in use for the BHW training package.

Technical validation

WHO technical review of the digitized curriculum against HEARTS and PhilPEN 2025 — the same review point the training APW already defines in Output 3.

Normative alignment

Position the platform's certification pathway as a country implementation of WHO's community health worker competency recommendations.

Convening

WHO's standing with DOH, BLHSD and CHD Region VI to carry this from a regional training package to a national platform commitment.

Ownership of the platform, its source code, its data and all published content remains with DOH throughout and after, under a Memorandum of Agreement executed before implementation.

15 Every Gap, Closed

The APW trains them. This is what keeps them trained.6 closed

G1
Frozen contentUpdate once, everywhere, the same day

Content lives in a versioned, DOH-approved Knowledge Base with named owners and review-due dates. A PhilPEN change is an edit, not a reprint.

G2
Cascade decayNo retelling — every BHW gets the original

Each BHW takes the same modules directly and is assessed individually against the same rubric. Quality stops depending on who taught whom.

G3
Thin coverageOnboarding, not roadshows

Activation of an already-profiled workforce through the same CHD and LGU channels that delivered nationwide profiling in a year.

G4
No route to nationalThe platform is the mechanism

Region VI is the proof-of-concept, not the boundary. The architecture was designed from the outset to serve the full national base.

G5
One competency, onceA curriculum engine, not a curriculum

Courses, quizzes, assessment and certification are generic capabilities. HHP+ is module one; ttCF and the rest are content and approval.

G6
Paper competenceThe same rubric, scored and queryable

The 29-criterion return demonstration digitized and auto-scored against the identical 87-point standard, issuing a QR-verified certificate on the national registry.
