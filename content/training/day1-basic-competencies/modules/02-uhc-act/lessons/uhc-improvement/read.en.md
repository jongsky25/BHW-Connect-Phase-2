## [section-6] Propose it, and talk it through first

On two visits, BHW Vlanche hears residents give different answers about whom to approach with a health station question. “Who should I ask?” says Mang Ernesto. Vlanche notes the question without names or personal details. This does not establish that the whole system is wrong. It identifies something to clarify with a fellow BHW and the midwife before proposing a change.

## [section-7] The first small step

Vlanche proposes: “On my visits next week, could we use one short message about whom to ask?” The starting point is small: one message during scheduled visits, rather than a whole campaign. It is still a proposal. Before using it, she needs to confirm the information, discuss it with the midwife, and check that the task fits her role and time. The proposal may change after that discussion.

## [improvement-confirm] Confirm the facts and listen

Vlanche asks the midwife: “Who is the current contact for this kind of question? Do we already have a message?” There may be a new schedule or local policy she does not yet know about. She listens to the explanation and her fellow BHW’s suggestion. If the contact or message is unconfirmed, she holds it back and asks the responsible person to clarify it. A BHW does not change services or policy on their own.

## [improvement-plan] Make the plan concrete

In this example, they agree on a plan after the midwife confirms the contact and message. Vlanche will explain it during her scheduled visits next week. She will use a small guide checked by the midwife; the plan needs no new campaign or unagreed service. On Friday, they will review questions that remain unclear. Write down the action, the responsible person and required agreement, the time and materials available, and when to review the result.

## [improvement-feedback] Try it and review what happened

During the agreed trial, Vlanche asks after explaining: “Who would you approach with this kind of question?” She notes how many people can name the correct contact and what questions remain, without personal details. On Friday, she and the midwife review the notes and feedback. If the message is unclear, they revise it before trying again. A few answers are a small indicator; they do not prove that the whole system or everyone’s health has improved.

## [improvement-check] Choose a workable plan

You have an idea to make information clearer during visits. Before starting, you need a confirmed contact and message, the right agreement, and feasible time and materials. Afterwards, you need to check whether people understood it and what still needs adjustment. Choose the plan with a small first step and a clear review with the health team. In your barangay, what is one real gap, whom should you discuss it with, and what would you clarify first?
