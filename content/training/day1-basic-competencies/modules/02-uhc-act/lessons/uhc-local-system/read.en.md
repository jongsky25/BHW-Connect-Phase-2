## [section-5] A recurring question in the barangay

In this fictional situation, Mang Ernesto asks BHW Vlanche: “Where can we get accurate information about the barangay health activity?” Other residents are also confused by the different information they have heard.

Vlanche listens. She does not immediately blame anyone or announce a new policy. She notices the recurring question and asks herself: “What have I actually observed, and who in the health team can confirm the information?”

The manual describes provincial and city health boards as partners in the local system, alongside stronger BHW skills and intensified health promotion. Vlanche will identify her own contribution and its boundaries.

## [local-system-board] Health board and health team: distinct roles

Under the UHC Act, Provincial and City Health Boards oversee and coordinate integration of health services into province-wide and city-wide health systems. They also have duties for the Special Health Fund and supervision of facilities and health personnel within the law’s scope.

The Local Government Code also establishes local health boards in provinces, cities and municipalities, with budget-proposal and advisory functions for the sanggunian. Do not treat all boards as one body or assume every place follows the same process.

A BHW does not automatically sit on or lead a board. Vlanche asks the midwife or designated health-team contact who should receive her observation and how to share it locally.

## [local-system-observation] A clear, careful observation

Vlanche prepares a short summary: “In conversations this week, residents have asked where to get current information about the health activity. They have heard different information. I have not yet confirmed the correct details.”

She separates what she actually heard from guesses about the cause. This summary does not need names, diagnoses or other details that identify residents. She does not put a private conversation in a public post.

Her question to the appropriate health-team contact is specific: “Who can confirm the information, and how can I share the suggestion to make the message clearer?”

## [local-system-promotion] Health promotion that responds to the question

Recall Module 1: as a barangay-level Health Education and Promotion Officer, or HEPO, a BHW helps share accurate health information and encourage community participation. Intensified health promotion is one of the changes described in the manual.

Instead of repeating an unconfirmed announcement, Vlanche asks the health team for current material and confirmation. She uses a simple explanation suited to the residents’ questions and the language they understand.

She can say: “Let us first confirm the current information with the health team.” Once it is confirmed, she explains it, invites questions, and asks the resident to describe their understanding in their own words.

## [local-system-feedback] Skills, suggestions and follow-up

In this example, Vlanche rehearses her summary with the midwife. The midwife helps her make the question clearer and remove assumptions. Vlanche suggests a clearer explanation using information the health team can confirm.

She asks: “Who can decide on this suggestion, and when and how can I learn the response?” They discuss a locally agreed next step. She does not promise that the suggestion is approved or has reached a board.

She will follow up with the agreed contact and share only the confirmed response with residents. This practices identifying an opportunity, discussing an idea with others, and following an agreed change.
