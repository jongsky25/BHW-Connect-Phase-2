# Facilitator notes — Module 2: The UHC Act of 2019

## Timing

TESDA Training Regulations set **3 hours** for the competency
*Contribute to Workplace Innovation* (facilitator-guide PDF 21).

Suggested split:

| Part | Time |
|---|---|
| Opening: "Has this happened to you before?" | 15 min |
| UHC's goal and the four changes | 30 min |
| Mini-quiz / video about the UHC Act | 30 min |
| Which of this has already happened for us (per-barangay discussion) | 40 min |
| The health board and the BHW's part | 20 min |
| Exercise: propose, discuss, first step | 45 min |

At Normal density, only about 15-20 minutes is actually readable
content — the rest is discussion and practice.

## Delivery script (follows the lesson's sections)

1. **Open with Mang Ernesto's question.** Before showing any content,
   ask the group: "Has something like this happened to you — being
   unsure about a new policy?" Let them share before introducing the UHC
   Act.
2. **Use the Learning Activity from the Facilitator's Manual**
   (mini-quiz, asking about UHC's changes, or a video about it) as a
   warm-up activity after the "UHC's Goal" section — it doesn't need to
   happen before the topic is introduced.
3. **On "Four Concrete Changes",** use the visual as a guide while
   naming each one — don't hand over the full list right away; ask
   first: "Which of these do you already know?"
4. **On "Which of this is already happening for us?",** make this a
   real group discussion, not an individual worksheet — answers differ
   by barangay, and that is where the module's most important part
   surfaces.
5. **On the health board part,** remind them it isn't just an abstract
   agency — a BHW's suggestion can reach it, through the midwife and the municipal or city health office.
6. **On the closing exercise,** give each participant time to discuss
   what they noticed with a partner before asking them for one concrete
   first step — don't let it stay theoretical.

## Named misconception

**"Everything is free now because of UHC."**

This is the most common misconception, and usually comes from a
half-heard piece of news, like Mang Ernesto's question. Don't correct it
by saying "you're wrong" — instead go back to the specifics: PhilHealth
now covers outpatient consultation, and every Filipino is now
automatically covered — that is a major step, but it does not mean
nothing will ever cost money again. The accurate explanation is more
persuasive than any correction.

## Discussion prompts

- "Of the four changes, which did you feel the soonest in your
  barangay? Which one hasn't arrived yet?"
- "Have you noticed a gap where UHC isn't yet fully carried out in your
  area? What is it?"
- "Who would you bring a suggestion to if you wanted to improve how UHC
  is carried out in your barangay?"

## Answer key to the retrieval check

1. **"Mang Ernesto heard that 'consultations are free now.'..."** →
   Consultation can be covered if he is registered with a PhilHealth primary care provider, but not every service is free. (It isn't true that everything is free, and it isn't true that nothing changed either.)
