## [ana] Before Apple starts work

Apple is a BHW at a fictional health station with a planned household visit. The new dialogue is an adaptation, not a DOH quotation. From a safe position, she notices a wet entrance, a container near its fill line and a hot route. She does not open the sharps box or touch its contents. “I will be careful” is not a sufficient report. Before starting, she identifies the hazard, who could be harmed, an immediate control and a reporting contact. The contact and safe route are still unknown; she needs to confirm them first.

## [inspect] Inspect the area and make a report

At the entrance, the wet floor could cause a BHW or visitor to slip. From a safe position, keep people away from the wet area and request correction from designated staff; confirm who that is. A blocked exit could delay escape. Do not lift the obstruction alone; ask responsible staff to address it and confirm a safe route. A sink without soap is an incomplete hand-hygiene station. Report the missing supply and request an approved alternative before a task requiring it; gloves do not replace handwashing. Record four fields: hazard, potential harm, immediate control and contact. “Be careful” is insufficient. Check the condition again; sending a report does not mean the hazard has been corrected.

## [scope-and-contact] Clarify scope and contact

Before assisting, Apple asks authorized staff: “Which tasks are within my training? Who should I notify about the wet entrance? What if that person cannot be reached?” She needs to confirm the name or role, contact method and local alternative contact. In practice, write “unknown—confirm” when an answer is missing. Do not invent a number or policy. Pause the related task when it exceeds training or essential protection is missing, and seek an authorized decision. For an actual exposure or injury, seek immediate medical evaluation and report through the local process; paperwork must not delay evaluation.

## [five-hazards] Recognize five groups

Use five groups in practice. First, sharps: a container near its fill line presents a puncture risk; do not touch the contents, and report to authorized staff. Second, infection exposure: missing soap means a complete hand-hygiene arrangement is needed before the related task. Third, musculoskeletal strain: a heavy bag on a long walk could cause discomfort; request help or adjust the load under the local plan. Fourth, stress or excessive workload: simultaneous tasks without breaks could cause fatigue and errors; clarify priorities and request support. Fifth, accidents: a wet floor or blocked exit could cause a slip or delay escape. Field heat is an additional consideration: confirm breaks, water and a safe route. These are possible effects, not diagnoses. PPE alone does not resolve every hazard.

## [practice] Report, receive feedback, retry

Draw five boxes and give a station or home-visit example for each hazard group. Choose one and complete hazard, potential harm, immediate control and contact. In a triad, the BHW reports, the partner answers using only the scenario card, and the observer checks the four fields. If the answer is only “be careful,” ask: “Where? What harm? What specific action? To whom and how will you report?” Repeat the report and add when the condition will be checked again. If the contact is unknown, explain how to confirm it. Working alone, write or speak using the same checklist; this is rehearsal, not trainer observation. Before the next duty, perform a short hazard check and confirm the contact.
