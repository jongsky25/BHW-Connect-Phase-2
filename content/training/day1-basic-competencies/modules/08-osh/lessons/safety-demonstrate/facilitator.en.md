## [purpose] Purpose

Using dummy equipment, follow the trainer-approved safety checklist and demonstrate immediate reporting and referral for a simulated needlestick. Apple (she/her) is a fictional BHW; the dialogue is an adaptation, not a DOH quotation. This lesson rehearses observable actions and referral; it does not grant certification.

## [time-materials] Time and materials

75 guided minutes: 5 opening + 15 trainer modelling + 35 observed simulations/feedback/retry + 12 incident/near-miss debrief + 8 transfer = 75. The four lessons retain 45 + 60 + 60 + 75 = 240 minutes. Measured six-screen self-study narration: 549.251 seconds Filipino and 509.127 seconds English. Allow a further 3–6 minutes for the decision and reflection; this is an authored allowance, not measured reading time. Optional story/replay is additional. The 75 minutes remain facilitated practice.

For 30 learners, prepare ten rotating triads, ten safe dummy stations or paper station layouts, labelled empty dummy boxes with trainer-verified fill lines, approved checklist copies, role/scenario cards, the exposure-response aid and pens. No real sharps, body fluids, chemicals or personal records. Accessible spoken/paper practice is available; physical demonstration still needs trainer observation.

## [prepare] Prepare

An authorized clinical trainer must approve container position/replacement cues, hand hygiene/PPE items, reporting contact and backup, evaluation route, and local pause/resume rules before running the clinical demonstration. Write verified contacts on the setup card; leave unknowns explicitly unconfirmed. Do not improvise a PPE sequence, treatment, emergency number or benefit entitlement. Inspect each station for safe dummy-only materials. If clinical review is unavailable, use a nonclinical paper reporting rehearsal and record physical demonstration as not observed.

Invite a learner who wants reading assistance to work with a reader. Tell learners how to say “Pause.” Prepare an observation log with fictional practice IDs only: attempt, exact action/words, prompt, correction, retry, trainer seen/not observed. Peer feedback is a separate record.

## [opening] Opening — 5 minutes

0:00–2:00: Read: “Apple reports a simulated used-needle injury and has no symptoms. What happens first?” Ask everyone for a spoken or written response without revealing the quiz.
2:00–5:00: Ask: “Does no symptom mean safe? Must a form be finished before evaluation?” Expected: no; wash with soap and water, report immediately, obtain immediate qualified evaluation. Explain dummy-only practice and observation limits.

## [steps] Trainer modelling — 15 minutes

0:00–3:00: Inspect the dummy station aloud. Prompt: “Which items or local rules are not yet verified?” Pause if critical conditions are missing.
3:00–8:00: Model container position, fill line/replacement cue, and only the reviewed local hand hygiene/PPE checklist. Never touch box contents or use real sharps. Gloves do not replace hand hygiene. Model comfortable chair/bag adjustment without painful stretching.
8:00–12:00: Model fictional exposure: say wash with soap and water, report immediately to the verified simulated contact, and arrange immediate evaluation via the verified pathway. Ask the contact to repeat the route; document later. No real call, wound or medication.
12:00–15:00: Model a deliberately verbal delay (“I will finish forms first”), ask learners to say “Pause,” explain the delay, and replay the correct reporting/evaluation sequence. Do not physically model an unsafe act.

## [expected-answers] Expected answers

Exposure report: “This is a simulated used-needle injury. I will wash with soap and water, report immediately and obtain immediate evaluation. No symptoms do not establish safety. I will not choose medication or finish forms first.”

Near miss: “At 10:00 at the dummy station a wet walkway nearly caused a slip; no injury or exposure occurred. I stopped using that route and told the trainer. The next control and resume decision remain unconfirmed.” Use only scenario facts; unknown contact/route is an honest gap to verify. A report does not prove a clinical outcome or benefits eligibility.

## [misconception] Misconceptions and unsafe errors

Stop the simulation immediately for real sharps/body fluids/chemicals, painful movement, an unreviewed critical step, or delay of evaluation. Remove the unsafe practice condition through the trainer; do not ask a learner to handle it. Record the exact omission and offer a safe retry after correction. If an actual incident occurs, end practice and follow the local real-incident care process.

Correct: gloves do not replace hand hygiene; PPE does not fix every hazard; no symptoms do not clear exposure; paperwork follows care; more volunteer hours are not the default safety control. Revisit the five source hazard groups during debrief: needlestick, musculoskeletal strain, accidents (including heat), workload/stress, and infection (including vectors). This is rehearsal of actions, not new treatment advice.

## [practice] Practice — 35 minutes

Ten triads work simultaneously. First 24 minutes: three 8-minute rotations so every learner demonstrates once. Per rotation: 0:00–2:00 safe dummy attempt; 2:00–4:00 precise peer feedback; 4:00–6:00 safe retry; 6:00–8:00 record actual actions, unknowns and swap roles. Roles: demonstrator / simulated contact / observer. The observer says “Pause” for a verbal delay or unsafe condition.

Final 11 minutes: one trainer can directly observe three selected learners, three minutes each (1:30 attempt, 0:30 feedback, 1:00 retry), with two minutes total for movement/logging. Sample across the room and include a learner who needed a prompt; do not claim all 30 were seen. Only these three can have a trainer-observed rating if critical steps were visible. The other 27 remain not observed unless another qualified trainer actually observes them. Additional observation requires separately scheduled time; three minutes may be insufficient, in which case record incomplete rather than pass.

Debrief — 12 minutes: 4 minutes compare near miss and exposure; 4 minutes separate immediate care from later paperwork; 4 minutes ask “Which fact, contact, route or control must we verify?” Use the fictional scenario cards.

Transfer — 8 minutes: 3 minutes write contact/backup/route gaps; 3 minutes plan verification with the authorized supervisor; 2 minutes record retry/observation appointments without promising readiness. A solo paper/spoken rehearsal can prepare reporting but cannot substitute for physical demonstration.

## [answer-key] Answer key

Keep all three original choices and correct index 0.
1. Wash, report immediately and obtain immediate medical evaluation. Correct: washing alone is insufficient; immediate reporting and evaluation are required.
2. Wash and wait until the visits are finished. Unsafe: waiting delays assessment; no symptoms do not establish safety.
3. Choose medication from available supplies. Unsafe: a qualified provider decides assessment and treatment.

After any choice, show all rationales and the corrected fictional ending: Apple demonstrates safely under trainer observation and rehearses the immediate reporting/evaluation pathway; documentation follows care. No medical outcome, treatment or actual local agreement is claimed. Clicking an answer does not establish competence.

## [observe] Observe

Use the single objective_index 0 indicator and its six bilingual level entries in competency.json. Watch actual actions: safe dummy setup/position/fill-line replacement cue; reviewed hand hygiene/PPE; immediate simulated reporting/evaluation; documentation after care; verification of unknown contacts/routes. Record critical steps done / prompted / not yet, exact evidence, and retry evidence.

Ready: independently demonstrates all reviewed critical steps and the immediate reporting/evaluation pathway. Needs practice: safe attempt requiring a prompt or incomplete clarification; give a specific correction and observe a retry. Not yet: unsafe critical omission, real sharps, or delayed evaluation; pause and correct before retry. “Not observed” is an evidence state outside the rubric, never a passing level. No attendance-based pass or new certification rule.

## [support] Support

Read the scenario aloud or use the learner’s preferred language. Offer a paper layout and a sentence scaffold: “I will ___, report to ___, obtain evaluation at ___; ___ is not yet verified.” For a weaker attempt, identify one missed step, model it safely, ask the learner to explain it, then observe a fresh attempt. Record prompts and changes. Respect mobility needs; do not require painful movement. Spoken or written reporting can support practice; arrange a safe physical observation with the trainer before judging the demonstration objective.
