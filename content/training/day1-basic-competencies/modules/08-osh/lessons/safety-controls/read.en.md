## [sharps] A needle is not ordinary waste

This is a fictional rehearsal. Apple, a BHW, selects controls; the new dialogue is an adaptation, not a DOH quotation. She notices the fill line of a nearly full sharps box without opening it or touching its contents. She says, “Pause the task that needs this container. Who is authorized to replace it under the local procedure?” The contact is unknown and must be verified, not guessed. The trained user places a used needle or lancet directly into the proper sharps container at the point of use. Do not recap, bend, hand-pass, compress the contents or use an ordinary bin. Follow the fill line and local replacement procedure. For loose sharps, keep others away and obtain trained assistance; never pick them up by hand. Gloves do not authorize continuing without the proper container.

## [infection] Choose controls for the hazard

Apple separates cards for hand hygiene, ventilation, waste and bite prevention. Infection routes differ: exposure may involve blood or body fluids, respiratory particles, or mosquito bites. Select prevention for the task and local protocol; one item does not solve every route. Controls include hand and respiratory hygiene, ventilation, safe waste handling and task-appropriate PPE. Gloves do not replace hand hygiene. For mosquitoes, prevent bites and address breeding sites; this is not equivalent to merely wearing gloves. For cleaning chemicals, follow labels and facility instructions; never mix them or invent a dilution. Apple asks, “Which route does this control? What still needs verification?” If essential protection is missing or the task exceeds training, pause the affected task and obtain authorized correction.

## [body] Protect the body during work

The bag is heavy in the fictional example and the task involves repeated bending. Apple arranges the chair and supplies within easier reach. She says, “I need lifting assistance; I will not continue a painful movement.” Reduce heavy carrying and repeated bending, plan breaks and position changes, and use comfortable footwear and supportive seating. Ask for carrying assistance and report pain; obtain assessment if needed. Do not force painful stretches or endure pain as proof of diligence. For a hot walk, verify opportunities for rest and heat avoidance under the local plan. Gloves do not fix a heavy bag or supplies out of reach. Choose changes to the work and surroundings; verify assistance before resuming unsafe carrying.

## [workload] Time and capacity have limits

Apple is asked to make an extra evening visit while already exhausted. The route on the card is dark and has not been verified as safe. She tells the coordinator, “This is what I can do within agreed hours. I am exhausted and the route is uncertain. Who can arrange another schedule, assistance or a safer way?” Request reassignment and rest; verify the response and safer alternative before continuing the affected visit. A new schedule or companion has not yet been agreed in this example. Clear workload limits and support control stress; a safe route and avoiding threats control accident risk. PPE is insufficient for both. Extra volunteer hours are not the default solution. Reporting risk and asking for help are compatible with caring for others.

## [practice] Try it, then repeat

With Apple, match five pairs: sharps–proper containment and trained handling; infection–route- and task-appropriate prevention, including bite prevention; body pain–work adjustment, breaks and assistance; stress–agreed workload and support; accidents–a safe route and avoiding threats. Vector control belongs within the infection group while retaining its own concept coverage. In ten triads, rotate as BHW, contact and observer. Everyone explains five pairs and answers, “What pauses? Which contact must be verified? What correction is needed before resuming?” Attempt, receive specific feedback, then retry. Correct two mismatches: gloves for a heavy bag, and extra hours for an exhausted BHW. Alone, speak or write before comparing with the answer key and retrying. Peer feedback or an unobserved attempt is not a staff-observed pass. Check one control before the next duty.
