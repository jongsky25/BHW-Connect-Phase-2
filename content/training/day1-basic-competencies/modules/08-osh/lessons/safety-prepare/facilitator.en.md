## [purpose] Purpose

Build a before-work checklist for station work and field visits covering supplies, route, reporting contact and explicit pause conditions.

Lesson 1.8.3 prepares and verifies readiness. Hazard identification, control selection and trainer-observed physical demonstration retain their separate lesson boundaries.

## [time-materials] Time and materials

60 facilitated minutes: 5 opening + 10 modelled readiness check + 25 rotating checklist/briefing/feedback/retry + 12 combined-hazard debrief + 8 transfer. The four lesson allocations remain 45 + 60 + 60 + 75 = 240 minutes. This is guided practice, not measured self-study or certification.

Self-study timing awaits actual complete new narration; optional story and replays are additional. Prepare bilingual A4 cards, five-field checklists, verification prompts, role cards, pause/contact aid, pens and dummy equipment without needles, chemicals or body fluids.

## [prepare] Prepare

For 30 learners, set up ten triads. Assign BHW, contact-or-partner and observer roles; rotate all three. Confirm current local task scope, hand-hygiene/PPE requirements, appropriate container, incident/referral contact, route/buddy rules and pause/resume authority with an authorized clinical trainer before demonstrations. Mark unresolved local rules as awaiting verification. No real household addresses or patient records.

Read fictional cards aloud if needed. Keep follow-up and answer cards covered initially. Explain that a requested correction is not a verified correction. An unavailable clinical reviewer does not permit improvising procedures. Use discussion and dummy equipment; defer unverified demonstrations.

## [opening] Opening — 5 minutes

Ask: “There is no soap and a threat on the route. Which task pauses, whom do you contact and what remains unknown?” Allow 1 minute of private thought, 2 minutes of triad discussion and 2 minutes for two brief responses. Do not reveal the answer key. State that clicking a quiz or attending is not observed competence.

## [steps] Modelled steps — 10 minutes

Minutes 5–7: read the fictional station/route card; ask “What is within Apple’s task scope?” Minutes 7–10: model filling all five fields and reporting missing soap and the threatened route. Minutes 10–13: reveal the follow-up card; distinguish soap present and dry entrance checked from route/contact unconfirmed. Minutes 13–15: model repeating the role-play agreement—mock briefing first, affected visit deferred—without claiming the threat vanished.

Ask: “What evidence changed ready/not-ready? Is a nod proof of agreement? What do you do when there is no contact response?” Expected: record unknown, pause affected work, seek the authorized decision and recheck. Do not demonstrate injections, chemical mixing or real sharps handling.

## [expected-answers] Expected answers

Five fields include scope, supplies/protection, route/weather, contact/return time and pause conditions. At first, handwashing supplies are incomplete and the route is threatened: pause affected work and coordinate. After the follow-up card, soap and dry entrance are verified for the specified mock task. Visit route/contact remain unconfirmed. The role-play alternative is a mock station briefing and deferred visit, not proof that all work is safe.

Five hazard groups: biological (hand-hygiene gap), chemical (unknown chemical), physical (slip/heat), ergonomic (heavy bag), psychosocial (queue pressure). Retain vector/heat planning for visits. Essential protection gaps and work beyond training pause; safe changes require the appropriate decision. Extra unpaid hours and PPE alone are insufficient.

## [misconception] Misconception and unsafe errors

“I wrote a full supplies list, so I can start.” Ask for verification evidence, contact and pause conditions. “I reported it, so it is fixed.” Ask what was actually checked. “A buddy makes any route safe.” Recheck the threat and local protocol.

If a learner proposes entering the threat, proceeding without essential protection, inventing authorization or delaying medical evaluation for paperwork: stop the rehearsal, identify the unsafe statement, return to the affected-work pause/contact step, provide a prompt and require a revised briefing. Record not yet for the unsafe attempt; record the retry separately. Do not infer infection or prescribe treatment.

## [practice] Rotating practice — 25 minutes

Minutes 15–17: read and allocate roles. Minutes 17–38: three 7-minute rotations, each 4-minute attempt + 2-minute specific peer feedback + 1-minute retry. Minutes 38–40: transitions/collection (2 minutes). All ten triads work concurrently. Each learner fills all five fields and briefs ready/not-ready evidence, proposed alternative and unknowns. Reveal the follow-up during each attempt; do not improvise it.

One trainer can sample one complete 7-minute attempt/feedback/retry sequence per rotation: at most three directly observed learners, not 30. Select one learner in each of three different triads in advance. Record everyone else as not observed by trainer; peer records remain practice. Arrange further observation separately if required. Do not mark unobserved learners passed.

Minutes 40–52 (12-minute debrief): 3 minutes pause/change/authorize sorting; 4 minutes five-group/heat/vector discussion; 3 minutes compare evidence versus unknowns; 2 minutes correct one unsafe proposal. Minutes 52–60 (8-minute transfer): 3 minutes repeat a corrected briefing, 3 minutes identify local procedures/contacts needing confirmation, 2 minutes record the next verification step. Physical demonstration in 1.8.4 still needs actual trainer observation.

## [answer-key] Answer key — reveal after decisions

1. Continue alone to finish quickly: unsafe; the threat remains.
2. Avoid the threat and arrange an alternative with the supervisor: correct; confirm conditions before departure.
3. Change the route without telling anyone: unsafe; contact/return information becomes inaccurate.

First: Continuing alone still enters the threat; the target does not justify it. Second: Avoid the threat and arrange an alternative with the supervisor; conditions still need confirmation before departure. Third: Not reporting a new route leaves the contact without accurate location and return-time information. In the fictional role-play, the agreed alternative is a mock station briefing and deferral of the affected visit. The new route and visit contact are unconfirmed: the visit is not ready and the threat is not assumed to have vanished. Repeat the agreement and verify before starting.

## [observe] Observe actual actions/products

Completes five fields; identifies the affected work to pause, safe alternative and authorized contact; includes heat, route and return time; records evidence for ready/not-ready and honest unknowns; repeats the briefing after feedback.

Ready: Completes five fields; identifies the affected work to pause, safe alternative and authorized contact; includes heat, route and return time; records evidence for ready/not-ready and honest unknowns; repeats the briefing after feedback. Does this without prompting; never marks an unverified condition ready.
Needs practice: Makes a safe pause decision but misses a field, contact or verification evidence. Complete it with prompting and repeat the briefing.
Not yet: Continues an unsafe route or unprotected task simply to finish the target.

For each observed attempt, record learner practice code, observer role, exact words/product, prompting, feedback, retry and remaining unknowns. Use done / prompted / not yet; label absent trainer observation explicitly. A single sampled performance does not establish cohort readiness. No attendance-based passing or new certification rule.

## [support] Support and solo alternative

Offer the preferred language, read cards aloud and permit writing or speaking. Scaffold a weaker attempt with five blank fields: “Which work? Which gap? Which contact? Which evidence? What pauses?” Keep unknown contact as unknown; do not supply a made-up number. Require the learner to fill the gap and repeat the briefing.

Alone: complete the same card with the key covered, compare all five fields and ready/not-ready evidence, correct one gap and retry aloud or on paper. Record self-review as practice. Solo speech cannot replace the actual trainer-observed physical demonstration in 1.8.4.
