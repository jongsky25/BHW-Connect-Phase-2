## [station-preparation] Prepare the area and equipment

This is a fictional exercise. Apple, a BHW, is preparing for station duty and visits. The new dialogue is an adaptation, not a DOH quotation. The cards contain no real patient or address.

Apple: “What is my task, and which parts are within my training?” Clarify this with the authorized supervisor before starting. At the mock station, the entrance is slippery and the handwashing area has no soap. Keep people away from the hazard and report what needs correction. Do not mark it corrected without verification.

Check supplies, an appropriate sharps container if required for an authorized task, hand-hygiene supplies and PPE under the current local procedure. Only dummy equipment is used here; no needles or body fluids. The checklist does not authorize injections or handling real sharps. Gloves do not replace hand hygiene, and PPE does not resolve every hazard.

Apple: “Essential protection is still missing. I will pause the affected task, obtain a decision and recheck the condition.” Pause and escalate work beyond your training. Also prepare the incident contact.

## [field-preparation] Plan a safe field visit

On the fictional route card, the path from the mock station crosses Bridge A to meeting point B. A threat has been reported at Bridge A; the safety of another route is unknown. The map contains no real homes. Apple: “I will not enter a threatened route just to finish the visit list.”

Before leaving, check weather, heat, slippery paths, dogs, vectors such as mosquitoes and possible threats. Plan drinking water, shade and rest, with locally appropriate vector prevention. Confirm the route, reporting contact and expected return time. Use a buddy system when local protocol requires one; having a companion does not guarantee safety.

If the contact or safe route is unknown, record the unknown and pause the affected visit while coordinating an alternative with the supervisor. Unconfirmed conditions are not ready. Communicate any agreed changes to the route and return time.

Report accidents, bites, exposures or illness promptly and seek appropriate immediate medical evaluation or referral under the procedure. Finishing the visit list or paperwork is not a prerequisite. A qualified provider decides assessment and treatment; absence of symptoms does not clear an exposure.

## [combined-hazards] When hazards combine

At the mock station, clients are arriving, the room is hot and essential protection for one task is missing. Separate the decisions: what must pause, what can safely change, and what requires the authorized supervisor? Do not automatically continue or stop every activity without examining it.

Apple: “The task without essential protection stays paused. Could we change the queue and schedule while awaiting your decision?” This is a proposal, not real authorization. Consider staff capacity, client flow, heat and rest. Extra unpaid BHW hours are not the sole safety control. Targets do not justify work beyond training.

Check five groups: biological, chemical, physical, ergonomic and psychosocial hazards. Examples are a hand-hygiene gap, an unknown chemical, a slippery entrance or heat, a heavy load, and queue pressure. This does not authorize chemical mixing, clinical procedures or painful lifting. Retain vector and heat prevention in the visit plan.

Record the proposed alternative, who must decide and what remains unconfirmed. Reporting a gap starts correction; actual readiness still needs verification before work begins.

## [verify-readiness] Verify before starting

Apple: “Someone said it was corrected. What have we seen or confirmed?” Record the condition, remaining action, responsible contact, verification evidence and ready or not-ready status. “Correction requested” differs from “corrected and checked.”

On the fictional follow-up card, soap is present at the mock station and the dry entrance was checked in the role-play. Those two conditions are ready for the specified mock task. However, an alternative route and visit contact remain unconfirmed. The visit is not ready; this does not mean the threat at Bridge A has vanished. Without a follow-up card or contact response, record not verified.

Only within the role-play, the supervisor confirms an alternative: conduct the mock station briefing, defer the affected visit and check together again before departure. Apple: “I will repeat the agreement: briefing first; no threatened route. The new route and contact are unconfirmed, so the visit still needs a decision.” Confirm the response; silence or a nod is not proof of agreement.

In real work, follow the authorized local pause/resume procedure. The story does not replace local authorization. Pause and report again if the hazard returns or new unknowns arise.

## [practice] Try it, then repeat

Complete the five checklist fields: task and scope; supplies and protection; route and weather; contact and return time; pause conditions. Use the fictional station/route card, verification card and role instructions. Say: “There is no soap and a threat on the route.” Identify the affected work, proposed safe alternative and contact needing confirmation.

In a triad, rotate as BHW, contact-or-partner and observer. Each rotation has a 4-minute attempt, 2-minute feedback and 1-minute retry. Three rotations total 21 minutes; 2 minutes to read the card and 2 for transitions bring the activity to 25 minutes. Observer: name the exact gap, for example “Return time is missing,” and ask for another briefing after it is completed.

Alone, write or speak the same response with the answer key covered. Check the five fields and ready/not-ready status, correct one gap and retry. Use reading support as needed. Peer or self-feedback is practice; an attempt the trainer did not observe is not observed, not passed.

Before the next real visit, confirm the local contact, route and pause/resume procedure. This checklist or solo briefing does not replace actual trainer-observed demonstration in lesson 1.8.4.

## [check] Choose the next step

Revisit Apple’s fictional route card: a threat is reported at Bridge A and another route is unconfirmed. A complete supplies list is insufficient. What is the safe next step?

Choose an answer and explain why before viewing feedback. Then compare it with your own briefing. Clicking an answer or watching a story does not establish demonstrated competence.
