# Facilitator guide — Occupational Safety and Health

## Timing and preparation
Weighting: 4 hours, 240 minutes—30 hazard walk-through, 35 preparation/protocol, 45 dummy sharps/exposure reporting, 45 hand hygiene/PPE, 25 ergonomics/field planning, 25 workload/incident practice, 35 return demonstrations/retrieval. Add breaks. Prepare local protocols, reporting/referral contacts, dummy sharps and a display container, hand-hygiene supplies and task-appropriate PPE. Use no live needles, used lancets or body fluids.

## Delivery script
1. **Ana’s day / Prepare:** Ask, “Who could be harmed, and what control is needed before starting?” Complete the area checklist and identify the supervisor/contact.
2. **Needles / If stuck:** Demonstrate dummy disposal and the fill line. Rehearse a reporting call: wash with soap and water, report immediately and seek immediate medical evaluation. Do not teach self-prescribed prophylaxis. Separate paperwork from obtaining urgent help.
3. **Controls for the hazard:** Demonstrate hand hygiene and PPE from the approved local protocol. Have learners distinguish sharps, respiratory and mosquito controls. Gloves do not replace hand hygiene.
4. **Body / Visits / Capacity:** Adjust a chair and bag; plan route, water, shade, contact and workload boundaries. A simple movement break is not an exercise prescription for an injured person.
5. **Work area / Demonstration / Near miss:** Each learner demonstrates a control and reports an incident card. In the deep scenario, identify work to pause and the supervisor decision needed.

## Misconception: “No blood or symptoms means no action”
Correct calmly: symptoms cannot be the only basis for decisions after exposure. Return to immediate reporting and evaluation.

## Discussion and assessment
Who could be harmed by sharps in an ordinary bin? What if the proper container is missing? How can excessive workload be reported? Use the four competency indicators; stop, correct and repeat an unsafe demonstration before marking competence.


## Retrieval-check answer key

- **A needle is not ordinary waste:** Arrange replacement according to the fill line and local protocol before overfilling. — Compressing waste and using ordinary bins put the next handler at risk.

- **If stuck, act immediately:** Wash, report and obtain immediate medical evaluation. — Absence of symptoms after exposure does not establish absence of risk.
