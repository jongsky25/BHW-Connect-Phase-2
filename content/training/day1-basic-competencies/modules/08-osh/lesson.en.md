## [scenario/core] Before Ana starts work {m8.requirements}

Ana, 33, will assist at the health station before household visits. The entrance floor is wet, the sharps box is nearly full and a hot walk lies ahead. “I will be careful” is not enough. She identifies each hazard, who might be harmed and the control needed. She checks the local protocol and confirms the reporting contact and tasks within her training with the midwife.

:::takeaway
Workplace safety starts by identifying hazards and local requirements before the task.
:::

## [concept/core] Prepare the area and equipment {m8.prepare, m8.practice}

Arrange for the slippery entrance to be corrected and keep people away from the hazard. Check for the correct sharps container, hand-hygiene supplies and PPE required for the task. Know the safe route and incident contact. If essential protection is missing or a task exceeds training, pause and escalate to the supervisor. Gloves or a mask do not replace removing a hazard when removal is possible.

:::visual 1
:::takeaway
Prepare controls and equipment; report gaps before undertaking unsafe work.
:::

## [concept/core] A needle is not ordinary waste {m8.sharps}

The trained user places a used needle or lancet directly into the proper sharps container at the point of use. Do not recap, bend or hand-pass it. Keep it out of ordinary bins and never compress a nearly full container’s contents. Follow its fill line and local replacement procedure. If loose sharps are found, keep others away and obtain trained assistance; do not pick them up by hand.

:::takeaway
Safe sharps disposal requires the right container and trained handling.
:::

:::

## [practice/core] If stuck, act immediately {m8.exposure}

After a used-needle injury, wash the wound with soap and water. Report immediately to the midwife or designated supervisor and obtain immediate medical evaluation from a qualified provider. Do not wait until the end of the shift or for symptoms. A clinician decides on assessment and any post-exposure treatment. Document the event through the local incident process so the cause can also be addressed.

:::takeaway
Report and obtain immediate evaluation after a needlestick; washing alone is insufficient.
:::
:::check

> Absence of symptoms after exposure does not establish absence of risk.
:::

## [concept/core] Choose controls for the hazard {m8.infection, m8.vectors}

Infections do not all spread through the same route. During client contact, follow hand hygiene, respiratory hygiene, ventilation and task-appropriate PPE under local protocols. Gloves do not replace hand hygiene. Mosquito exposure calls for bite prevention and attention to breeding sites. Keep the area clean and handle waste safely. Do not mix cleaning chemicals or invent dilutions; follow labels and facility instructions.

:::takeaway
Infection prevention combines appropriate practices, equipment and surroundings.
:::

## [concept/core] Protect the body during work {m8.ergonomics}

Ana walks far and often stands for long periods. Plan breaks and changes of position, use comfortable footwear and a supportive chair. Arrange supplies to reduce repeated bending and heavy carrying; ask for lifting assistance. Report pain to the supervisor and seek assessment when needed. Enduring pain is not proof of dedication.

:::takeaway
Safer posture, breaks and sensible carrying are part of work preparation.
:::

## [concept/core] Plan a safe field visit {m8.accidents, m8.heat}

Before leaving, check weather, slippery paths, dogs and possible threats. Share the route and expected return time; use a buddy system when local protocols call for one. Carry drinking water and plan shade and rest during heat. Do not enter an unsafe situation; arrange an alternative. Report accidents, bites or illness promptly and follow the appropriate referral rather than finishing the household list first.

:::takeaway
A safe route and clear reporting contact matter as much as the visit list.
:::

## [concept/core] Time and capacity have limits {m8.stress}

Ana is asked to make an extra evening visit while already exhausted. Explain what can be done within agreed hours and what needs reassignment. Ask the midwife or coordinator for support and arrange rest. Clear goals and boundaries for volunteer duties help prevent overload. Reporting risk and asking for help are compatible with caring for others.

:::takeaway
Reporting excessive workload and setting boundaries are part of workplace safety.
:::

## [practice/standard] Inspect a work area

A fictional station has a wet floor, a blocked exit and no soap at the sink. For each, name the hazard, possible harm, immediate control and reporting contact. First keep people away from danger and seek the right assistance. A checklist that only says “be careful” is incomplete. Check that the problem is corrected before removing the warning.

:::takeaway
A checklist should lead to controls and follow-up, not merely count hazards.
:::

## [practice/standard] A safe return demonstration

Using dummy equipment, show where the sharps container belongs and when replacement is needed. Demonstrate hand hygiene and PPE use/removal following the trainer-approved local protocol. Practice uses no real used needles or body fluids. In a short posture exercise, adjust the chair and bag; avoid painful stretching. The facilitator observes actions rather than relying only on verbal answers.

:::takeaway
Competence is shown through a safe demonstration within the scope of training.
:::

## [practice/standard] Report a near miss too

Ana nearly slips but nobody is hurt. Report the hazard, time, location and control through the local process. After an incident, prepare available information for referral and any applicable benefits process, but paperwork must not delay urgent care. BHW insurance and benefits are not identical; confirm your applicable arrangements with the coordinator.

:::takeaway
Reporting a near miss creates a chance to prevent the next injury.
:::

## [practice/deep] When hazards combine

Clients arrive in a surge, the room is hot and some PPE is unavailable. As a group, identify tasks to pause, tasks that can be modified and who must decide. Consider staffing capacity and client flow; extra BHW hours should not be the only answer. Propose one safer alternative for supervisor approval.

:::takeaway
Combined hazards may require changing the work and obtaining the appropriate decision.
:::
