# Facilitator guide — Identifying Problems

## Timing and materials
Weighting: 3 hours, 180 minutes—20 problem/evidence, 35 Five Whys, 30 Rosario/problem tree, 30 scoring, 35 action plan, 20 reporting/retrieval. Add breaks. Use fictional case cards, paper, markers and the rubric; do not use an identifiable death review.

## Delivery script
1. **Three visits:** Ask, “What do we know, and what did we guess?” Write the problem without labeling a family.
2. **Every why:** Show the chain. Each answer needs a source or an explicit verification gap. Assign resident, BHW and health-center perspectives. Branch when causes differ rather than forcing one story.
3. **Diarrhoea / Rosario:** Use the brief lesson adaptation and, if needed, source workbook pp.48–51. Rosario is a child, not a pregnant woman. The ZFF worksheet uses branching cause analysis; Five Whys is the DOH guide’s overlay. Never treat historical treatment details as current advice.
4. **Priorities / Scoring:** Distinguish magnitude, seriousness, feasibility and urgency. Calculate 19, 17 and 16; ask what local evidence would justify those scores. Emergency referral does not wait for ranking.
5. **Solutions / Agreed work / Partners:** Compare two options. Write the activity, owner, resources, deadline and outcome measure. Ask, “Who must agree before we promise this?”
6. **Deep practice:** Give the partly unsuccessful trial and have learners revise an assumption. Present the tree and recommendation against the four indicators.

## Misconception: “Five questions mean we are done”
Explain that an arbitrary count cannot replace evidence and an actionable cause. Treat uncertainty as an invitation to consult the right person.

## Discussion and observation
Which cause can the BHW address, and which needs a partner? What evidence could change the ranking? Why is a completed meeting insufficient as an outcome? Collect the cause map, score sheet and action plan; give feedback on one assumption and have the learner repeat the recommendation.


## Retrieval-check answer key

- **Every why needs evidence:** Ask and check which costs and access barriers are involved. — Investigate the explanation with the household; the number of questions does not establish a root cause.

- **Match the solution to the cause:** Discuss a possible alternative time with the health center and households. — It addresses the cause while recognizing that the health center must agree to the change.

- **Try scoring priorities:** Review evidence, urgency and resources with the group. — A tie calls for discussion rather than an arbitrary ranking.
