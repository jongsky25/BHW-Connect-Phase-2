## [purpose] Purpose

Build a cause map with two possible branches and verification steps, without treating five questions alone as proof. Observe the map, an evidence-seeking question and correction, rather than attendance or a meeting count.

## [time-materials] Time and materials

50 guided minutes: 6 opening + 10 demonstration/source discussion + 18 attempts, feedback and retry + 10 debrief + 6 transfer. Module allocations remain 35 + 50 + 45 + 50 = 180 minutes. Ten triads for 30 participants, paper, pens and the four-page A4 practice kit. No actual resident record. Independent study estimate is pending measured new audio; optional story and replay are additional and do not replace guided practice.

## [prepare] Prepare

Print one four-page kit per triad, with the last page as a separate one-page task aid. Prepare cards A, B, C and the staff correction. Keep the historical DOH and ZFF cases separate from Carole’s fictional missed visits. Confirm any real access or local referral procedure with authorized staff before teaching it as a rule; those reviews remain pending.

## [opening] Opening

0–6 minutes. Ask: “The group has asked five whys and blames a mother. What evidence is missing?” Expected: the count proves nothing; ask the family and staff about evidence and other barriers. State that cards describe classroom facts, not local prevalence. Do not ask for personal disclosures.

## [steps] Demonstrate and debrief

6–16 minutes: 3 minutes model two reported branches using A and B; 3 minutes ask which source supports each arrow; 2 minutes reveal the staff card and remove “only one time offered”; 2 minutes contrast the manual systems example and Rosario’s environment/livelihood/service branches. Ask: “Does two times offered prove two times received?” Expected: no. Ask: “Does the schedule explain fare?” Expected: no.

16–34 minutes: three six-minute triad rounds as below. 34–44 minutes debrief: 3 minutes compare corrected maps, 3 minutes discuss all quiz rationales, 2 minutes identify an honest unknown, 2 minutes choose a proportionate verification step. Ask: “Which arrow changed, on whose evidence, and what remains unknown?” Expected: remove one time offered; retain resident report of one time received; cause and fare remain to check. 44–50 minutes transfer: 2 minutes plan a question/person or authorized record for each branch, 2 minutes arrange observation/retry for unseen attempts, 2 minutes restate evidence limits. Do not promise a schedule or service.

## [expected-answers] Expected answers

Card A reports childcare/timing; B reports insufficient fare that day; C has no reply. The staff card verifies only two offered times in the fictional practice schedule. It does not verify message receipt or the reason for a missed visit. Each branch needs a source, status and verification question/person or authorized record. Include the resident’s correction and preserve uncertainty.

## [misconception] Common errors

Five is a tool name, not a quota or proof. A report is not automatically a verified causal arrow. Silence, a nod or a facial expression is not agreement or motive. Avoid lazy, uncooperative or noncompliant as explanations. Rosario is a child aged one year and four months, not Carole or the mother. Historical medicines are not current treatment directions.

## [practice] Practice and retry

Ten triads: BHW, resident/staff, observer. Each six-minute round: 2 minutes first map and evidence-seeking question; 1 minute specific observer feedback; 2 minutes revision and retry with correction card; 1 minute record the exact action and rotate. Repeat three times so all 30 practise as BHW. Observer asks: “Which arrow lacks support?” The learner changes it rather than merely agreeing with feedback.

A single facilitator cannot directly observe all 30 full performances in 18 minutes. Sample one BHW’s question, two-branch map and correction in each triad across the rounds (about 90 seconds per triad, with three minutes for movement/notes). Record the exact portion seen and learner; the other 20 attempts are not observed, never passed. Arrange later direct observation or additional qualified observers. Peer feedback supports practice only.

## [answer-key] Answer key

First: five questions are not proof; do not stop investigating because of the count. Second: check the evidence and other barriers with the family and health team before acting. Third: a lecture may not address fare, income or access to services. In the fictional ending, Carole corrects the arrow: the practice schedule contains two times, but the resident reports receiving only one. Why remains unknown. Childcare and fare barriers are reported; their causes still need investigation. The map contains two attributed branches with verification steps, not one blamed person.

## [observe] Observe

One indicator at objective_index 0: produces two plausible branches with sources and evidence status; asks a verification question/person or authorized record for each; incorporates the resident’s perspective and corrects an unsupported arrow while preserving unknowns.

Ready: does this independently. Needs practice: with prompts, draws branches but misses a source/status/check or correction; scaffold and retry the missing part. Not yet: persists in a blaming unsupported explanation, invents certainty or cannot produce two branches after support. Not observed is a separate recording status, not a failure or pass. Record date, words/actions, portion observed and support; re-observe the revised attempt. Do not infer ability from video completion or quiz clicks.

## [support] Support and solo option

Offer large print, spoken or written responses, reading help and preferred language with the same observable standard. Scaffold with two empty branch boxes and reported/verified/to-check tokens, ask the learner to place the source before drawing an arrow, then fade prompts on retry. Without internet, read cards aloud. Alone: draw, ask a written question, read the staff correction, revise, and arrange later direct observation. No real personal record, upload or disclosure is needed.
