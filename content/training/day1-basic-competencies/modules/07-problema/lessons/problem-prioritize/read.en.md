## [criteria] Make priorities explicit

Carole is the BHW in this fictional classroom exercise. Three households reportedly missed scheduled visits. This is only a report; the reasons and the number affected across the barangay are unknown. The new dialogue is an authoring adaptation, not a DOH quotation.

For planning, score each problem from 1 to 5 on four criteria: magnitude or number affected; severity of the effects; feasibility with available resources; and urgency. Higher feasibility means more achievable, not harder to do. The report from three households is not enough to assign local scores.

Carole: “What supports each score? Who else should we hear from?” Add the four scores and discuss evidence with the group. A high total suggests a starting priority. Refer someone needing urgent care immediately under the appropriate referral procedure; do not wait for a planning score.

## [worked-scores] Try scoring priorities

These teaching-deck example rows are separate from the report about three households. They are fictional practice scores, not measurements of your barangay. The four scores are ordered: magnitude, severity, feasibility, urgency.

BHW training gap: 4 + 5 + 5 + 5 = 19.
Safe-water teaching gap: 4 + 3 + 5 + 5 = 17.
Household water-preparation knowledge gap: 3 + 3 + 5 + 5 = 16.

Carole: “In the first row, 4 + 5 = 9; add 5 = 14; add 5 = 19.” In the second: 7, 12, 17. In the third: 6, 11, 16. Thus 19 ranks first, 17 second and 16 third in this example. This does not establish such training gaps or risks locally. Before using a score in a real plan, obtain relevant counts, effects, available resources and reasons for urgency.

## [score-evidence] What supports the score?

Carole: “Three missed visits were reported. Does that immediately make magnitude a 4?” Resident partner: “Not yet. We do not know the overall count or whether the reasons are the same.” List the report and what remains unknown; do not fill gaps with guesses.

For each score, ask: Magnitude—how many people or households, over what period, and what is the source? Severity—what effects have supporting evidence? Feasibility—which people, time, materials and permissions are actually available? Urgency—why give this planning priority now? Silence, a nod or an expression does not prove agreement. Labels such as “lazy” or “uncooperative” are not evidence.

The 1–5 scores are ordered categories, not necessarily equal intervals. Addition is a simple equal-weight planning aid: all four criteria have equal weight in this example, but it is not a validated formula or clinical triage tool. Equal totals can hide different effects. Record the evidence, unknowns and verification questions; discuss these limits before deciding.

## [tie-and-urgent-care] Resolve a tie; proceed with urgent care

In a separate fictional tie exercise, Plan A and Plan B both score 17. Plan A clarifies visit information; staff time is available within the role-play. Plan B arranges transport; a vehicle and partner are unconfirmed. No real schedule or funding is promised. This is not a new scoring of the three deck rows.

Carole: “The totals match, but which resources are confirmed?” Partner: “There is time to clarify information for Plan A; transport for B is still a question.” Revisit evidence for each score and urgency. Correct an error with its supporting evidence. Record unknowns where evidence is missing. Ask each partner for reasons before agreeing. Do not choose solely by list order or a leader’s preference.

Planning and urgent referral are separate. If someone needs urgent care, proceed with referral under the appropriate procedure while planning discussion continues. Completing the table is not a prerequisite. This exercise teaches no new clinical triage rule.

## [practice] Try, give feedback, then repeat

Use the score/evidence cards, role cards, observer checklist and row-by-row tally aid. Calculate all three deck totals: 19, 17 and 16. Explain the ranking and why higher feasibility means more achievable. Give an evidence question for each criterion before applying scores locally.

On the separate tie card, Plans A and B both score 17. Discuss available staff time for A and unconfirmed transport for B; ask a partner for reasons and record the agreement and unknowns. Explain why urgent referral proceeds even if ranking is unfinished.

In a triad, rotate BHW/facilitator, resident-or-partner/recorder and observer. Each learner gets a 3-minute attempt, 1 minute of specific feedback and a 1-minute retry; three rotations plus 1 minute for transitions total 16 minutes. Correct an arithmetic or evidence error. Alone, write the answer with the key covered, then check and retry the error. Self-review is not direct assessment. At the next planning meeting, ask where the scores came from.
