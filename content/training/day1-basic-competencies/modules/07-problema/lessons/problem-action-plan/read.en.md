## [compare-solutions] Match the solution to the cause

In this fictional classroom exercise, three households were invited and only one attended. The scenario supplies verified barriers: visit hours conflict with work, and there is no available person to care for a child. These are not actual barangay data or findings supplied by another lesson. Carole is the BHW making a recommendation.

A resident says, ‘We knew about the invitation, but we had work and a child to care for.’ Carole compares two solutions: a poster alone, or discussion of a small schedule trial with households and the midwife. A poster may help with notice, but it cannot by itself resolve work and childcare barriers.

Ask: Which addresses the verified cause? Who has authority to decide? What time, people and costs are needed? A suitable time and childcare support remain unknown. Do not promise a new service, schedule or funding until the responsible authority and partners agree.

## [plan] Turn a proposal into agreed work

‘This is still a proposal,’ Carole says. Fill seven fields: cause; proposal; owner; resources; deadline; measure; review date. Keep agreement separately: who agreed, to which contribution, and what remains pending. Silence or a nod does not establish permission.

Example: cause—work and childcare; proposal—consult the three households this week and discuss one trial with the health team; owner—Carole for consultation, the responsible health team for the schedule decision; resources—paper, consultation time and staff time still requiring confirmation; deadline—discussion on Friday; measure—attendance among those invited, with reasons for missing visits; review—Monday after the agreed trial.

Pass on aggregated preferred times without unnecessary personal details. The trial date and resources still await agreement. If there is no agreement on Friday, return the question to the team; do not implement. Report whether barriers decreased, rather than only whether a meeting finished.

## [partners] Who should take part?

‘I can consult residents and summarize preferred times,’ Carole says. ‘We need the responsible health team’s decision on schedules and staffing.’ The midwife asks, ‘Who agreed, and is staff time allocated?’ The answer is, ‘Not yet confirmed; I will mark it pending.’ Not every task can belong to the BHW.

On the role map, separate BHW consultation and reporting, health-team review and decisions, and needs for the barangay or other agencies such as transport or water. If funding is needed, ask the responsible authority; do not promise it. Confirm each contribution before setting a trial.

Include affected residents, especially those who seldom attend meetings. Ask which consultation methods they can access; do not assume they refuse to participate. Return with a clear agreement or the decisions still pending. Follow authorized local access, privacy and referral processes; the actual local rules still require review.

## [review] When the first plan does not work

For this separate review exercise, the scenario supplies partner agreement to an earlier trial of an earlier time. This does not approve the new proposal. Before: three households invited, one attended. After: three invited again, one attended again—two still missed the visit. These are fictional counts, not a prevalence estimate.

Separate activity, output and outcome: the meeting is an activity; an agreed trial schedule and three delivered invitations are outputs; attendance of 1 out of 3 is the measured outcome. Attendance did not increase. The counts alone do not tell us why each household missed a visit or whether other barriers decreased.

‘An earlier time was insufficient,’ Carole says. ‘Let us recheck work, costs, childcare and notice.’ Do not label residents lazy or uncooperative. Record reports and unknowns; compare invitations with attendance before and after. Revise the assumption and propose a small change for renewed agreement. One trial does not establish that the whole problem is solved.

## [practice] Try it, then repeat

Use the fictional scenario: one of three invited households attended; work and childcare barriers are confirmed for this exercise; preferred times, staff availability, costs and agreement to a new trial remain unknown. Compare a poster and a schedule trial before choosing. Fill the seven plan fields and separate agreement status.

In a triad, assign a BHW, a resident or partner, and an observer. Give a one-minute recommendation. The partner asks, ‘Who decides? Which resources are confirmed? Who agreed? What will you measure if it fails?’ Identify unknowns; revise the plan using feedback and repeat the one-minute recommendation. Rotate roles so everyone rehearses as BHW.

Alone, write or speak the recommendation, answer the same questions, mark gaps and repeat. Bring the proposal to the team before implementation. Solo or peer practice does not automatically establish a pass; direct assessor observation is required. Watching a story or clicking a quiz is not competency assessment.

## [check] Choose the next step

Carole returns to the team: the meeting finished, but before and after the trial only one of three invited households attended. Two still missed visits. Choose the next step and explain why before viewing the feedback.

Compare your answer with your seven-field plan. Separate completed activity from outcome and agreement from a pending decision. The ending is an honest proposal and review plan, without a promise of a schedule, funding or resolution of the whole problem.
