## [trial] One change, an owner and a measure

Charlaine is a fictional BHW preparing a proposal to reduce pages reprinted because the wrong version was used. This remains a proposal: permission and team acceptance have not been confirmed. No real patient record is used.

On the trial card, record the problem, change, responsible person and replacement, baseline, quality measure, authorisation and review date. Her proposal is to check the approved handout before each print run. She proposes preparing the checklist herself and asking the midwife to verify the version; first ask whether these responsibilities are feasible and authorised. The proposed review is two weeks after an authorised start. While permission is unknown, mark it pending and do not start.

Count wrong-version reprints, all printed pages and sessions delivered. Also check whether handouts are correct and readable and whether service was delayed. Plan the review before starting: continue, revise or stop based on evidence and quality. A complete card does not guarantee a successful trial.

## [compare] Try a simple comparison

Use the fictional tally: wrong-version reprints were 20 out of every 100 printed pages before and 5 out of every 100 afterward. Both denominators are 100 pages. The rates are 20% and 5%; the difference is 15 per 100 pages, or 15 percentage points. These are practice tallies, not a report from a real BHS.

The relative reduction in reprints is (20 − 5) ÷ 20 = 75%. This is not a 75% reduction in total cost. Prices, other resource costs and the cause of the change are not supplied. Comparable session types and counts, correct handouts for everyone, delays and complaints have not been confirmed either. Record these as unknown.

Before choosing, use the same 100-page denominator and ask whether workload and quality are comparable. Patient names are unnecessary. Answer the question before opening the explanation and summary.

## [communicate] Share the proposal with colleagues

Charlaine says, “There are two versions in the drawer and some pages are being reprinted. Could we check which version is approved?” She describes the observation without blaming a person or claiming a proven cause. Show the proposal, proposed responsibilities and review date. Ask: “What is the barrier? Is this routine feasible? Who can verify the version?”

In this situation, the colleague printing handouts has no internet and cannot download the file then. Propose a controlled paper master from the approved version, with a clear version/date and a designated verifier. Separate outdated copies from the usable master under the approved routine. Ask for the colleague’s feedback and confirm the owner and replacement; do not invent agreement.

If the version or authorisation cannot be verified, do not use an unapproved copy. Report the barrier to the locally authorised contact and request correct material. Answer the question before viewing the explanation.

## [workload] When workloads differ

Five teaching sessions were delivered in one week and ten in the next. Total paper use may rise because the workload doubled. Those session counts alone cannot give a reprint rate: reprint and page counts for each week are also needed. If they are not supplied, record them as unknown; do not guess or borrow the separate 20/100 and 5/100 example.

For comparable session types, divide reprints by sessions delivered or compare reprints per 100 printed pages. Also compare correct versions, readable material, complaints, delays and services delivered. Raw totals may fall because services were canceled; that does not prove a better process.

Protect water for handwashing, clinical single-use rules, cold-chain and critical systems, required ventilation, accessible teaching and timely or urgent service. Practice uses fictional paperwork and dummy teaching kits only: no utility repair, actual equipment shutdown, medicine handling or cleaning experiment.

## [handover] Keep the routine when people change

When Charlaine is absent, the replacement still needs to use the routine. Do not rely on her memory. Prepare a short handover: where the controlled master is kept, how to check its version, who owns the routine and replaces them, how reprints and sessions are recorded, who receives stock-gap reports and when quality is reviewed. Confirm authorised contacts locally; no invented phone number is supplied.

Ask the replacement to use the dummy kit and explain the next step if the master is outdated or paper is missing. If the version or contact is unknown, mark the gap and ask before starting. Orienting a replacement does not prove the trial was accepted or successful.

At review, remove unhelpful steps while preserving version verification, correct material, privacy and service. Record why the routine changed and the next review. If quality falls, revise or stop through the authorised process.

## [practice] Try it, then repeat

Use the fictional trial card and dummy handout. Fill in the problem, change, owner/replacement, baseline, quality, authorisation and review date. The trial is not approved; mark permission pending. Use 20/100 and 5/100 to calculate rates, a 15-percentage-point difference and a 75% relative reduction in reprints. Do not call this cost savings.

In a triad, rotate as BHW, colleague without internet and observer. Show the controlled master, ask about the barrier and explain the handover. Ask the replacement to explain version verification, stock-gap reporting and review. Observer: give one specific piece of feedback. BHW: repeat the step needing improvement. Record done, prompted or not yet.

If working alone, write or speak the trial and handover using the same fictional cards. Reading support is available. Solo and peer rehearsal are practice; they do not replace actual trainer observation or produce an automatic pass.
