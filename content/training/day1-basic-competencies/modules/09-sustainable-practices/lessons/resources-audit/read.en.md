## [elena] Supplies run out before the week ends

BHW Elena, 38, is preparing health teaching at the BHS. A tap leaks, incorrect handouts are repeatedly printed, and missing supplies are bought again. She does not immediately conclude that the budget is too small. She records where water, paper, electricity and time are being wasted. Effective workplace practice delivers needed services while reducing wasted resources.

## [inventory] Find it, count it, return it

Assign labeled places for approved supplies. Count stock before requesting more and check expiry under the local inventory procedure. Separate and report expired or damaged items; do not distribute them simply to use them up. Return reusable teaching materials to their place. Orderly storage reduces duplicate ordering and searching time while making real shortages visible.

## [seven-s] 7S as a daily routine

Use 7S to maintain order: remove unnecessary items, arrange what is needed, clean the area, standardize the method, ensure safety, practice self-discipline and sustain the routine. For example, give the teaching kit a label, contents checklist, cleaning schedule and a person checking it before and after use. A single cleanup is insufficient if disorder returns the following week.

## [practice] Try it, then repeat

Make an audit with resource, observed waste, evidence and a safe next step. Use Elena’s three examples; do not invent bills or quantities. Show a partner how labels and stock counts prevent duplicate orders. Alone, check a fictional teaching kit. At the next duty, observe one resource and discuss findings with the supervisor.
