## [purpose] Purpose and evidence

Choose a resource-saving action that preserves infection prevention, critical equipment and timely services, and explain the rejected option. Charlaine is a fictional BHW; the adaptation is not a DOH quotation. Product: a proposal with a safeguard, rejection, verification and retry. A quiz, attendance or watching the story is not observed competence.

## [time-materials] Time and materials

5 opening + 12 modelling + 25 practice/feedback/retry + 10 safeguards debrief + 8 transfer = 60 minutes. The 60 + 60 + 60 = 180 is the module practice allocation. Self-study audio and optional story duration are separate; this does not grant TESDA certification.

For 30 learners: ten triads, BHW/colleague/observer. Provide the bilingual A4 kit: option cards, version/cause sheet, safeguard checklist, proposal/retry record and one-page decision aid. Use dummy teaching sheets and kits only, with no patient records, medicines, clinical items or actual utility work. The kit supplies all fictional inputs; the trainer need not invent them.

## [prepare] Prepare before starting

Read the six paired screens and both original checks. Review local document-control, clinical single-use and facility procedures with an authorized reviewer before workplace transfer. Policy approval in the story remains unknown. Without an approved procedure, conduct discussion and dummy rehearsal only.

Print the kit, read the version A/B inputs and prepare roster T1–T10, learners A/B/C. Trainer sample: round 1 T1-A, round 2 T2-B, round 3 T3-C. Explain that the other 27 learners will not be trainer-observed in this run. Schedule their next observation opportunity; do not mark peer records as passed.

## [opening] Opening: 5 minutes

Say: “What waste could we reduce while preserving every protection?” Show the high-bill/vaccine-refrigerator card without switching equipment. Ask: “What must stay?” Expected: cold chain, required ventilation and handwashing water. Say: “We will make a proposal, not perform repairs or remove services.” Read the objective and original utilities check, or invite a learner to read.

## [steps] Modelling: 12 minutes

Minutes 0–4: compare utilities cards. Say: “Investigate unnecessary lights under procedure; do not switch off the refrigerator.” Model unknown authorization and savings.

Minutes 4–8: lay out drawer A, printout A, controlled master B and supervisor confirmation B. Ask: “What is confirmed? What remains unknown?” Record the version mismatch. Show two causes: drawer copied versus wrong file selected. Neither is proved; the person who printed has not answered. Verify the source before changing the routine.

Minutes 8–12: model the weaker “Reusable saves money.” Feedback: “What instructions remain? Which single-use reuse must be rejected?” Retry: “Only an approved teaching visual where suitable, with complete instructions; no clinical reuse. Policy approval is needed.” Show the proposal/retry record. Do not reveal the check ending before a response.

## [expected-answers] Expected answers and products

Safe proposal: ask an authorized person to inspect/repair the reported leak while preserving handwashing water, or investigate an approved teaching visual where suitable with complete accessible instructions. Rejection: handwashing restrictions or clinical single-use reuse. A high bill does not prove the refrigerator is the problem.

Verification product: drawer A; printout A; master B; simulated supervisor confirms B. The mismatch is confirmed. Whether the drawer or computer file caused it is unknown. Next: compare copies/files and ask the person who printed under procedure. No cost, measured savings, repair completion or actual approval is supplied.

Complete record: proposal selected; possible waste reduced; safeguard; rejected option and reason; authorization/verification needed; first attempt; specific feedback; observable retry; trainer-observation status.

## [misconception] Misconceptions and safe stopping

“Less paper even if wrong” → restore approved-version verification and complete instructions. “All plastics are reusable” → distinguish nonclinical teaching visuals from clinical single-use items. “Both A, so the drawer caused it” → consistent evidence is not proof. “High bill, stop the refrigerator” → protect the cold chain.

If actual shutdown, repair, clinical reuse or patient records are proposed, say: “Stop. Option cards only; we will preserve essential protection.” Stop the unsafe demonstration, explain the safeguard and repeat the dummy case. An invented cleaning recipe is not a correction. Ask the authorized facility/infection-prevention reviewer to review local procedure.

## [practice] Practice, feedback and retry: 25 minutes

Three six-minute rounds = 18 minutes, followed by seven minutes of shared debrief. Each round: 2 context/setup + 2 attempt + 1 feedback + 1 retry = 6. Round 1 A/B/C are BHW/colleague/observer; round 2 B/C/A; round 3 C/A/B.

Round 1: leak versus handwashing restriction. Round 2: approved reusable teaching visual versus clinical single-use reuse. Round 3: version/cause sheet and paper-saving proposal. Each attempt asks: “What do you choose? What protection stays? Why reject the other option? What is unknown?” Observer records exact actions/words, not impressions.

Feedback names one gap, such as “Authorization is missing.” Retry adds “Confirm with the supervisor first; this is still a proposal.” The trainer observes all four attempt/feedback/retry minutes for one sampled learner per round; the other two minutes allow setup/movement. Debrief compares unknowns; there is no attendance-based passing.

## [answer-key] Answer key for both checks

Utilities: index 0, first option. 1: investigate unnecessary lights under procedure while the refrigerator operates. 2: overnight refrigerator shutdown is unsafe. 3: required-ventilation shutdown is unsafe. Charlaine chooses safeguarded investigation, rejects both cuts and submits for an authorized decision. Approval and savings remain unknown.

Check: index 2, third option. 1: do not omit needed instructions. 2: do not use up old stock without approved-version verification. 3: check the approved version and an approved reusable visual where suitable with complete accessible instructions. Charlaine submits the safeguarded proposal; no measured savings, repair completion or observed competence is claimed.

Read every rationale after the response. Water, single-use rules, critical equipment, ventilation and urgent referral stay protected. Prompts and choices are exactly original; do not add fictional factual inputs to the quiz.

## [observe] Observation and debrief: 10 minutes

Trainer sample: T1-A round 1 minutes 2–6; T2-B round 2 minutes 8–12; T3-C round 3 minutes 14–18, relative to practice start. Only three complete four-minute observations; at least 27 learners remain not trainer-observed. Peer practice is not a pass.

Use the objective_index 0 indicator: safe choice, two-cause evidence checking, safeguard and rejection, verification before workplace action. Ready: without prompting. Needs practice: safe choice but incomplete explanation, followed by feedback and retry. Not yet: unsafe cut, followed by safe stop and supported retry. Record actual observed words/actions and support; do not turn this rubric into a certification rule.

Follow-up roster: T1-B/C, T2-A/C, T3-A/B and T4–T10 A/B/C = 27. Give each learner a separate four-minute observation slot plus setup/movement time; this cannot fit into the ten-minute debrief. Ask what evidence and protection must remain.

## [support] Support and transfer: 8 minutes

Read cards aloud, use large print and the learner’s chosen language. A colleague may record a spoken answer; the objective stays the same. Scaffold: “I choose __. __ stays. I reject __ because __. First verify __.” Remove prompts gradually on retry and record support.

Solo: write/say the same four lines, compare with the aid, correct and retry. No solo self-pass; schedule trainer observation. Transfer prompt: “On your next duty, which proposal and approved procedure will you check first?” Record the verifier and any follow-up that remains unconfirmed. No actual implementation without authorization.
