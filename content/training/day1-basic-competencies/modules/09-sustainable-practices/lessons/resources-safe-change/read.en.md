## [materials] Reduce unnecessary use, preserve safety

Someone suggests a durable health-teaching visual to Charlaine. It may reduce repeated disposable decorations if approved and suitable for its users. Preserve correct information and needed instructions, including a copy when the learner needs one.

An unnecessary nonclinical disposable differs from a clinical single-use item. Plastics reduction does not authorize reusing needles, lancets or other clinical single-use supplies. Follow local policy and proper segregation; do not invent cleaning or reuse methods.

“If I do not know the classification or policy, I will ask the supervisor first,” Charlaine says. Practice compares option cards only. No actual clinical item is used or cleaned. Approval of a new visual is not yet confirmed.

## [workflow] Improve the flow, not just the speed

Before printing, Charlaine and her colleague check the handout date and version. A drawer copy is not automatically the approved master. Find the controlled master and confirm the current approved version with the supervisor under the document-control procedure.

Assemble dummy teaching materials together to reduce searching and repeated trips. Supplies may be delivered together on a planned safe route when authorized, but never delay urgent referral to save travel.

Productivity means quality, timely service with less waste and rework. The number of rushed clients does not prove good work. Organizing the kit is still a proposal; it does not prove measured time or financial savings.

## [practice] Try it, then repeat

Compare two pairs of fictional option cards: ask an authorized person to repair the reported leak versus restrict handwashing water; and use an approved reusable teaching visual where suitable versus reuse a clinical single-use item. Practice includes no actual repair or clinical handling.

Tell a partner: “What might be reduced? What could be harmed? What protection must remain?” Choose a proposal, name a safeguard and explain the rejected option. Include the authorization needed; when unknown, write not yet confirmed. No savings quantity or cost is supplied.

A weaker attempt is: “Reusable saves money.” Feedback: “What must remain, and what must never be reused?” Charlaine retries: “Only an approved teaching visual where suitable, with complete instructions. I reject clinical single-use reuse. Confirm local policy first.”

Alone, write or say the proposal, safeguard, rejection and next verification. This is rehearsal; trainer observation is still needed. A correct quiz answer does not replace observed performance.
