# Facilitator notes — Module 3: Policies at the Barangay Health Station

## Timing

TESDA Training Regulations set **4 hours** for the competency *Practice
Entrepreneurial Skills in the Workplace* (facilitator-guide PDF 22).

Suggested split:

| Part | Time |
|---|---|
| Opening: role-play of the BHS visitor | 20 min |
| The four policies (milk code, pharma ban, breastfeeding act, plastics ban) | 60 min |
| Role-playing: explaining each policy to the community | 60 min |
| Filling out CSC Form 101-H (if time allows) | 20 min |
| Discussion: other policies and efficient resource use | 40 min |

## Delivery script (follows the lesson's sections)

1. **Open with role-play, not a lecture.** Before showing the
   policies, ask: "Has this happened to you — being offered a gift by
   a company in exchange for promotion? What did you do?" Use the
   answer to open the "The Visitor at the BHS" section.
2. **On the two promotion bans,** don't just list the four policies
   separately — show the two similar ones (milk code, pharma ban) as
   one group first, then the two "active expectation" ones
   (breastfeeding act, plastics ban) as another group. Two groups are
   easier to remember than four separate laws.
3. **Use the Role Playing activity the Facilitator's Manual
   recommends** (explaining these policies to community members) after
   the "How to Decline Respectfully" section — let them practice the
   actual wording, not just read the script.
4. **On "Other Policies and Using Resources Efficiently",** connect it
   back to their own BHS — ask: "What's the most wasted resource at
   your barangay health station?"
5. **If there's time,** show the CSC Form 101-H and explain where it
   connects to a BHW's work-related documentation — this links forward
   to Module 4.
6. **If running at Long density,** use the Wrong/Right section as a
   closing role-play.

## Named misconception

**"It's free anyway, and maybe it'll hurt his feelings if I say no."**

This is the usual reason BHWs accept a gift even when they know it's
prohibited — not disrespect for the policy, but avoidance of an
awkward moment. Don't correct it by saying "you need to be stricter."
Instead, show the script in "How to Decline Respectfully" as a way to
avoid both the awkward moment and the policy violation — there's no
need to choose between the two.

## Discussion prompts

- "Has this happened to you — being offered a gift by a company? How
  did you handle it?"
- "Of the four policies, which comes up most often at your barangay
  health station?"
- "What is one thing at your BHS you could make more efficient
  starting next week?"

## Answer key to the retrieval check

1. **"Why is it prohibited to accept a gift..."** → Because it could
   influence the proper service or advice people should receive — not
   because BHWs can never accept any gift, and not because a permit is
   needed.
