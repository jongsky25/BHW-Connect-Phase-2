## [purpose] Purpose

By the end of this short lesson the BHW can explain that this competency, *practice entrepreneurial skills in the workplace*, goes beyond the four policies. It also means knowing the workplace's policies and structure, and finding safe ways to use electricity, water and supplies more efficiently. Faced with a resource problem at the BHS, the BHW can say the first action: explain the proposal to the supervisor before acting, never cut supplies alone.

## [time-materials] Time and materials

- **Time:** about 20 minutes in a face-to-face session: about half of the module's 40-minute "Discussion: other policies and efficient resource use" block, from the timing table in the subchapter guide. The other half goes to "One workable suggestion". Self-study of the lesson alone takes about 5 minutes (one short section and one self-check).
- **Materials:** manila paper and markers; the lesson's slide ("Other policies and using resources efficiently"); two situation cards for the practice activity (below).

## [prepare] Prepare

- Read the lesson in both Read and Slides so you know exactly what the BHW has seen.
- Know, for the participants' BHS, who is responsible for what: who they bring a proposal to (usually the midwife or RHU staff) and who handles supplies. Do not assume; ask them during the lesson.
- Know the scope: the Facilitator Guide lists the other topics under this competency as workplace best practices, workplace policies, efficient resource use (for example, saving electricity or water), being resourceful, and workplace productivity. You may also present other relevant national and local policies, but only ones you can name from an official source.
- This lesson is about **why knowing the workplace's policies and structure comes before changing anything**. Writing a specific suggestion is the next lesson, "One workable suggestion". Do not teach which clinical supplies can be reduced; that is for the facility to decide.

## [opening] Opening

Ask the group the module's question: *"What's the most wasted resource at your barangay health station?"* Write every answer on manila paper under three headings: electricity, water, supplies. Keep the list up; the next lesson uses it too.

## [steps] Steps

1. **Beyond the four policies (Read "Other policies and using resources efficiently", the slide's first line).** Say that the competency also covers knowing the workplace's policies and structure. Ask: *"At your BHS, who decides about supplies? Who would you tell about a water or electricity problem?"* Let them answer from their own BHS.
2. **Efficient and resourceful (the slide's second line).** Point to the opening list. Say the competency means finding ways to use electricity, water and supplies more efficiently, and being resourceful when resources are limited. Pick one item from the list and ask: *"What would be a safe way to use this more efficiently?"* Stress the word *safe*.
3. **The same skill.** Ask: *"What do Corazon declining the milk company and a BHW proposing to save water have in common?"* Guide them to the lesson's answer: it is the same skill, looking at a situation, spotting a better way, and explaining it clearly to others.
4. **Explain before acting (the slide's third line).** Ask: *"Why explain your proposal to the supervisor before you do it?"* Guide them to see that the supervisor knows the policies and what the BHS needs, and that a change made alone can cause new problems.
5. **Self-check.** Read the check question aloud and let the group discuss before you reveal the answer. Ask one BHW to explain the answer using one item from the opening list.

## [expected-answers] Expected answers and how to respond

- **"There's nothing to waste — we don't have enough to begin with."** This is honest and common. Respond: *"That is exactly why being resourceful matters. With so little, what do we need to protect most?"*
- **"It's not our bill, so it's not our problem."** Do not argue. Ask: *"If the electricity or water money runs short, what does the BHS lose?"* Link it back to the service to residents.
- **"I'd just start doing it tomorrow."** The energy is good. Point to the slide's third line: explain the proposal before acting. Ask who they would explain it to first.
- **They suggest cutting clinical supplies** (for example, using fewer of an item to save). Acknowledge the intention, then remind them of "Breastfeeding and the environment": do not remove necessary clinical supplies on your own. That decision goes to the supervisor.

## [misconception] Common misconception

**"Being efficient means cutting back on my own."** A BHW who wants to help may quietly use or give out less of a supply, thinking it saves resources. Correct it without embarrassing anyone: ask the group, *"If the midwife doesn't know a supply is being held back, what could happen when it's needed?"* Being efficient means spotting a better way and explaining it first. It does not mean deciding alone what the BHS can do without.

## [practice] Practice activity

In pairs, each BHW draws one card and has about one minute to say, in their own words: the main point, the first action, and what needs checking with the midwife, RHU or supervisor.

- **Card A:** After clinic hours, the lights and electric fan are often left on in an empty room. A fellow BHW says, *"It's not our bill, never mind."*
- **Card B:** A fellow BHW says, *"To save, let's take some of the supplies off the shelf and keep them aside, without asking anyone."*

Expected direction: for A, name the specific waste, suggest a safe change (switching off when the room is empty) and bring it to the supervisor. For B, do not remove supplies without authorization; bring the concern about running short to the supervisor and ask what the policy is. Walk around and listen. This is where you observe.

## [answer-key] Self-check answer key

The lesson has one check. It appears in the Read section "Other policies and using resources efficiently" and again on the slide of the same name with the same wording and options, so it is covered once here.

- **"Why learn resource-use policy?"** → **To suggest safe and more efficient work.** Knowing the workplace's policies and structure lets the BHW spot a better way and bring it to the right person, safely.
  - **"To remove any supplies without authorization"** is wrong. The slide says to explain the proposal before acting. Removing supplies without authorization skips the supervisor and could take away something needed for safe care. That is not efficiency.

The feedback asks the BHW to explain why using the lesson situation. Card B from the practice activity is a good way to do this.

## [observe] What to observe

Use the lesson's observation indicator during the practice activity: the BHW explains the main point and an appropriate first action in the situation, including what must be checked with the supervisor.

- **Kaya na:** without prompting, the BHW names the resource problem, gives a safe first action, says they will explain it to the supervisor before acting, and says what to check (the policy, who is responsible, whether a supply is needed for care).
- **Kailangan pa ng practice:** the direction is correct, but you have to ask follow-up questions for the details. For example, they suggest a fix but forget to bring it to the supervisor first, or cannot say what to check.
- **Hindi pa:** the BHW agrees to take supplies away without asking anyone, promises a change they cannot decide alone, or cannot say what the main point is.

Observing this in the room is separate from the BHW finishing the online self-check. A correct self-check answer is not evidence of the skill.

## [support] If a BHW needs more support

- Pair them with a BHW rated **Kaya na** and let them answer the same card second.
- Narrow the task: give Card A first (electricity), since it has no safety risk. Give Card B once they can say "explain it to the supervisor first" without help.
- Re-observe at the next session with the same card, not a new one. The next lesson, "One workable suggestion", is another chance to see the same skill.
