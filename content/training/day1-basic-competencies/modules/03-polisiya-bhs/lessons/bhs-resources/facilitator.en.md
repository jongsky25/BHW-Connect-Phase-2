## [purpose] Purpose

Practice three actions: describe a specific resource concern factually; identify the policy, responsibility and service requirements to check; communicate an appropriate first step without independently cutting or reallocating resources. This develops the preserved workplace competency objective 0. Detailed proposal writing belongs to 1.3.5.

## [time-materials] Time and materials

Suggested face-to-face allocation: 20 minutes, retaining half of the module plan’s 40-minute resource discussion block. Self-study estimate: about 10 minutes for six screens and the check, with optional video additional. These are authored estimates. The original manual recommends at least four hours for the whole competency; the inherited module table totals 200 minutes, leaving a 40-minute planning gap for the training organizer to resolve across the whole competency. Do not treat the table as satisfying that minimum or repeat four hours for each lesson. Materials: six bilingual screens, blank observation cards, fictional scenario cards; no patient data, real repairs or equipment demonstrations.

## [prepare] Prepare

Confirm the actual responsible contact and local closing procedure with the health team. Check whether the chosen nonclinical meeting-area example is suitable locally; substitute another observation-only nonclinical example if needed. Mark unknown rules and contacts as requiring confirmation. Read the source audit. Do not assume the supervisor has authority for every action or that electricity is risk-free.

## [opening] Opening

Read Mimi’s meeting-area observation (2 minutes). Ask: What did she see, and what does she not yet know? Expected: lights and fan on, no one seen at that time; next use, reason and closing rule are unknown. Avoid blame or invented savings.

## [steps] Steps

1. Review policies before changes and the responsible contact questions (3 minutes).
2. Separate the observation from an assumption; make a short non-identifying record (3 minutes).
3. Contrast checking an empty meeting area with independently setting supplies aside; name service and safety requirements (3 minutes).
4. Model Mimi’s factual message and questions to the confirmed responsible person (3 minutes).
5. Pair practice with both cards; swap BHW and responsible-person roles (4 minutes).
6. Answer the application check and explain all choices (2 minutes). Watching the optional story is not required for online completion.

## [expected-answers] Expected answers

What should Mimi report? The specific lights/fan observation, time and place, with next use unknown. What must she check? Schedule, closing rule, who is responsible, authority and service/safety requirements. Who should she ask? The locally confirmed responsible person, not an assumed universal chain. First action? Share the concern and questions before changing resource use. Is an empty room proof the fan can be switched off? No; check the requirements. Can she set supplies aside while awaiting approval? No; that already changes availability. Can a supervisor waive a prohibited action? Do not assume that; check the policy and authority.

## [misconception] Common misconception

“Being resourceful means making cuts first and reporting afterwards.” Ask what service could need the supplies or area. Efficiency protects service, follows policy and stays within role. Neither good intentions, a colleague’s agreement nor an apparently empty area establishes permission. A nonclinical example may still have safety implications.

## [practice] Practice activity

Card A: after a meeting, lights and a fan remain on in an empty nonclinical meeting area; the next schedule and closing rule are unknown. Describe only the observation and ask the confirmed responsible person about use, procedure and authority. An appropriate agreed first step is to review the schedule and existing checklist with that person, not immediately switch off or repair anything. Card B: a colleague proposes putting some supplies aside without permission to save resources. Explain that availability and service needs must be checked, decline to make that independent cut, and raise the concern through the local process. Role-play a short factual message for each card, swap roles and record the three observed actions.

## [answer-key] Self-check answer key

Choice 1 is correct: factual record and confirmation of schedule, rule, responsibility and authority before changes, with no independent supply cut. Choice 2 changes resource use before service needs and the closing rule are known; delayed reporting cannot authorize it retrospectively. Choice 3 already restricts supply availability before permission and may compromise service. Have the learner explain the difference in their own words.

## [observe] What to observe

Observe three actions grouped under objective index 0: (1) describes a specific resource concern factually without blame, invented measurements or patient data; (2) identifies the relevant policy, responsible person and service/safety requirements to check; (3) communicates an appropriate first step before changes, without independently cutting, reallocating or repairing resources. Kaya na: all three in both cards without prompting. Kailangan practice: direction is appropriate but one or more questions or boundaries need prompting. Hindi pa: acts first, authorizes an unsupported cut, or cannot identify what needs checking. Record practical observation separately; online completion is not evidence of observed workplace skill.

## [support] If a BHW needs more support

Use a cue card: facts → rule and responsible person → service needs → coordinate first. Model the message for Card A without touching controls. Let the learner repeat in their own words, then fade prompts. Re-practice Card B and ask why setting supplies aside is already a change. Confirm the local contact and rule together. Re-observe both cards after feedback; record the supported practice outcome.
