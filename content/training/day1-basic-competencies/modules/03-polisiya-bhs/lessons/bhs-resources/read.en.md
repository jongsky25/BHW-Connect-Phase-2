## [section-5] Policies before changes

Mimi, a BHW, notices that the lights and electric fan are on in an empty meeting area after an activity. Resources at the BHS are limited. How can she help without affecting service?

The workplace competency includes understanding policies and responsibilities, using electricity, water and supplies efficiently, and being resourceful. Efficiency does not mean simply reducing everything. First, Mimi will check the rule, who is responsible, and whether the area is still needed for service. Mimi is also the BHW who declined the company offer in the previous lesson; here she applies the same understanding and clear communication to a different situation. The story is optional; you can complete Read or Slides without watching it.

## [know-responsibilities] Know the rule and responsibility

Mimi does not assume she makes the decision because she noticed the concern. She asks the confirmed responsible person: “Who is responsible for the meeting area after use? Is there a closing checklist, and who may authorize a change?”

The right contact may be the midwife, facility supervisor or another designated person at their BHS; confirm local responsibilities rather than assuming one universal chain of command. If the contact is absent, follow the local referral process. A colleague agreeing with an idea does not automatically authorize it. The action must comply with policy and the limits of the role.

## [observe-resource-use] Describe what you observed

In this fictional example, Mimi records: “After the meeting, the lights and fan were on in the meeting area; I saw no one there when I checked.” She does not write that a colleague is careless or that a particular amount of money is definitely being wasted. She does not yet know whether another activity is planned or there is another reason.

If a local record is needed, include only the date, time, place and specific observation. Omit patient names or data, guesses about motives, and invented consumption or savings measurements. An observation starts a conversation; it is not a conclusion or permission to change something.

## [protect-service] Protect service needs

A colleague says: “Let us reduce the supplies on the shelf to save resources.” Mimi does not do this independently. Those supplies may be needed for service; noticing lights in a meeting area is different from deciding which supplies to reduce.

For the meeting area, she first checks the next planned use, requirements for the area, and who is responsible for the closing procedure. She does not repair electrical systems, operate clinical equipment controls, reuse clinical supplies or ration services. If there is a possible hazard, refer it through the local safety process; do not attempt repairs beyond training. A nonclinical example is not automatically free of safety risks.

## [check-before-change] Coordinate before acting

Mimi tells the confirmed responsible person: “I noticed the lights and fan were on in the meeting area after the meeting. Is another activity planned? What is the closing rule, and who is responsible?” She explains the specific observation and her questions instead of announcing a change she has already made.

In this example, they agree first to review the schedule and existing checklist with the person responsible. Mimi records that agreed first step if the local process requires it. This does not automatically approve shutdowns or cuts. If the rule or authority is unclear, ask for guidance before changing anything. Lesson 1.3.5 develops one detailed suggestion; here the focus is appropriate first coordination.
