## [section-3] A supportive BHS

A parent carrying an infant arrives at the barangay health station. “Is there someone I can talk to about breastfeeding?” the parent asks BHW Mimi. Mimi listens first.

Two distinct policies matter here: breastfeeding support under RA 10028, and reducing unnecessary single-use plastic under DOH DC 2021-0486. They meet in a respectful, safe environment. Mimi can help confirm local support and safe practices without promising a service that has not been verified.

## [facility-roles] Facilities and roles

RA 10028 strengthens breastfeeding support. It requires lactation stations: private, clean and well-ventilated spaces for breastfeeding or expressing milk. A station must not be in a toilet. Equipment and facility requirements apply, and exemptions have a formal process.

The institution is responsible for complying with the requirements. A BHW does not decide an exemption or establish a facility alone. Mimi asks the midwife or supervisor: “Who is trained to help, and what suitable space and guidance are available here?” If something is missing, she raises it with the supervisor rather than making a promise.

## [listen-connect] Listen and connect

“What would you like to ask, and would you like to speak with someone trained to support you?” Mimi asks calmly. She respects the parent and avoids discussing personal concerns in front of other people. She does not impose a feeding choice or shame the parent.

She confirms who can help and where a private conversation can take place. With the parent’s permission, she helps make the connection to a trained health worker under local guidance. She does not give a diagnosis, treatment or her own clinical feeding instructions. If the answer is not yet known, she says she will check with the health team.

## [unnecessary-plastic] Unnecessary plastic

Mimi also notices disposable items in the staff break area. DOH DC 2021-0486 addresses unnecessary single-use plastics in hospitals, other health facilities and DOH offices. Its list includes thin plastic cups, drinking straws, coffee stirrers, plastic cutlery and certain thin plastic bags.

The circular set successive phases beginning in 2022 and 2023 for specified settings and groups. It does not say that all plastic is illegal everywhere. Mimi checks the facility’s current directive with the supervisor: which items are unnecessary, and which safe alternatives are allowed?

## [safe-alternative] A safe alternative

Mimi suggests: “Could staff use their own reusable lunch containers in the break area, under the guidance here?” This is a non-clinical example that needs the supervisor’s confirmation. It is not a suggestion to reuse medical supplies.

The circular considered infection prevention and control in its phased implementation. A BHW must not independently remove or reuse sterile or necessary clinical supplies. Trained staff guidance governs infection control and handling healthcare waste. If the safety of a change is uncertain, ask first. Saving resources must not override safe care.

## [check] Mimi’s decision

The parent wants someone to talk to about breastfeeding, but Mimi has not yet confirmed the contact or space. Staff also want to reduce disposable items in their break area.

Mimi needs a next step that respects the parent, checks local arrangements and protects safe care. Start with listening and privacy; confirm trained support and the non-clinical alternative with the supervisor. Which response can she carry out within her role?
