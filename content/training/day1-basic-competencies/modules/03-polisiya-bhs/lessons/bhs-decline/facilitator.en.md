## [purpose] Purpose

Practice a clear respectful refusal, an accurate policy reason matched to the product, and a factual handover to the supervisor. Preserve objective index 0. This lesson assesses communication in a role-play, rather than repeating recognition of offers from 1.3.1.

## [time-materials] Time and materials

Suggested 30-minute activity within the existing four-hour BHS policy module, not an added mandated training allocation. Use the six screens, optional narrated story, blank refusal/report cue cards and fictional offer cards. No real products, brands, patient data or gifts.

## [prepare] Prepare

Confirm the actual facility supervisor and reporting process with the health team. Mark unknown contacts as to confirm. Read the source audit. Prepare two separate cards: infant-formula samples for mothers/poster/gift for promotion, and prescription samples for distribution to families with a personal gift. Do not mix their policy explanations.

## [opening] Opening

Read Mimi’s first refusal (3 minutes). Ask what makes the response both polite and clear. Expected: acknowledges the visit, says no to specific actions, gives a correct reason, and promises only to report facts. Do not guess the visitor’s intentions.

## [steps] Steps

1. Model the refusal and identify its four parts (4 minutes).
2. Match each of the two offer cards to its relevant policy reason (4 minutes).
3. Add “It is free; I will leave it here.” Practice calmly repeating the boundary without taking the box or offering another distribution site (4 minutes).
4. Report the visit using factual details, your response and no patient data; confirm the local process (4 minutes).
5. Pair role-play and swap roles (8 minutes).
6. Answer the final check independently and explain alternatives (3 minutes). The optional video is not required for completion.

## [expected-answers] Expected answers

What is a clear no? “I will not accept, display or distribute these items.” What policy? Milk Code for the formula promotion scenario; pharmaceutical order for prescription samples. Why not keep the box? That still accepts the offer. What if details are unknown? State the uncertainty, pause acceptance and ask the supervisor. Can referral make prohibited promotion permissible? No. What to report? Time/place, product, offer, requested activity, your refusal and actions. Same-day reporting is the fictional example, not a new legal deadline.

## [misconception] Common misconception

Respect does not require accepting a gift or saying “maybe later.” A correct tone cannot fix a wrong reason or a secret agreement. Milk Code is not the reason for prescription-sample restrictions. Some educational items/scientific activities have conditions; a BHW does not approve exceptions independently.

## [practice] Practice activity

Visitor: “Please give these infant-formula samples to mothers, display our poster, and accept this gift.” BHW: give a respectful specific refusal, explain the Milk Code basis and state the supervisor report. Visitor presses: “It is free; leave it outside instead.” BHW repeats the boundary and makes no alternative arrangement. Then use the prescription-sample card and change the policy explanation. Give a factual verbal handover without patient details. Swap roles. Score the three indicators separately; allow learners to use their own respectful words.

## [answer-key] Self-check answer key

Option 1 is correct: accurate pharmaceutical reason, no acceptance/distribution, asks the visitor not to leave the box, and informs the supervisor. Option 2 accepts custody while delaying distribution. Option 3 misapplies the Milk Code and offers an improper workaround. Ask each learner to state a reason; law-number recall is not the scoring criterion.

## [observe] What to observe

Observe three actions against objective 0: (1) a respectful, explicit refusal naming what will not be accepted/displayed/distributed; (2) an accurate reason matched to formula versus prescription samples, maintaining the boundary under pressure without a promised exception; (3) a factual handover to the confirmed supervisor through the local process, without patient data.

Kaya na: all three without prompting. Kailangan pa ng practice: direction is correct but prompts are needed for an accurate reason, firmness, or report. Hindi pa: accepts, agrees to a workaround, cites the wrong policy without correction, or omits reporting. Online completion is not proof of observed practical skill. Record role-play observation separately.

## [support] If a BHW needs more support

Use a cue card: acknowledge → clear no → correct reason → factual report. Model one response, then fade the prompts. Practice the visitor’s follow-up at a slower pace. Confirm the real reporting contact and repeat the role-play. Refer feeding or clinical questions to trained professionals; do not blame family feeding choices.
