## [section-4] Clear and respectful

The company representative returns to the BHS with infant-formula samples for mothers, a product poster, and a personal gift in return for promotion. Mimi knows she will decline this offer. How can she say no respectfully?

Mimi says: “Thank you for coming. I will not accept the samples or gift, or display the product poster. This falls under the Milk Code: the facility cannot be used for this promotion, and an inducement for promotion cannot be accepted. I will tell our midwife or facility supervisor the details.” She does not take the items, assume the visitor’s motives, or promise permission later. ## [match-policy] Match the reason to the product

Different products need different policy explanations. The first situation involves infant-formula samples for mothers, a BHS product poster, and a gift for promotion; the Milk Code is the relevant basis.

If Mimi is asked to give prescription-medicine samples to families instead, she needs a different explanation: “I cannot distribute prescription samples to the public. Under the pharmaceutical order, samples are for licensed physicians and dentists under its rules. I will refer the details to the supervisor.” That order covers prescription pharmaceutical products and medical devices. Some educational items and scientific activities are permitted under conditions; this is not automatic permission for a BHW. If the product or conditions are uncertain, state what is unknown, pause acceptance while checking, and ask for guidance. Referral cannot make prohibited promotion lawful.

## [hold-boundary] When the visitor presses

“It is free. I will just leave it, and you can decide later,” the visitor says. Mimi does not need to argue or invent an excuse. She calmly repeats the boundary: “I understand the offer, but I will not accept or distribute these items. Please do not leave them with us. I will inform the supervisor about your visit.”

She does not arrange distribution outside the facility, accept secretly, or display the poster while waiting for the midwife. She does not promise that another offer will be approved. If the conversation becomes difficult, she asks the supervisor for help through the local process. Respect is expressed in how she speaks; it does not require agreement.

## [report-facts] Report what happened

After the conversation, Mimi informs the midwife or facility supervisor through the local process. In this fictional example, she reports that day. This is not a new legal deadline.

She says: “A representative offered infant-formula samples for mothers, a BHS poster, and a gift for promotion. I declined and accepted, displayed, and distributed nothing. I said I would tell you the details.” If a record is required, include the time, place, product, offer, requested activity, and her response. Follow local rules for handling records; omit patient data and guesses about motives. Ask the supervisor to determine the next step. Do not blame families for feeding choices; a health professional handles feeding or clinical questions.

## [section-7] Compare two responses

Both responses concern the same infant-formula offer. Danilo accepts the samples for mothers and gift because “saying no might be rude.” He suggests distributing them outside instead. Accepting and arranging an alternative cross the boundary; a polite tone does not correct that.

Mimi thanks the visitor, clearly declines the samples, gift, and poster, explains the Milk Code as it applies to the offer, and makes no alternative promotional arrangement. She informs the supervisor of the facts. In the medicine-sample situation, she would use the pharmaceutical explanation, not the Milk Code. A good response combines respect, an accurate reason, and actions consistent with the refusal.
