## [section-6] From concern to proposal

In the previous lesson, BHW Mimi checked why the lights and fan were on in an empty meeting area. In this fictional continuation, she confirms another activity is scheduled there; she does not simply turn them off. She also learns there is no clear reminder of who checks the schedule after a meeting.

Now she will form one workable suggestion: a small first step with a reason, consistent with local rules and service needs. It is not yet permission to act or a promise of savings. The story is optional; you can complete Read or Slides without watching it.

## [factual-concern] State the factual concern

Mimi says: “After the meeting, the lights and fan were on in the meeting area. I confirmed another activity was scheduled, but there was no clear reminder of who checks the schedule.” This is the concern to discuss, without accusing anyone of carelessness.

If a record is needed, include the date, time, place and observation using the local process. State what remains unknown, such as an existing checklist or assigned duty. Omit patient data, guesses about motives and savings amounts that have not been measured.

## [one-feasible-proposal] Propose one feasible first step

Mimi proposes: “Could we review whether a short meeting-end checklist is needed, so the designated person checks the next scheduled use and shares concerns with the right contact?” It is one proposal; she does not claim the duty or tell everyone to use the checklist immediately.

Check for an existing process before creating another. If one exists, clarifying its reminder may be enough. If a change is needed, check the time, materials and person who could carry it out. Do not include independent repairs, reductions in clinical supplies or new tasks beyond the role.

## [reason-service-checks] Explain the reason and limits

Mimi explains: “A clear reminder could help people remember to check the schedule and coordinate after a meeting.” The purpose is to reduce confusion about responsibility; electricity or money savings have not been measured.

She asks whether the checklist is practical, who has time for it and whether another group needs the area. It must follow the rules and protect service and safety. If a possible hazard is present, use the local safety process; a checklist does not replace a repair or clinical procedure.

## [responsibility-followup] Confirm responsibility and follow-up

Mimi first confirms who is responsible for the area and who has authority to approve a change. It may be the midwife, facility supervisor or another designated person, depending on local roles and the proposal. A colleague agreeing does not automatically give permission.

In the fictional conversation, the contact agrees to review the existing process with Mimi at the next meeting. They record who will check, what will be reviewed and when they will speak again. If there is no decision, Mimi reports the status and follows the local referral process. She does not promise approval, repairs or savings, and does not implement a new checklist yet.

## [proposal-check] Choose the workable suggestion

A colleague wants to post a new checklist immediately and tell everyone to follow it. Which response from Mimi is appropriate? Choose the suggestion with a specific concern, practical first step, reason, local checks and an agreed follow-up.
