## [section-1] The visitor at the BHS

A milk-company representative arrives at BHW Mimi’s barangay health station with samples, a tarpaulin, and a small gift. “Please give the samples to mothers and hang the tarpaulin. The gift is for you.” Mimi listens: what product is being offered, and what is expected in return? She does not accept, display, or distribute the items.

## [section-2] Two policy topics

The Milk Code covers infant formula, other products marketed as breastmilk replacements, and feeding bottles and teats. It prohibits promotion and product posters in health-care facilities and inducements to health workers to promote covered products. DOH AO 2015-0053 has separate rules for prescription pharmaceutical products and medical devices. It includes prohibitions and activities allowed under conditions; the two policies have different scopes.

## [identify-offer] What exactly is being offered?

Mimi identifies four details: the product, requested promotion, place and audience, and gift or inducement. Here she sees samples whose scope must be checked, a BHS tarpaulin, distribution to mothers, and a gift alongside the promotion. For a medicine offer, ask whether it is a prescription product and who would receive the sample. Under AO 2015-0053, prescription samples cannot be distributed to the general public; only licensed physicians and dentists may receive these samples under its rules.

## [pause-route] Pause and coordinate

Mimi says: “Thank you for approaching us. I will not accept or distribute these items. I will refer the details to the facility supervisor.” She does not hang the tarpaulin or pass samples to families. AO 2015-0053 prohibits using a government facility for pharmaceutical or medical-device promotion, with a stated exception for scientific conventions. The supervisor checks the applicable process and required authorization; a BHW cannot create an exception independently.

## [independent-information] Information independent of promotion

Mimi does not endorse a brand. She uses information approved for the facility’s health education and asks the health team to review company materials. Following local guidance, she records or reports the product, offer, requested activity, time and place, without patient data. Do not blame families for their feeding choices. Refer feeding, medicine, or treatment questions to a trained health professional.

## [recognition-check] Recognize before deciding

Two offers arrive. Offer A: infant-formula samples for mothers, a BHS product poster, and a gift if Mimi helps promote. Offer B: an anatomical model for a scientific activity already coordinated by the supervisor, with no endorsement in return. The two are not automatically identical. The pharmaceutical order permits certain educational items under conditions, but that does not authorize a BHW to accept independently. What is the correct recognition and first step?
