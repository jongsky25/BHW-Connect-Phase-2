## [purpose] Purpose

Help the BHW recognize a company offer by naming the product, requested promotion, place/audience, and inducement; distinguish the Milk Code from the prescription pharmaceutical/medical-device rules; pause acceptance, display, and distribution and coordinate with the facility supervisor. Observe recognition and routing. Detailed refusal practice belongs to lesson 1.3.3.

## [time-materials] Time and materials

Suggested 35-minute activity within the existing four-hour BHS policies module, not an additional mandated allocation. Self-study: about 8 minutes.

Use the six bilingual screens, optional story, four-detail worksheet, two unbranded offer cards, and current local facility guidance. Do not bring real promotional samples or patient records.

## [prepare] Prepare

Confirm who supervises offers at this facility and how BHWs report factual details. Mark unknown local contacts or procedures as “to confirm.” Read the source audit and relevant policy sections. Prepare Offer A (formula samples for mothers, BHS poster, personal gift for promotion) and Offer B (anatomical model for a supervisor-coordinated scientific activity, no endorsement). Do not describe B as automatically approved; its conditions still require verification. No feeding or prescribing exercise is included.

## [opening] Opening

Read Mimi’s visitor scene or play the optional story (3 minutes). Ask participants to repeat what the visitor asks Mimi to do. Put only stated facts on the board; avoid guesses about the representative’s motives.

## [steps] Steps

1. **Identify the offer (6 minutes).** Fill four columns: product, requested promotion, place/audience, gift/inducement. Ask what is known and what must be confirmed.
2. **Distinguish policy topics (6 minutes).** Milk Code scope concerns covered substitutes and related products. The pharmaceutical order concerns prescription products and medical devices. Highlight facility promotion and inducements; a BHW does not authorize exceptions.
3. **Trace the first action (5 minutes).** Mimi pauses acceptance, display and distribution, and routes factual details to the facility supervisor. Practice one brief coordination sentence.
4. **Compare A and B (5 minutes).** Identify concrete problems in A. B is a different educational offer with conditions to verify. “Free” and “educational” labels alone give no permission.
5. **Pair practice (7 minutes).** Use the scenario below, swap roles, and observe the recognition and routing words.
6. **Check and feedback (3 minutes).** Answer the final screen independently, then explain why the alternatives fail.

## [expected-answers] Expected answers

- **What is the inducement?** A personal gift offered alongside Mimi’s help with product promotion.
- **What must be checked?** Product scope, intended audience and activity, facility rules, relevant authorization, and the correct supervisor/process.
- **Can a BHW distribute prescription samples to families?** No. The order restricts prescription samples to licensed physicians/dentists under its rules.
- **Is every educational contact identical to A?** No. Certain educational items are permitted under conditions. The BHW must still coordinate rather than independently approve.
- **Why protect independent information?** Care and education should follow patient needs and accurate approved information, rather than a promotional reward.

## [misconception] Common misconception

A small or free gift is not automatically harmless, and an “educational” label is not proof of authorization. Conversely, the two policy regimes do not make every industry interaction identical. Government-facility promotion restrictions and conditional scientific activities must be checked in their actual scope. Supervisor coordination cannot legalize a prohibited promotion.

## [practice] Practice activity

In pairs, one person describes an unbranded offer: “We have samples for mothers, a product poster for your BHS, and a gift if you help us.” The BHW identifies the four details, names the relevant policy topic and what remains unknown, pauses acceptance/display/distribution, and refers the offer to the confirmed supervisor. Add the model scenario B and ask which conditions need checking. Swap roles. Observe the BHW’s explanation; do not score exact law-number recall or detailed refusal delivery.

## [answer-key] Self-check answer key

Option 1 is correct. A contains formula samples for mothers, BHS product promotion and a gift for promotion. Do not accept, display or distribute it. B has a different purpose, but its conditions and authorization still require the supervisor’s verification. Option 2 mistakes free/educational labels for permission. Option 3 erases distinctions and prevents proper recognition and routing.

## [observe] What to observe

- **Kaya na:** independently identifies product, requested promotion, place/audience and inducement; distinguishes the policy topics; pauses the offer and names the supervisor verification step; gives a factual, non-identifying explanation.
- **Kailangan pa ng practice:** recognizes a gift/promotion concern and asks for help, but needs prompts to identify scope, audience or the coordination step.
- **Hindi pa:** accepts/displays/distributes the items, independently approves an exception, or treats every contact as identical without checking details.

Online completion is not proof of observed practical skill. Record the role-play observation separately against objective index 0.

## [support] If a BHW needs more support

Give a four-detail cue card and ask the BHW to repeat the scenario. Practice stating “This detail is unknown; I will ask the facility supervisor.” Confirm the real local contact with the health team, then re-observe recognition and routing. Do not replace this with memorizing law numbers.
