## [purpose] Purpose

By the end of this short lesson the BHW can look at a company's offer at the BHS (samples, a tarpaulin, a gift), say which policy it touches, and name the first action. The policy is the Milk Code (EO 51) for milk substitutes, or the ethical rules on pharmaceutical promotion for medicine. The first action is not to accept the inducement and to refer the offer to the supervisor to check current policy.

## [time-materials] Time and materials

- **Time:** about 50 minutes in a face-to-face session. That is the module's 20-minute opening role-play of the BHS visitor plus about half of the 60-minute "four policies" block (the two bans on promotion), from the timing table in the subchapter guide. The other half of that block goes to "Breastfeeding and the environment". Self-study of the lesson alone takes about 5 minutes (two short sections and one self-check).
- **Materials:** manila paper and markers; the lesson's slides ("The visitor at the BHS" and "Two bans on promotion"); simple props for the visitor role-play (an empty box for "samples", a sheet of paper for the "tarpaulin"); offer cards for the practice activity: one card for a milk company and one for a medicine company.

## [prepare] Prepare

- Read the lesson in both Read and Slides so you know exactly what the BHW has seen.
- Find out who the supervisor is at the participants' BHS (usually the midwife or RHU staff) and how an offer from a company reaches them.
- Know the lesson's wording by heart: the Milk Code (EO 51) restricts promotion of covered breastmilk substitutes in the health-care system and inducements to health workers. For pharmaceutical promotion, ethical rules also apply; the source training material cites DOH AO 2015-0053.
- This lesson is about **recognizing the offer and the first action**. How to word the refusal is the next lesson, "Declining respectfully". Do not rehearse the full refusal here. Do not explain the details or exceptions of AO 2015-0053: the lesson itself says its exact scope still needs primary-source review. Do not teach that every contact with a company is forbidden.

## [opening] Opening

Ask the group the module's opening question: *"Has this happened to you — being offered a gift by a company in exchange for promotion? What did you do?"* Write the answers on manila paper without judging them. Then play the milk company representative yourself, hold up the props and say: *"Could we leave these inside, for you to give out to the mothers? We also have a small gift for you."* Ask one volunteer: *"What would you say right now?"* Do not correct the answer yet.

## [steps] Steps

1. **Scene (Read "The visitor at the BHS", first slide).** Tell Corazon's story. Ask: *"What exactly is being offered, and what is being asked in return?"* List both on manila paper: samples, tarpaulin, a gift, in exchange for promotion to the mothers. Read the slide aloud: a gift is offered in exchange for promotion; do not accept it on the spot; refer it to the supervisor and check policy.
2. **Why the refusal needs a basis.** Ask: *"If Corazon only said 'I don't want to', what might the visitor say back?"* Show that declining is stronger once you know the policy behind it.
3. **Two bans on promotion (Read "Two bans on promotion", second slide).** Present the two as one group, as the module script suggests: milk on one side, medicine on the other. The Milk Code (EO 51) restricts promotion of covered breastmilk substitutes in the health-care system and inducements to health workers, so do not accept gifts in exchange for promoting such products. Pharmaceutical promotion also has ethical rules; the source training material cites DOH AO 2015-0053. Ask: *"What is the same about these two?"* Guide the group to the principle: no company should be able to influence the service given inside a barangay health station.
4. **What not to do alone.** Point to the last line of the slide: check the scope; do not approve an offer alone. Ask: *"At your BHS, who would you bring this offer to?"* Stress that the lesson does not treat every contact with a company the same way. The first action is to refer and check, not to decide alone, either to say yes or to chase the visitor away.
5. **Self-check.** Read the check question aloud and let the group discuss before you reveal the answer. Ask one BHW to explain the answer using Corazon's situation.

## [expected-answers] Expected answers and how to respond

- **"I'd accept the samples, but not the gift."** Acknowledge that they are thinking about the mothers. Then ask: *"What are the samples asking of you in return?"* Bring it back to the lesson: the offer is tied to promotion inside the BHS, so it goes to the supervisor.
- **"I'd send them away — all companies are banned."** The instinct to protect the BHS is right. But the lesson says not to treat all industry contact as identical. The first action is to refer the offer and check current policy with the supervisor.
- **"I didn't know there was a rule."** This is honest and common. Thank them: that is exactly why this lesson exists. The refusal is stronger once you know its basis.
- **They cite details of AO 2015-0053 beyond the lesson.** Acknowledge what they know, then say the exact scope of the order is still being reviewed. For any specific case, they check the current policy with the supervisor.

## [misconception] Common misconception

**"It's only a small gift, and nobody will know."** The rule is not about getting caught. It is about who shapes the service given inside the BHS. Correct it without pointing at anyone: ask the whole group, *"If the mothers knew the BHW had received a gift from the company, would they trust the advice in the same way?"* A related reason BHWs give, "it's free anyway, and maybe it'll hurt his feelings if I say no", is the module's named misconception. It is handled in the next lesson, "Declining respectfully".

## [practice] Practice activity

In pairs, give each pair an offer card: a milk company or a medicine company, with samples or a tarpaulin and a small gift. One BHW plays the visitor and reads the offer. The other BHW answers in their own words, in about one minute:

1. What is being offered, and what is asked in return?
2. Which policy does it touch: the Milk Code or the rules on pharmaceutical promotion?
3. What is the first action?
4. What needs checking with the midwife, RHU or supervisor?

Then they switch roles and cards. Walk around and listen. This is where you observe.

## [answer-key] Self-check answer key

The lesson has one check. It appears in the Read section "Two bans on promotion" and again on the "Two bans on promotion" slide with the same wording and options, so it is covered once here.

- **"What should you do about a gift offered in exchange for promotion?"** → **Do not accept the inducement; refer it to the supervisor under facility policy.** The Milk Code restricts inducements to health workers, and the lesson says not to approve an offer alone. Referring it lets the supervisor check the current policy.
  - **"Accept it as long as nobody knows"** is wrong. Whether anyone finds out does not change what the gift is: an inducement in exchange for promotion. Keeping it hidden lets the company influence the service at the BHS, and it bypasses the supervisor completely.

The feedback asks the BHW to explain why using the lesson situation. Ask them to use Corazon and the milk company visitor.

## [observe] What to observe

Use the lesson's observation indicator during the practice activity: the BHW explains the main point and an appropriate first action in the situation, including what must be checked with the supervisor.

- **Kaya na:** without prompting, the BHW says what is offered and what is asked in return, names the right policy for the product, says they will not accept the inducement and will refer the offer, and says what must be checked with the supervisor.
- **Kailangan pa ng practice:** the direction is correct (does not accept, will refer), but you have to ask follow-up questions for the details. For example, they mix up which policy covers milk and which covers medicine, or they forget what to check with the supervisor.
- **Hindi pa:** the BHW accepts the offer, promises the visitor something (for example, to put up the tarpaulin "just this once") without checking with the supervisor, or cannot say what the main point is.

Observing this in the room is separate from the BHW finishing the online self-check. A correct self-check answer is not evidence of the skill.

## [support] If a BHW needs more support

- Give them the four practice questions on a card and let them answer after a pair rated **Kaya na** has gone first.
- Narrow the task: start with the milk company card only, since Corazon's scene is about milk. Add the medicine card once they answer the milk card without help.
- Re-observe at the next session with the same offer card, not a new one. The role-play in "Declining respectfully" starts from the same scene and is a natural second chance to observe.
