## [open-question] Open first, then clarify

Gibs, a male BHW, talks with Liza, 29, about her missed health-center visit. This story is fictional. They have already agreed on the purpose, language and a comfortable place. “What happened when you could not attend?” Gibs asks. “I had nobody with me,” Liza replies. He gives her time to finish the story. “You mean you did not want to go?” already assumes a motive. An open invitation lets Liza describe events in her own words. Then clarify one needed detail. A yes-or-no answer can confirm a specific detail; it does not replace listening to the story.

## [clarify-detail] What does “with me” mean?

“I had nobody with me” is still unclear to Gibs. “Do you mean someone to travel with you?” he asks. “No. Someone to watch my child,” Liza corrects him. “Thank you for clarifying,” Gibs replies. He does not insist on his first interpretation. “What do you mean by with me?” is another useful question when the meaning is uncertain. Ask one question at a time; do not request the date, time and reason all at once. If relevant to the agreed task, follow with “When was that?” or “When was your last visit?” Do not guess a date the person cannot remember. In this story, first clarify who was unavailable and what their role was.

## [barriers] Clarify barriers without judging the person

In Liza’s own account, nobody could watch her child that day. This differs from the neighbor’s claim, “She just does not want to go.” That is the neighbor’s statement, not Liza’s confirmed explanation. “Which part of getting there was difficult?” Gibs asks. Timing, childcare, language or access may be barriers; let the person explain. Do not turn possibilities into a list of expected answers. The Rosario case in Module 7 connects poverty, service gaps and misinformation. First clarify what the family was told, then separately ask what prevented them from getting help. A barrier does not prove laziness or disobedience. Ask only for details relevant to the agreed task. Do not promise childcare, transport or an appointment that has not been confirmed.

## [confirm-summary] Offer the summary as a question

“Have I understood correctly: getting there is always difficult because nobody can watch your child?” Gibs asks. “Not always. Only when my sister is away,” Liza corrects him. “When your sister is away, it is difficult to find someone to watch your child. Is that right?” “Yes,” she confirms. Gibs removes always from his summary. “Would you like us to check the current service hours?” he offers. Liza agrees to check the hours first; she does not yet know when she can attend. This is a confirmed next step, not a promise of an appointment or childcare. Return a brief relevant summary, not every private detail. Use information for the authorized purpose under local policy. If immediate help is needed, follow the local process; do not delay it to finish a routine conversation.

## [practice] Try it, then repeat

Use fictional BHW, Liza and observer cards. In the first round, start with an open invitation. Liza replies, “I have nobody with me.” Clarify whether this means a travel companion or someone to watch her child, then ask her to check your summary. The observer records the exact question, answer and correction; do not guess a motive. Repeat a step that needs support before rotating roles. Across three rounds, everyone practices as the BHW. In the next round, clarify an ambiguous time; in the third, distinguish the neighbor’s claim from Liza’s own answer. Alone, write and say the invitation, clarification and summary aloud, then use the question aid. Peer feedback is not a facilitator grade. At the next authorized visit, clarify one ambiguous answer before recording it. No real resident record or private account is uploaded for practice.

## [check] Choose the next step

Liza says, “That day did not work.” Gibs listens with his clipboard closed. He does not yet know what happened; no solution has been promised. Look at the scene, choose the next question and explain your reason before viewing feedback. Then compare your decision with your own practice attempt.
