## [purpose] Purpose

Rehearse an open invitation, one relevant neutral clarification, room for an answer or correction, and a confirmed corrected summary. Keep the exact 1.6.2 objective. This is not diagnosis, teach-back or formal reporting.

## [time-materials] Time and materials

90 guided minutes: 10 opening + 15 demonstration + 30 three-round triads + 20 retry/correction + 10 debrief + 5 exit check = 90. This is part of 90 + 90 + 120 + 90 + 90 = 480 minutes, not an additional eight-hour program. Prepare cards and question aids for ten triads, paper and pens. No real resident records. Internet and a projector are optional. Authored independent estimate: 10–13 minutes, using the longer language track. Actual six-screen narration is 363.89 seconds in English and 395.99 seconds in Filipino; allow 3–6 minutes for the check, brief rehearsal and reflection. Optional story or replay is additional.

## [prepare] Prepare

For 30 participants, form ten triads. Give the answer and correction only to the resident role; the BHW should not receive them in advance. Explain that every scenario is fictional. Check preferred language and reading support. Personal disclosure and forced eye contact are unnecessary. Confirm the authorized purpose, access and urgent-help process with the local health team before using this in an actual visit.

## [opening] Opening · 10 minutes

Ask: “How does What happened? differ from Did you not want to go?” Invite two examples of assumed motives. Show the unresolved check scene without its ending. Say: “A barrier is information. Liza explains; we do not guess.” Briefly connect to respectful listening in 1.6.1.

## [steps] Demonstration · 15 minutes

Read open-question and clarify-detail. Demonstrate the incorrect travel-companion interpretation, then allow Liza to correct it. Exact sequence: “What happened?” → “I had nobody with me.” → “Someone to travel with you?” → “No. Someone to watch my child.” → “Thank you for clarifying.” Demonstrate confirm-summary: always → not always → when my sister is away → “Is that right?” → agreement to check hours first. Ask: “Which word changed? Where was the correction accepted?” This dialogue is an authored adaptation, not a DOH quotation.

## [expected-answers] Expected answers

An open invitation does not supply a motive. Round one means childcare, not a travel companion. Round two means that afternoon, not every afternoon. In round three, unwillingness is the neighbor’s claim; unclear service hours are Liza’s own answer. A forgotten date or an unchecked service stays uncertain. Liza must be asked to confirm the corrected summary.

## [misconception] Common errors

Examples: “You just do not want to?”; three questions at once; turning sometimes into always; insisting on a first guess; treating the neighbor’s statement as Liza’s answer; inventing a date, appointment or childcare. Response: pause, ask one relevant detail, allow an answer, and return the corrected summary. Yes/no can confirm a detail; it cannot replace an open invitation.

## [practice] Three rounds · 30 minutes; retry · 20 minutes

Each round takes 10 minutes: 1 to read cards, 4 for conversation, 2 for exact feedback, 3 to retry. Round 1: BHW A / Liza B / observer C. Round 2: BHW B / Liza C / observer A. Round 3: BHW C / Liza A / observer B. Everyone practices as BHW once. The facilitator directly samples triads 1–3 in round one, 4–6 in round two, and 7–10 in round three; unobserved performances of other learners remain not observed. During the 20-minute retry, give a precise prompt and repeat the sequence; schedule later direct observation for unsampled learners. Debrief for 10 minutes: “Which guess was corrected? What remains unknown? Which next step alone was confirmed?”

## [answer-key] Exit check · 5 minutes

First: “What got in the way that day?” gives Liza room to explain in her own words. Second: “You did not want to go?” assumes a motive before hearing her answer. Third: the reminder question immediately limits the story to one possible cause. In the fictional continuation, Liza says, “Nobody could watch my child.” “Only on that day?” Gibs clarifies. “Yes, my sister was away then.” “On that day, nobody could watch your child because your sister was away. Is that right?” Liza confirms the summary. She agrees to check the current service hours first; the date of her visit is still unconfirmed. No appointment or childcare is promised.

In the final 5 minutes, each participant chooses and explains first; only then read every rationale and the ending. The MCQ does not establish direct performance evidence.

## [observe] Direct observation

One indicator at objective_index 0: open invitation → one relevant neutral clarification → room for an answer/correction → confirmed corrected summary. Record exact words, round, observer and prompts. Ready: complete independent sequence. Needs practice: sequence achieved with prompts or summary needs a retry. Not yet: in an observed attempt, insists on a motive or incorrect summary. No evidence / not observed is a separate status; absence of observation is not a low performance grade. Peer feedback, completion and watching are not facilitator grading or certification.

## [support] Alternatives and support

Alone, write and read both roles with the question aid; this is practice, and direct observation still needs arranging. Without internet or a projector, use printed cards and read the dialogue. Offer a reading assistant, larger print, pauses, or spoken/written responses in the preferred language. Keep the same standard; an assistant must not answer for the learner. No personal disclosure or resident upload.
