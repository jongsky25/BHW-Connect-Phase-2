## [purpose] Purpose

In a simulated household interview, ask permission, establish a comfortable setting and summarize the concern without blame.

## [time-materials] Time Materials

90 minutes of guided practice within the same eight-hour communication program. The five lesson allocations (90 + 90 + 120 + 90 + 90) and the shared 480-minute run sheet are two views of one program, not additive hours. The 90-minute lesson allocation is authored, not an official standalone minimum. Print the Gibs/Liza role cards, observer checklist and one-page listening aid in the practice kit; use paper and pens. No real personal records. Authored independent estimate: 8–11 minutes, using the longer language track. Actual six-screen narration is 245.81 seconds in English and 273.16 seconds in Filipino; allow 3–6 minutes for the check and reflection. Optional story or replay is additional.

## [prepare] Prepare

Prepare ten triads for 30 participants, numbered 1–10. Confirm the authorized purpose, information use, privacy arrangements and urgent-help process with the local team. Explain that all roles and details are fictional. Print the kit before a no-internet session; without a projector, read the scene aloud and pass the printed illustrations. Offer spoken or written participation and reading assistance.

## [opening] Opening

Opening and setting choice, 10 minutes: show the crowded scene. Ask what Gibs needs to check before continuing. A glance or silence is a cue to ask about comfort, never a diagnosis. Let the participant choose language, place and support-person preferences.

## [steps] Steps

Worked demonstration, 15 minutes: model permission → setting/language → uninterrupted account → summary and accuracy check. Gibs says “May we talk?” Liza chooses the quieter place and Filipino. He puts his phone away, invites her account and allows a pause. Liza explains the childcare barrier. He asks whether his summary is right; she adds “when my sister is away” and he checks the corrected summary. No childcare or appointment is promised. Keep detailed clarification in 1.6.2, teach-back in 1.6.3, recording in 1.6.4 and formal handoff in 1.6.5.

## [expected-answers] Expected Answers

Expected: explain purpose and relevant authorized use; invite permission and choices; listen without interruption or blame; summarize as a question Liza can correct. Comfortable eye contact is optional. Neither a neighbor’s answer nor extra personal detail makes a profile accurate.

## [misconception] Misconception

Misconception: “A quiet person has nothing to say, so another person may answer for them.” Correct it by asking Liza what makes talking comfortable and returning the opportunity to her. A pause is not proof of refusal, deception, depression or abuse.

## [practice] Practice

Triad practice and rotation, 30 minutes: three 10-minute rounds. Each round gives 6 minutes for the conversation, 2 for observer feedback and 2 for repeating a missed step. Rotate Gibs/BHW, Liza and observer so everyone practices the BHW role. The assessor directly samples triads 1–3 in round 1, 4–6 in round 2 and 7–10 in round 3; record the individual BHW performances actually seen. Observer feedback and repeated attempts, 20 minutes: first arrange direct observation for the unsampled participants; if time is insufficient, record “not yet observed” and schedule another observed attempt. Peer feedback never supplies an assessor rating. Solo: speak both roles aloud using the kit, self-check, repeat; later obtain direct observation. Use fictional details only.

## [answer-key] Answer Key

Check and debrief, 10 minutes. Correct answer: 2. First: the neighbor’s answer may not be Liza’s; do not treat it as her answer. Second: ask Liza about comfort and choices so she can speak for herself. This is the correct first step. Third: advice before listening may miss the actual concern. In the ending, Liza chooses a quieter place and Filipino. She explains that visits are difficult when her sister cannot look after her child. Gibs summarizes this as a question and Liza confirms it. No childcare or appointment is promised. Ask for the reason before revealing feedback. All three choices have a written rationale.

## [observe] Observe

At objective index 0, observe permission; setting and language choice; time for an uninterrupted account; paraphrase; accuracy check. Mark each action done / prompted / not yet and record exact words. Ready: performs all five without prompting. Needs practice: listens but needs a prompt for permission or checking the summary; repeat that step. Not yet: guesses or accepts another person’s answers without consulting Liza. Keep “not observed” distinct from “not yet able.” A quiz answer cannot substitute for observed performance.

## [support] Support

Transfer action, 5 minutes: rehearse the opening for the next authorized visit, without uploading a resident’s private account. Without internet, use the printed kit and narrated demonstration by the facilitator. Without a projector, read dialogue aloud and pass pictures. For solo practice, record only your fictional rehearsal words. Allow preferred language, reading support and written responses; preserve the five action criteria. Ordinary interview permission must not delay urgent assistance under the local process.
