## [purpose] Purpose

In a sensitive conversation, acknowledge the concern, give one next step within the BHW role and ask the person to explain it in their own words.

## [time-materials] Time and materials

120 minutes of facilitated practice within the subchapter's 8 hours; short Read completion does not replace that training. Use the scenario and checklist below, paper and a pen. No real personal records in practice.

## [prepare] Prepare

Verify the local referral contact and emergency pathway. Use the lesson scenarios, not participants’ personal experiences.

## [opening] Opening

During teach-back, Liza describes a different plan. What next?

## [steps] Steps

Read the situation. Model one attempt against the Observe checklist. Run the practice in pairs or triads. Give specific feedback and repeat the part needing improvement. Ask the check before revealing the explanation.

## [expected-answers] Expected answers

The third response repairs the explanation and checks again. Blame may silence the person; faster repetition does not address the misunderstanding.

## [misconception] Common misconception

Diagnoses or selects treatment, or delays help in immediate danger. Hear the concern and check the clarity of your explanation while staying within the BHW role.

## [practice] Practice activity

Choose Jo, Ben or Ramon. In triads, play BHW, resident and observer. Acknowledge the concern, give one safe next step and invite an explanation in the resident’s own words. The observer identifies one phrase to change; repeat. Alone, write both sides. At the next visit, use teach-back with one approved message.

## [answer-key] Answer key

3. Explain another way and ask her to describe the step again.

The third response repairs the explanation and checks again. Blame may silence the person; faster repetition does not address the misunderstanding.

## [observe] Observe

Acknowledges without judgment, offers one appropriate step, uses teach-back and repairs misunderstanding, and refers when the concern exceeds scope or poses immediate danger.

Ready: Acknowledges without judgment, offers one appropriate step, uses teach-back and repairs misunderstanding, and refers when the concern exceeds scope or poses immediate danger. Completes this without prompting.

Needs practice: Offers an appropriate step but relies on a nod; model a teach-back question and repeat.

Not yet: Diagnoses or selects treatment, or delays help in immediate danger.

Mark each criterion done / prompted / not yet and record the exact words or action. Peer feedback is practice; staff observations cover only performances actually observed. One demonstration does not establish that everyone is ready.

## [support] Support

Offers an appropriate step but relies on a nod; model a teach-back question and repeat. Allow spoken or written responses and reading assistance. Use the participant’s preferred language while keeping the same criteria.
