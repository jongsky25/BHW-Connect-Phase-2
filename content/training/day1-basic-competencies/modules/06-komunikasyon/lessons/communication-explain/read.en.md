## [teach-back] One step, then hear it back

Gibs, a male BHW, listens to Liza, 29. “It is hard to go when nobody can look after my child,” Liza says. “Your concern matters. If you want, we can ask the midwife about scheduling,” Gibs replies. He promises neither an appointment nor childcare.

Give one step in plain words: “First we will ask the midwife when she can be contacted.” Then ask: “To check that I explained clearly, how would you describe the next step in your own words?”

“Do I have an appointment tomorrow?” Liza asks. “Not yet. My explanation was unclear,” Gibs replies. “We will ask about scheduling first; there is no confirmed appointment. What will we do first?” “Ask about the schedule; I am not booked yet,” Liza says. “Yes, that is the first step,” Gibs confirms.

Teach-back checks the BHW’s explanation, not the person’s intelligence or memory. Explain, ask, repair the misunderstanding, and check again. Use a helpful picture and the person’s preferred language; stay within approved messages and the BHW role.

## [family-planning] Room for questions and choice

Jo, 24, is worried about something she heard about family planning. “What specifically worries you?” Gibs asks. He listens without mocking her belief. “I do not want someone choosing for me,” Jo says. “You decide; you can raise your concern with a qualified provider,” Gibs replies.

Use only general information from approved materials and your own training. Choosing an appropriate method and clinical counseling are discussions between Jo and a midwife or qualified provider. Gibs does not select a method or prescribe. He does not pressure Jo to decide or disclose private details.

In this fictional rehearsal, the contact’s name and hours are unknown. “If you want, I will ask the health team for the appropriate contact and how to request a private conversation. The schedule is not confirmed. To check that I explained clearly, what is the first step?” “Find out the contact first; not choose a method now,” Jo replies.

Before an actual visit, verify the contact, BHW role and arrangements for a private conversation with the local health team. Explain approved information use and who needs to receive it under local policy. Do not promise absolute secrecy.

## [distress] Listen without diagnosing

Ben, 37, says he has felt low for several weeks. Gibs might ask, “Thank you for telling me. What feels hardest right now?” Ben may choose not to share details. Ask whether the setting is comfortable; do not infer a diagnosis from silence, posture or looking away.

“I want someone to talk to, but I do not know who,” Ben says. “I can listen. If you want, I can help identify a qualified health professional through the local health team,” Gibs replies. He does not say “just think happy thoughts,” diagnose, or choose treatment.

In rehearsal, the contact’s name and availability are unknown. “I do not have a confirmed time yet. How do you understand our first step?” “Find out the contact first; this is not an appointment yet,” Ben replies. If the meaning differs, explain another way and ask again.

In a real immediate danger to safety, seek help at once according to verified local emergency procedures; do not delay for an ordinary interview or report. Do not invent a number or a universal protocol. Do not promise to conceal danger from people who need to help.

## [smoking] Discuss without blame

Ramon, 45, feels attacked when called “undisciplined.” “May we discuss smoking and the support you want?” Gibs asks. If Ramon does not want to discuss it now, respect his answer. “I want to cut down, but it is hard when I am stressed,” Ramon says.

“You want a change, and stress is a barrier. Is that right?” Gibs checks. Listen to the answer. Offer only a step within the BHW role: ask the local health team about a qualified contact and support actually available. Neither a service nor a time is confirmed in this fictional situation.

“If you want, we can first find out who you could speak with. To check that I explained clearly, what will we do first?” “Will you give me medicine?” Ramon asks. “I will not give or choose medicine. We will identify the contact so you can ask a qualified provider. How would you describe the first step?” “Find out the contact; medication is a discussion with the provider,” Ramon replies.

No lecture, medication, dose or promised outcome. Before an actual conversation, verify available local support and authorized messages; do not invent a service.

## [practice] Three situations, everyone tries

Use fictional cards for Jo, Ben and Ramon. No real resident record or forced personal story. In triads, play Gibs or BHW, resident, and observer. Rotate roles over three rounds so everyone plays BHW and the group practices all three situations.

BHW: acknowledge the concern without blame, explain one correct step in plain words, and ask for understanding in the person’s own words. Resident: use the concern and misunderstanding on the card. Observer: record one helpful exact phrase and one phrase to change; do not assign a facilitator grade.

Repair the confusing explanation and check again. End with the resident describing the correct step, including what remains unconfirmed. Contact details and availability are not supplied on the cards; checking with the health team is the step, not a guaranteed service.

Alone, write or speak both sides of the fictional conversation and repeat the repair and recheck. A quiet pair, reading assistance, a preferred language, and practice without internet or a projector are available. The standard stays the same; direct facilitator observation is still required. At the next authorized visit, use teach-back with one approved message.

## [check] Choose a respectful repair

Gibs and Liza agree to ask the midwife about scheduling first. There is no confirmed appointment. During teach-back, Liza says, “I have an appointment tomorrow.” That differs from Gibs’s explanation.

What should he do next? Choose an answer and explain why. After choosing, read all three rationales and the conversation’s ending. Compare it with your practice. Answering the check or watching the optional story does not replace an actual demonstration.
