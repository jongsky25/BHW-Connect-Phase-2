## [sources] Reported, observed, and unconfirmed

Gibs, a male BHW, visits Liza, 29, for an authorized household profile. He asks permission and listens first. Liza says: “Getting there is difficult when nobody can look after my two children.” The neighbor says: “She is not interested.” This rehearsal is not an actual record. Separate four columns: Liza’s report, the neighbor’s report, direct observations, and unconfirmed details. When the appointment card is requested, Gibs observes that no card is shown during the conversation. This does not prove that Liza had no appointment or did not attend. Gibs has not seen the authorized attendance record. The visit date and status remain unconfirmed. A nod, silence or glance toward the door is only an observation, not proof of agreement, refusal, feelings or a diagnosis.

## [relevant-detail] Only relevant details

“This is for an authorized household profile and health-service planning,” Gibs explains. “Only relevant information will be used by the authorized health team under local policy. May we clarify the barrier to getting there?” Liza chooses Filipino and a quieter place. The access barrier is relevant to this task; gossip about the neighbor’s debt is not. Real names or addresses are unnecessary on the rehearsal sheet. Do not guess missing entries on an official form. Explaining information use is not a promise of absolute secrecy. For questions about correction, access or keeping records, ask the authorized custodian about the actual local process; do not invent a universal form or deletion date. If immediate help is needed, follow the local process and do not delay it to finish the interview.

## [conflicting-accounts] When accounts differ

Liza says: “The schedule was canceled.” The neighbor says: “She did not want to go.” Gibs does not choose a story because one voice is louder. “What do you mean by canceled?” he asks Liza. “I could not go when my sister was away. I am not sure whether the center canceled it,” she clarifies. Gibs summarizes: “You could not go when your sister was away; the schedule status is still uncertain. Is that right?” “Yes,” Liza replies. Record this as Liza’s clarification, keeping the source of the first statement and its correction clear. If the neighbor’s report is relevant to the task, attribute it to the neighbor; it is not proof of Liza’s motive. Trust and careful verification work together. Do not label a resident dishonest or noncompliant merely because accounts differ.

## [verify-gap] Verify the gap and correct honestly

Incorrect initial note: “The center canceled the appointment.” It treats unconfirmed information as established. Gibs corrects the fictional practice sheet: “Liza first reported a canceled schedule. Liza clarified during the conversation that she could not attend when her sister was away and was unsure whether the center canceled it. No card was shown. Date and status: not yet confirmed.” He does not replace one guess with another. One suitable step is to ask the authorized midwife to check the relevant attendance record with him under the local access procedure. Ask only what is needed; do not investigate Liza’s entire life. Liza confirms the summary of her own report and is willing to clarify the date she remembers. That is the confirmed ending of the conversation, not confirmation of attendance or a new appointment. Until suitable verification is available, keep the gap marked “not yet confirmed.” In a real record, follow the local correction process and preserve the source of the change.

## [practice] Try it, then repeat

First draw three boxes: “Reported,” “Observed,” and “Unconfirmed.” Split the first box into “Liza reports” and “neighbor reports” to make the recording aid’s four columns. Sort the fictional source cards into the correct column. Write a short note containing Liza’s clarification and one step to verify the date. Check it for guesses, diagnoses, judgments or irrelevant details. In a triad, rotate as BHW, resident and observer through three rounds so everyone attempts the BHW role. The observer records exact evidence and one step to repeat; this is feedback, not a facilitator grade. Alone, sort paper cards, underline sources and correct the note. Cards may be read aloud or used in the preferred language. No internet, projector, real resident record or upload is needed. Repeat the difficult part before continuing.
