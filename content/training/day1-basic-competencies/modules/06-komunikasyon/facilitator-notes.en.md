# Facilitator guide — Effective Communication

## Timing and preparation
The source weighting is 8 hours (480 minutes): 30 opening/demonstration, 60 listening/open questions, 60 assessment/recording, 60 plain messaging/teach-back, 120 four role-play stations, 60 reporting/peer feedback, 60 repeat practice/competency observation, 30 retrieval/closing. Split across sessions and add breaks. Reading the app quickly does not constitute eight hours of training or automatic NC II certification.
Prepare blank profile/report forms, fictional scenario cards and the rubric. Use no real patient records.

## Delivery script
1. **Why Liza is quiet / Listen:** Ask, “What should we notice before filling in the form?” Demonstrate a rushed encounter, then repeat with permission, privacy and pauses. Ask learners to name the exact change they heard.
2. **Open first / Reported, observed:** Each pair writes an open question and one clarification. Ask, “Which detail did we hear, and which did we guess?” Sort reports, observations and uncertainty on paper.
3. **Report / One message:** Have learners write Liza’s handoff and explain one next step. Model teach-back with the speaker taking responsibility for clarity. Use the diagram to show the workflow.
4. **Whole conversation / Household profiling:** Pair learners; observers record one piece of evidence for each objective. Correct the question, not the actor’s character.
5. **Four scenarios:** Rotate profiling, family planning, low mood and smoking stations. Family planning leads to midwife counseling; low mood calls for listening and qualified referral; smoking starts with the person’s goal and health-center support. These are communication exercises, not clinical-management examinations.
6. **Rosario / Audience / Improve the handoff:** Ask, “What barrier would yes-or-no questions miss?” Connect this to Module 7. Replace the judgmental report with a factual handoff.
7. **Deep sections:** Use the three-role exercise, differing accounts and one-minute meeting report for extended practice. At short density, still use the core exercise to observe all four objectives.

## Misconception: “A nod means it is clear”
Say, “We have all nodded when part of an explanation was still missing.” Model teach-back and a revised explanation without shaming anyone.

## Discussion and evidence
Which phrase gives someone room to speak? Which detail must stay out of public chat? What if a date is uncertain? Collect the fictional handoff and observe the role-play against all four indicators. A quiz score does not replace observation. Give one specific improvement and a chance to repeat.


## Retrieval-check answer key

- **Open first, then clarify:** “What got in the way of attending?” — It explores the barrier before deciding what caused it or how to solve it.

- **Reported, observed, and unconfirmed:** Date not yet confirmed; check the record. — A missing document is an information gap, not proof of nonattendance.

- **One message, then hear it back:** Invite her to explain the agreed step in her own words. — A nod may simply express politeness. Her own explanation shows whether the message was clear.
