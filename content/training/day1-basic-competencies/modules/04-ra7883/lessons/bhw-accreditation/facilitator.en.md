## [purpose] Purpose

Support the lesson’s single objective: explain and apply the accreditation process in a situation. Learners distinguish local health board responsibilities from committee assistance, separate known facts from missing information, and form a specific question about the process and official decision record. Do not determine any learner’s eligibility or accreditation status. Fictional Demi and Cards A–C are authored practice, not cases from a source or actual board decisions.

## [time-materials] Time and materials

Retain the existing 30-minute lesson share within the 180-minute module. Authored teaching split: opening 3 minutes; roles and records 8; fictional cards 6; question practice 5; application check 5; supportive re-practice 3. Optional story and replays are outside this split. Measure self-study against the completed encoded narration before final packaging; it is an authored estimate, not a TESDA accreditation requirement.

Use the six lesson screens, blank paper, three clearly fictional cards, role cards and a source sheet. Role cards read “committee: screen, interview, recommend, help maintain registry” and “board: act, record decision, issue certificates to qualified BHWs.” Never request real certificates, personal service records, accreditation status or a completed application. The digital illustration’s blank folder is a discussion prop.

## [prepare] Prepare

Read the source audit and confirm current local guidance with the responsible office before facilitation. Know the locally confirmed RHU, municipal/city health office or board contact; do not invent an office, delegation, form, fee, deadline, registry platform or certificate format. Explain that RA 7883 is the statute, the signed DILG–DOH circular is national administrative guidance, the facilitator guide is a training summary, and specific LGU orders apply in their respective localities. Complete national IRR and amendment history remain unverified in this audit.

Prepare Cards A–C using only the stated fictional facts. A: training certificate only. B: committee recommendation, no verified board decision. C: a resolution is mentioned, but its process, scope and verification contact are unknown. Leave all personal fields blank. Allow speaking, writing or pointing to the role cards.

## [opening] Opening

Show Demi’s new clinic scene. Ask: “Who reviews an application, and how could an official decision be verified?” Accept uncertainty as a useful starting point. State that asking, attending training or holding a title does not establish completion of registration or accreditation. No personal status disclosure is expected. Read the objective in the learner’s chosen language.

## [steps] Steps

1. Use the preserved section-5 screen to explain the board’s eight listed duties. Group them as process/decision/record, certificates/registry/monitoring, and benefits/funding. Grouping is an authored teaching aid. Benefit review using local funds and an accreditation decision are separate decisions.
2. Compare committee and board role cards. The board may create a committee; the circular encourages its creation. If absent or pending creation, the board still facilitates registration/accreditation. A committee recommendation alone does not establish an official board decision.
3. Model Card A: the known fact is training evidence; the missing fact is the actual registration/accreditation process and next step. Ask the locally confirmed office. Do not promise a result.
4. Have pairs compare B and C, identifying one known fact, one gap and one specific verification question.
5. Model Demi’s Card B question. Learners explain the role distinction before asking about the official decision, record and next step.
6. Complete the three-choice check, discuss all reasons and offer another fictional card for re-practice. Optional story supports learning but is independent of completion.

## [expected-answers] Expected explanations

The local health board has responsibility for acting on applications and recording decisions. A committee, where created, assists with screening, interviews, recommendations and registry work. The proper local arrangement needs confirmation. A recommendation is not automatic approval. Training, registration and accreditation evidence have different purposes. BHWE and job titles do not substitute for accreditation. A resolution mentioned in an unverified card requires checking its process and scope, not guessing a person’s status.

A useful response names the process, known fact, missing information and appropriate local contact. Learners need not recite all eight duties; they must explain the role/record distinction and apply it accurately.

## [misconception] Misconception and feedback

“The committee recommended it, so accreditation is automatic.” Say: “You noticed a relevant step. What remains unverified is the board’s official decision. Which record and office could clarify that?”

“A training certificate or job title replaces the process.” Say: “That fact may be relevant, but it is different from proof of registration or accreditation. Let us name the missing step without promising approval.”

Do not shame a learner for uncertainty. Do not turn a fictional card into a personal assessment, legal ruling or actual application instruction.

## [practice] Fictional role and process cards

Use a four-column blank sheet: known fact / responsible role / missing information / specific question. Worked A: “training certificate / local board process, health office guidance / next registration or accreditation step / which office confirms the current process and evidence?”

Worked B: “committee recommendation / committee assists, board acts / official decision and record / which office confirms whether the board decided, where it can be verified and what comes next?”

Worked C: “resolution mentioned / board record, authorized local contact / process and scope / which official record confirms what decision was made and whom it covers?” A mention alone proves neither approval nor refusal. No actual records are requested.

Partners exchange roles: one explains, the other asks for the missing information. Swap cards and repeat. Keep written output fictional; record only the observation level and brief feedback.

## [answer-key] Application check and reasons

Choice 1 is correct: committee recommendation and official board decision are different. The card leaves the decision and record unverified; the specific local question addresses that gap and the next step without asserting status.

Choice 2 is incorrect: screening or recommendation alone is not automatic approval. It does not establish that the board acted, recorded a decision or issued the appropriate certificate.

Choice 3 is incorrect: a training certificate, BHWE or a job title cannot replace registration/accreditation. These serve different purposes and do not establish the card’s official decision or status. Completing the online lesson also grants no accreditation.

After any choice, ask the learner to explain the correct distinction and revise one question. Provide the full equivalent Filipino or English rationale, including reasons for the unselected options.

## [observe] Observe and record

Use the one indicator aligned to the lesson objective. Observe the learner explaining board/committee roles, distinguishing known facts from a missing decision/record, and forming a bounded process question. Use the three levels in the chosen language: kaya na, kailangan ng practice, or hindi pa. Judge the explanation and application, not a real credential, accent, literacy speed or private status.

Online completion is separate from observed competency. Correctly selecting an option alone is insufficient evidence of an independent explanation. A learner may consult the source sheet; record prompts needed and give one concrete next practice step.

## [support] Support and re-practice

Sentence frames: “The committee can help with __; the board is responsible for __.” “The card says __; we still need to verify __.” “Which locally confirmed office can clarify __ and the next step?”

For reading support, read a card aloud and let the learner point to the role cards. For confidence support, model one answer, invite a partner explanation and then re-practise with a different fictional card. Give specific feedback: “You separated the recommendation from the decision. Now name the record or contact to verify.” No forced public disclosure.

## [sources-review] Sources and review limits

RA 7883 sections 3–4 and 7 support accreditation authority, registration/proof and local-fund review. The official signed DILG–DOH JMC No. 2023-001, 24 August 2023, PDF 2–7/printed 2–7, supports committee creation guidance, board duties and municipal/city health office assistance. Original 126-page Drive facilitator guide PDF 25/printed 18 summarizes board/committee duties; the 150-page TCI version’s corresponding page is PDF 27/printed 18. These offsets were independently visually checked. The guide is not the complete IRR. Official Roxas and Marilao orders corroborate committee tasks only within those LGUs.

The original guide’s PDF 23/printed 16 recommends three hours for the whole competency. Official TESDA Barangay Health Services NC II Revision 01, promulgated 11 January 2019, unit 400311213, PDF 19–20/printed 15–16 and PDF 86–87/printed 82–83, independently allocates three one-hour outcomes. Neither establishes a separate TESDA 30-minute accreditation requirement; retain the existing course allocation.
