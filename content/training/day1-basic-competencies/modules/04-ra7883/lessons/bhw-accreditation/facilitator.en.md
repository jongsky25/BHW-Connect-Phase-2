## [purpose] Purpose

By the end of this short lesson the BHW can explain that accreditation runs through a system: the local health board, with responsibilities under Section 5 of the RA 7883 IRR, and possibly a BHW Registration and Accreditation Committee. They can also say what to bring to an accreditation follow-up: their documents and a specific question about status or missing requirements.

## [time-materials] Time and materials

- **Time:** about 30 minutes in a face-to-face session. This is "The registration process (local health board, committee)" part of the 3-hour plan in the subchapter guide. Self-study of the lesson alone takes about 5 minutes.
- **Materials:** the lesson slide (with the draft "understand, verify, practice" visual); eight cards, each with one of the local health board's responsibilities as the lesson lists them; manila paper and markers; if your LGU has them, a sample certificate of registration or accreditation and the name of the committee or office that handles applications.

## [prepare] Prepare

- Read the lesson in both Read and Slides so you know exactly what the BHW has seen.
- Ask your RHU or LGU: does your local health board have a BHW Registration and Accreditation Committee? Who should BHWs ask about their application? What documents does your LGU require? The lesson does not list documents or timelines. Use only what your LGU confirms. The Facilitator Guide says local registration and accreditation processes may be presented during training. If you can, invite someone from the RHU or the local health board secretariat for ten minutes.
- This lesson covers **who is part of the accreditation process and what they do**. Do not invent document lists, processing times or fees. Do not promise anyone that their application will be approved.

## [opening] Opening

Ask the group: *"How would you explain to a new BHW why there's an accreditation process before they can start?"* Take two or three answers. Then ask: *"And who do you think decides — one person, or a group?"* Write the answers on manila paper and come back to them at step 3.

## [steps] Steps

1. **The local health board's responsibilities (Read "Who makes up the accreditation process", first paragraph; the slide).** Read that these responsibilities come from Section 5 of the RA 7883 IRR. Hand out the eight cards. Ask each card-holder to read theirs aloud in order.
2. **What leaves a record.** Ask: *"Which of these responsibilities leaves a paper you could ask about later?"* Listen for the board resolution or meeting minutes, the certificate of registration or accreditation, and the BHW registry given to the DOH. These are what a follow-up can point to.
3. **Not one person.** Go back to the opening answers. Read the takeaway: accreditation isn't decided by one person alone. It runs through a system, with assigned responsibility and a registry that's kept up to date. Say, as in the module script: *"You aren't waiting alone. There's a system, and a responsible person or committee behind it."*
4. **The committee (second paragraph).** Read that the local health board *may* create a BHW Registration and Accreditation Committee, which evaluates applications, interviews applicants, recommends action to the board and keeps the registry updated. Ask: *"Do we have one here? Who is in it?"* If you don't know, say so and write it down as something to confirm.
5. **What to bring.** Point to the slide line *"Keep documents and clarify the next step."* Ask: *"If you went to ask about your accreditation, what would you bring and what would you ask?"*
6. **Self-check.** Give the check question (*"What should you bring to an accreditation follow-up?"*). Let the group discuss before you reveal the answer.

## [expected-answers] Expected answers and how to respond

- **"The midwife (or the captain) is the one who decides."** Acknowledge that these people often help locally. Then point to the cards: *"The lesson says the local health board evaluates, records the decision and issues the certificate. Others may help, but the decision is recorded by the board."*
- **"I don't have a certificate. Am I not accredited?"** Do not judge their status. Say: *"That is a good specific question to bring to the local health board or your supervisor: 'What is the status of my accreditation, and do I have a certificate?'"*
- **"What documents do we need?"** Give only what your LGU has confirmed. If you don't know, say: *"The lesson doesn't list them, and it can differ by LGU. Let's find out from the local health board."*
- **"It's taking forever and no one tells us anything."** Accept the frustration. Say: *"That's exactly what the next lesson is about: how to follow up calmly and specifically."*

## [misconception] Common misconception

**"Accreditation depends on one person's approval, so if you're on good terms with the right person you'll get it."** People say this because the process is often invisible to them. Do not challenge anyone's experience. Show the system instead. Lay out the eight cards and ask: *"How many of these are about one person's feelings?"* None of them are. The board evaluates, records the decision in a resolution or minutes, issues a certificate and keeps a registry. Say: *"Because it's written down, you can ask about it."*

## [practice] Practice activity

Pairs, 2 minutes each, then swap:

- One BHW plays a new BHW who says: *"I submitted my papers months ago. Who do I even ask? Is it just up to the midwife?"*
- The other explains in their own words: (1) who handles accreditation, the local health board and possibly its registration and accreditation committee; (2) one record that exists, such as the board resolution or minutes, the certificate or the registry; (3) the first action, which is to go with copies of their documents and a specific question about status or missing requirements; and (4) what they should confirm locally, such as which office or committee to go to.

Two or three pairs present. The group gives one strength and one suggestion.

## [answer-key] Self-check answer key

This lesson has one check. It appears after the Read section "Who makes up the accreditation process" and again on that section's slide. It is the same check, so it is covered once here.

- **"What should you bring to an accreditation follow-up?"** → **Documents and specific questions about status or missing requirements.** The board evaluates applications and records its decisions, so a follow-up works best when you can show your papers and ask a specific question. The slide says: keep documents and clarify the next step.
  - *"A promise that no process is needed"* is wrong. The lesson describes a process the local health board must safeguard: evaluating applications, recording decisions and issuing certificates. No one can promise to skip it.

## [observe] What to observe

Use the lesson's observation indicator during the practice activity. The indicator is: *"Explains the main point and an appropriate first action in the situation, including what must be checked with the supervisor."*

- **Kaya na:** without prompting, the BHW explains that the local health board, and possibly its committee, handles accreditation. They name bringing documents and a specific status question as the first action, and say what to confirm locally.
- **Kailangan pa ng practice:** the BHW points in the right direction, for example "ask the health board", but needs your prompting to mention the documents, a specific question, or which office to confirm.
- **Hindi pa:** the BHW says it all depends on one person, suggests going without documents just to "ask when", or promises the new BHW their accreditation will come through.

Observing this in the room is separate from the BHW finishing the online self-check. A correct self-check answer is not evidence of the skill.

## [support] If a BHW needs more support

- Narrow the task. Ask them only for the first action: what to bring and one specific question. Add the "who handles it" part once that is solid.
- Give a sentence frame: *"Accreditation is handled by ___. I'll bring ___ and ask, 'What is the status of ___, and is anything missing?'"*
- Pair them with a BHW rated **Kaya na** and let them go second.
- Re-observe at the next session using the same new-BHW scenario. The follow-up lesson's role-play gives another chance to see it.
