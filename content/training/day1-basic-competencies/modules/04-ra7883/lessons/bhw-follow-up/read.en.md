## [follow-up-question] What does Demi know?

In this fictional continuation, Demi is waiting for news about her accreditation application. She knows she provided an application. She has not confirmed whether it was received and complete, who is handling it, or whether a decision exists. She does not know the cause of the delay.

At the planning corner, a colleague helps her prepare a question. Fear, cost or difficulty travelling and communicating may be barriers. She can seek support and choose an appropriate way to ask. The story video is optional; you can complete Read or Slides without watching it.

## [section-6] Pause and ask a specific question

If helpful, pause and breathe before asking. Follow-up can be respectful and firm even when you feel worried or frustrated. You do not need to hide your feelings to form a clear question.

With a locally confirmed contact, Demi might say: “Good day. I would like to ask about my accreditation application. Has it been received, and is anything still unclear when checking completeness? Who is the appropriate person to ask, and what is the next step?” This is a fictional example. Confirm the office and channel with the local health team; do not assume every staff member has the same responsibility.

## [known-and-unknown] Which card asks clearly?

Card A: “When will this happen?” It expresses concern but does not identify the application or information requested. Help make the purpose specific. Card B: “About my fictional accreditation application: has it been received, and how can I verify the status and next step?” It names the purpose and asks for unconfirmed information.

Card C: “A document must be lost; I will submit everything again.” It guesses the cause and requirement. Waiting does not prove loss. First ask whether an item needs verification, exactly which item and how to provide it safely through the appropriate channel. Practice uses no real documents.

## [section-7] Listen, repeat and clarify

Before: Demi thinks, “Perhaps it is approved because it has been a long time.” She has not confirmed this. After: in the fictional conversation, the appropriate contact says the application is still being reviewed and no date can be given. This is the example’s response, not a national procedure or timetable.

Demi repeats: “I understand it is still under review and there is no date yet. Is that right? Is there anything I should do now? Through which channel and with whom can I ask for the next update?” She listens to clarification and names any remaining uncertainty. The response establishes neither approval nor a missing document. Constructive follow-up does not guarantee an answer, accreditation, a benefit or an appointment.

## [next-contact-plan] Plan a realistic next contact

If there is no date, ask how to agree on an appropriate next contact. If an item needs verification, clarify the exact item and proper method before acting. If responsibility is unclear, ask the local health team to help identify the contact. There is no single prescribed interval for everyone.

In a practice note, write only the fictional purpose, confirmed response, remaining question and agreed next contact or channel. Use no private details or patient record. A support person, written question or another accessible method may help. Reflect on whether the question was clear and try again after feedback. A confirmed next question is useful even without an outcome.
