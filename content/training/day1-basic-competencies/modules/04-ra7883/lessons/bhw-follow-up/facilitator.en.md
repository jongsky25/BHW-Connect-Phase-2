## [purpose] Purpose

By the end of this short lesson the BHW can follow up on a slow registration or benefit calmly and specifically. In a role-play they ask about the status, any missing documents and the next step, instead of staying silent or complaining with no specific ask.

## [time-materials] Time and materials

- **Time:** about 45 minutes in a face-to-face session. This is the "Exercise: following up calmly + CSC Form 101-H demo" part of the 3-hour plan in the subchapter guide. Self-study of the lesson alone takes about 5 minutes.
- **Materials:** the lesson's two slides; role-play cards for the "local health board staff" part (see Practice activity); manila paper and markers; if you will do the demo, copies of CSC Form 101-H (revised 2011) for BHWs. The Facilitator Guide says it can be downloaded from the CSC website. Check that you have the current version.

## [prepare] Prepare

- Read the lesson in both Read and Slides so you know exactly what the BHW has seen.
- Know which office BHWs in your LGU should follow up with about registration, accreditation or benefits. Confirm it with your RHU or LGU.
- If you plan the CSC Form 101-H demo, fill in a blank copy yourself first so you can show it confidently. Skip the demo if you do not have the form or are unsure how to fill it in. The role-play is the priority.
- This lesson covers **how to follow up**, not what the rules are. Josie following up "every two months" is an example from the lesson, not a required interval. Do not promise that following up guarantees an answer or a benefit.

## [opening] Opening

Ask the group: *"Have you ever waited a long time on a process with no follow-up? What happened?"* Take two or three stories. Thank each person. Do not comment yet on what they should have done.

## [steps] Steps

1. **The question (Read "When the process is slow", first slide).** Read: *"You now know the whole process — but what do you do if months pass with no word?"* Ask the group what they would normally do.
2. **Vague versus specific.** Write both questions from the lesson on manila paper: *"When will this be done?"* and *"What's the status of my application, and is there anything else I need to submit?"* Ask: *"Which one is easier for the staff to answer? Why?"*
3. **Calm first.** Point to the slide: pause before following up, ask about status and missing documents, clarify the next step. Ask: *"What do you do to calm yourself before you walk in?"*
4. **Wrong (Read "Wrong versus right: asking for what you're owed", second slide).** Read the Wrong story. Ask: *"What did Josie's silence cost her?"* The answer is six months, and a missing document that an earlier follow-up would have caught.
5. **Right.** Read the Right story. Ask: *"What did Josie do differently?"* She followed up regularly with a specific question, reminded them calmly, and resubmitted the missing document right away. Note that "every two months" is what Josie did, not a rule.
6. **Self-check.** Give the check question (*"What is a constructive follow-up?"*). Let the group discuss before you reveal the answer.
7. **Role-play** (see Practice activity). Show the difference between a calm question and a complaint.
8. **If there's time,** show an actual CSC Form 101-H and demonstrate how to fill it in. This connects back to Module 1's section on documentation.

## [expected-answers] Expected answers and how to respond

- **"If I follow up, they'll think I'm impatient."** This is exactly what Josie thought. Say: *"Josie thought that too, and it cost her six months. A calm, specific question isn't impatience."*
- **"Sometimes you have to get angry before they act."** Don't lecture. Ask: *"When someone is angry at you at the health station, how easy is it to help them?"* Then point to the lesson: a clear, calm question is more likely to be answered.
- **"I don't know who to ask."** Give them the office you confirmed in Prepare. If you don't know it, say so and write it down to find out.
- **They suggest a written follow-up or asking through the supervisor.** Accept it. The lesson's point is the specific question, not the channel.

## [misconception] Common misconception

**"Staying quiet while you wait is the polite, respectful thing to do."** Many BHWs are raised to wait their turn, so this comes from good manners, not laziness. Correct it with the lesson's takeaway: staying silent in the face of a delay isn't politeness. It often just lets a problem drag on that could have been fixed much sooner. Say: *"Being respectful and following up aren't opposites. You can do both in the same sentence: 'Good morning po, what's the status of my application, and is anything missing?'"*

## [practice] Practice activity

Role-play in threes: one **BHW**, one **local health board staff member**, one **observer**. Rotate so everyone plays the BHW once, about 3 minutes per round.

Give the staff member a card with one of these situations:

- *"The application is still being reviewed. There is no date yet."*
- *"One document is missing from the file."*
- *"You are busy and at first answer only 'Come back next time'."*

The BHW has waited months with no word. They must, calmly: greet, ask about the status, ask whether anything is missing, and ask what the next step is. The observer notes whether the BHW's question was specific and whether they stayed calm. Afterwards, one or two groups replay for everyone. Ask the room: *"Was that a question or a complaint?"*

## [answer-key] Self-check answer key

This lesson has one check. It appears after the Read section "Wrong versus right: asking for what you're owed" and again on that section's slide. It is the same check, so it is covered once here. The first section, "When the process is slow", has no check.

- **"What is a constructive follow-up?"** → **Calmly ask about status, missing documents and the next step.** This is what the lesson recommends: calm yourself first, then ask a specific question. It is also what saved time in Josie's "Right" story.
  - *"Become angry before checking the reason"* is wrong. The lesson says to calm yourself first before frustration takes over. A complaint with no specific ask is less likely to be answered, and the reason for a delay, such as a missing document, is often something you can fix.

## [observe] What to observe

Use the lesson's observation indicator during the role-play. The indicator is: *"Explains the main point and an appropriate first action in the situation, including what must be checked with the supervisor."*

- **Kaya na:** without prompting, the BHW stays calm, asks specifically about status, missing documents and the next step, and still does so when the staff member first brushes them off.
- **Kailangan pa ng practice:** the BHW follows up in the right direction but asks something vague like "when will it be done?", or becomes a little emotional and needs your prompt to ask about missing documents or the next step.
- **Hindi pa:** the BHW stays silent, gives up after the first brush-off, or complains angrily with no specific ask.

For more detail you can also look at the subchapter's competency indicators, objective 4 (a calm, specific question rather than a complaint when facing a delay). Observing this in the room is separate from the BHW finishing the online self-check. A correct self-check answer is not evidence of the skill.

## [support] If a BHW needs more support

- Write the lesson's specific question on a card and let them read it the first time. On the second try, they say it in their own words.
- Give them the easiest staff card first (the missing document), then the brush-off card.
- Pair them with a BHW rated **Kaya na** and let them watch one round as observer before playing the BHW.
- Re-observe at the next session using the same role-play cards, not new ones.
