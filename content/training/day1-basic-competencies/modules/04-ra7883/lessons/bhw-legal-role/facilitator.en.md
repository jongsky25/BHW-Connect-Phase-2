## [purpose] Purpose

By the end of this short lesson the BHW can explain, in their own words and using Josie's situation, that a BHW's duty has a legal basis in RA 7883 and that accreditation comes from the local health board. They can also name one first action for Josie and what she should check with her supervisor instead of guessing.

## [time-materials] Time and materials

- **Time:** about 45 minutes in a face-to-face session. This is the opening (15 min) plus the "Legal basis and accreditation" part (30 min) of the 3-hour plan in the subchapter guide. Self-study of the lesson alone takes about 5 minutes.
- **Materials:** manila paper and markers; the lesson's two slides (with the draft "understand, verify, practice" visual); if you have them, copies of RA 7883 and its Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) to show the group (the Facilitator Guide suggests copies can be given to BHWs); small pieces of paper for anonymous questions.

## [prepare] Prepare

- Read the lesson in both Read and Slides so you know exactly what the BHW has seen.
- Find out, from your RHU or LGU, who handles BHW registration and accreditation for your city or municipality's local health board, and who BHWs should ask about their status. Have that name or office ready.
- Know the one legal line the lesson quotes. Under RA 7883, a BHW "voluntarily renders primary health care services" in the community after receiving proper training and accreditation from the local health board. Read it exactly as written. Do not paraphrase it into a stronger claim.
- This lesson covers **where the duty comes from and who accredits**. The list of benefits, the BHWE conditions and the local health board's duties come in later lessons. Do not teach them in detail here. Do not quote allowance amounts and do not tell anyone they will receive a benefit. The lesson gives no amounts, and conditions apply.

## [opening] Opening

Ask the group: *"Have you heard of a benefit you weren't sure applied to you too? Were you too shy to ask?"* Ask for a show of hands first. Then invite anyone who is willing to write their question on a small piece of paper without their name. Stick the papers on manila paper under the heading "Questions we were shy to ask". Leave it up. Later lessons in this module answer many of them.

## [steps] Steps

1. **Scene (Read "The question BHW Josie couldn't bring herself to ask", first slide).** Read Josie's thought aloud: *"I'm just a volunteer, maybe I'll look greedy if I ask about money."* Ask: *"Why didn't Josie ask? Have you ever felt the same?"*
2. **Asking is not greed.** Point to the lesson's line that goodwill alone is not the basis of Josie's work, and that knowing what she is owed is her right. Ask: *"Is asking about what you're owed the same as being greedy?"* Let the group answer before you give the takeaway.
3. **Where the duty comes from (Read "Where the BHW's duty comes from", second slide).** Read the RA 7883 line exactly as written. Ask: *"According to this, what has to happen before a BHW starts the work?"* The answer is proper training and accreditation from the local health board.
4. **Primary health care and the barangay health station.** Explain that the lesson links the same legal basis to the Primary Health Care Approach and to the barangay health station as a service hub. Ask: *"Where do you render service? Who is your supervisor there?"*
5. **More than an everyday "volunteer".** Ask each BHW to finish this sentence out loud: *"I am a BHW not only because I want to help, but also because ..."* Listen for the law (RA 7883) and accreditation.
6. **Self-check.** Give the check question (*"Who has a role in BHW accreditation?"*). Let the group discuss before you reveal the answer.

## [expected-answers] Expected answers and how to respond

- **"But we really are just volunteers."** This is true, and the lesson does not deny it. Point to the word "voluntarily" in the law: *"Yes, the work is voluntary. And it also has a legal basis. Both are true."*
- **"The midwife (or the barangay captain) accredits us."** Do not say they are wrong about who helps them locally. Say: *"The midwife or RHU may help you with the papers. The lesson says accreditation comes from the local health board."* Confirm the local steps with your RHU or LGU.
- **They ask how much the hazard allowance is.** Do not give or guess an amount. Say: *"Benefits come in the next lesson, and each one has conditions and a process. Let's write your question down so we can check it properly."* Add it to the manila paper.
- **"I don't know if I'm accredited."** This is common and nothing to be ashamed of. Say: *"That is exactly the kind of question to bring to your supervisor or the local health board. We'll practise how to ask it in the follow-up lesson."*

## [misconception] Common misconception

**"I'm just a volunteer, so I shouldn't bring up benefits or money."** This is the usual reason BHWs, especially new ones, don't ask for what they are owed. Don't correct it by saying *"you need to be braver."* That puts the problem on the person. Go back to the legal basis instead. These benefits are written into the law itself. They are not anyone's personal generosity. So asking about them is not greed. It is a right. You can say: *"Josie felt this way too, and she had six months of experience. It's a very common feeling. The law is on your side."*

## [practice] Practice activity

Pairs, 2 minutes each, then swap:

- One BHW plays Josie and asks: *"Am I even allowed to ask about the hazard allowance? I'm only a volunteer."*
- The other plays a fellow BHW. In their own words they explain (1) where Josie's duty comes from, (2) who accredits a BHW, (3) one first action Josie can take, for example writing down her question and asking her supervisor or the local health board office, and (4) what must be checked before anyone promises her anything, such as whether she is registered and accredited and whether the allowance applies to her.

Two or three pairs present to the group. The group gives one strength and one suggestion.

## [answer-key] Self-check answer key

This lesson has one check. It appears after the Read section "Where the BHW's duty comes from" and again on that section's slide. It is the same check, so it is covered once here.

- **"Who has a role in BHW accreditation?"** → **The local health board through the defined process.** The lesson quotes RA 7883: a BHW renders service after training and accreditation from the local health board.
  - *"Only the BHW themself"* is wrong. The BHW takes part by training and applying, but a BHW cannot accredit themself. The lesson places accreditation with the local health board, which is why a process exists before the work begins.

## [observe] What to observe

Use the lesson's observation indicator during the practice activity. The indicator is: *"Explains the main point and an appropriate first action in the situation, including what must be checked with the supervisor."*

- **Kaya na:** without prompting, the BHW explains that the duty has a legal basis in RA 7883, that the local health board accredits, and gives Josie one appropriate first action and what to check with the supervisor.
- **Kailangan pa ng practice:** the BHW goes in the right direction, for example "there's a law for us, you can ask", but needs your follow-up question to name RA 7883, the local health board, or what Josie should check.
- **Hindi pa:** the explanation stays at "we just volunteer because we want to help", with no legal basis or accreditation. Or the BHW promises Josie she will get the allowance without checking the conditions.

Observing this in the room is separate from the BHW finishing the online self-check. A correct self-check answer is not evidence of the skill.

## [support] If a BHW needs more support

- Give a sentence frame and let them practise with it once: *"My duty comes from ___. The one who accredits a BHW is the ___. Before promising anything, I will check ___ with ___."*
- Read the second paragraph of "Where the BHW's duty comes from" aloud together, then ask them to say the RA 7883 line in their own words.
- Pair them with a BHW rated **Kaya na** and let them play the fellow BHW second.
- Re-observe at the next session using the same Josie role-play, not a new scenario. The follow-up lesson's role-play is another chance to see the same explanation.
