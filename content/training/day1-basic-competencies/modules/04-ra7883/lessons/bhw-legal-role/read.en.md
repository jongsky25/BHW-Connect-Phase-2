## [section-2] The definition in RA 7883

Republic Act 7883 is the Barangay Health Workers’ Benefits and Incentives Act of 1995. Section 3 defines a BHW as a person who has undergone training programs under an accredited government or nongovernment organization. The person voluntarily provides primary health care services in the community after accreditation to function as a BHW by the local health board, following Department of Health guidelines.

This is a plain-language explanation of the definition, not a direct quotation. Goodwill or length of service alone does not establish that status. This does not say that RA 7883 created every barangay health station.

## [roles-and-authority] Who helps, and who accredits?

People and offices have different roles. A training provider can help clarify the training record. A supervisor or RHU can identify the current local contact and process. Section 3, however, identifies the local health board as the accreditation authority under DOH guidelines.

Demi does not accredit herself. A fellow BHW’s agreement or a supervisor’s invitation is insufficient proof of a board decision. Ask what evidence and official record can be checked with the appropriate local office; do not invent a document or procedure.

## [status-and-other-processes] Status, registration and eligibility

Under section 4 of RA 7883, registration with the local health board of the city or municipality where the BHW serves is required for the Act’s benefits and incentives. It also provides for appropriate proof of accreditation. Individual benefits have further conditions; an invitation is insufficient grounds to promise an allowance.

Civil Service Commission Barangay Health Worker Eligibility has separate conditions and an application. BHW accreditation is not an automatic government appointment or a professional clinical licence. Later lessons cover benefits, eligibility and the accreditation process. Here, first understand the distinctions and the proper authority.
