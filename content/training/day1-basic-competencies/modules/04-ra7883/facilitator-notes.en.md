# Facilitator notes — Module 4: RA 7883: Benefits, Rights, Accreditation

## Timing

TESDA Training Regulations set **3 hours** for the competency *Develop
Life and Career Decisions* (facilitator-guide PDF 23).

Suggested split:

| Part | Time |
|---|---|
| Opening: "Have you ever been too shy to ask?" | 15 min |
| Legal basis and accreditation | 30 min |
| Benefits under RA 7883 | 30 min |
| BHW Eligibility (BHWE) in the Civil Service | 30 min |
| The registration process (local health board, committee) | 30 min |
| Exercise: following up calmly + CSC Form 101-H demo | 45 min |

## Delivery script (follows the lesson's sections)

1. **Open with Josie's question.** Ask the group: "Have you heard of a
   benefit you weren't sure applied to you too? Were you too shy to
   ask?" Use this to open before introducing RA 7883.
2. **On the legal-basis section,** emphasize that the basis for the
   work is not just "goodwill" — it is law. This is the detail most
   easily missed in a rush.
3. **On benefits,** read the list slowly and ask after each one: "Did
   you already know this? Have you received it?" — don't assume they
   already know all of it.
4. **On BHWE,** use the four conditions as a personal checklist each
   participant reviews — let them quietly assess themselves before
   discussing as a group.
5. **On the registration process,** remind them they aren't waiting
   alone — there's a system, and a responsible person or committee
   behind it.
6. **On the follow-up exercise,** turn it into a role-play: one
   participant plays a BHW following up, another plays local health
   board staff. Show the difference between a calm question and a
   complaint.
7. **If there's time,** show an actual CSC Form 101-H and demonstrate
   how to fill it in — this connects back to Module 1's section on
   documentation.

## Named misconception

**"I'm just a volunteer, so I shouldn't bring up benefits or money."**

This is the usual reason BHWs — especially new ones — don't ask for
what they're owed. Don't correct it by saying "you need to be
braver." Instead, go back to the legal basis: these benefits are
written into the law itself, not anyone's personal generosity — so
asking about them isn't greed, it's a right.

## Discussion prompts

- "Is there a benefit you weren't sure applied to you too? What was
  it?"
- "Have you ever waited a long time on a process with no follow-up?
  What happened?"
- "How would you explain to a new BHW why an accreditation process
  exists before they can start?"

## Answer key to the retrieval checks

1. **"Who accredits a BHW..."** → The local health board under RA 7883
   (not the midwife or a fellow BHW).
2. **"Which of the following is NOT required for BHWE..."** → A
   four-year college degree is not required — BHWE only asks for two
   years of college.
