# Facilitator notes — Module 1: The Roles of a BHW

## Timing

**6 hours**, per the TESDA Training Regulations for the *Participate in
Workplace Communication* competency (facilitator-guide PDF 19). This is
the second-largest module in the chapter.

A suggested split across the 6 hours:

| Part | Time |
|---|---|
| Opening activity: "What is your work?" | 30 min |
| The UHC shift and the HEPO umbrella | 45 min |
| Health Educator | 45 min |
| Community Organizer | 45 min |
| Health Service Provider | 60 min |
| Records and documents | 45 min |
| Practice: which role is this + own examples | 60 min |
| Summary and retrieval | 30 min |

At Normal (Karaniwan) density the readable content is only about 30-40
minutes' worth — the rest is discussion, demonstration and practice. At
Long (Detalyado), the LIPH and Wrong/Right sections come in as well.

## Delivery script (follows the lesson's sections)

1. **Open with the question, not the list.** Before showing any slide,
   ask the group: *"What actually is your work in the barangay? Let's go
   round."* Write the answers on manila paper. Let them fill it.
2. **Expect nearly every answer to be a service** — weighing,
   immunisation, referral, taking blood pressure. The Facilitator's
   Manual predicts exactly this, and *it is not wrong*. Do not correct
   it. Set the list aside and say you will come back to it.
3. **Introduce the UHC shift.** Explain that UHC's direction is primary
   health care and health promotion, so the BHW has a bigger part in
   promoting health — not only in delivering a service. Now go back to
   the manila paper: *"How many of the things we wrote are teaching? How
   many are organising?"* Usually few of either — that is the point of
   the whole module, and it lands harder because they found it
   themselves.
4. **On the hub-spoke visual (HEPO and the three roles),** point to each
   role as you name it — don't read the screen out. The visual is there
   to make the *shape* of the role memorable, not to be read aloud.
5. **After each of the three roles,** call on one participant for an
   example from their own barangay before moving on. Don't save all the
   examples for the end — the connection is lost by then.
6. **On Community Organizer,** press on the *barangay planning team for
   health*. Ask: *"Who here already sits on your barangay's planning
   team?"* Usually few hands. That is data, not failure — discuss why.
7. **On the records,** bring real blank household profile and master
   list forms as used in your city or municipality. Show the actual
   paper. This is also where the TESDA outcome "complete relevant
   work-related documents" belongs.
8. **On the "Which role is this?" practice,** run it as a group
   discussion before revealing any answer — ask *"What do you think?"*
   first.
9. **If running at Long density,** use the Wrong/Right section as a
   closing role-play: split the group in two, one acting out "wrong,"
   the other "right."

## Named misconception

**"Health promotion and organising are extra work they've given us — on
top of our real job."**

This is the commonest misconception in this module, and it is usually
said wearily rather than in objection. There is truth in where it comes
from: what is expected of a BHW genuinely has grown.

Do not correct it by telling them they are wrong. Go back instead to the
three roles in RA 7883 — that is 1995, older than the UHC Act. Teaching
and organising are not new work; they have been part of the role for
decades, and UHC only gave them a name (HEPO) and attention. The
question is not *"why have they added to us?"* but *"which of the three
is least used in my barangay so far?"*

## Discussion prompts

- "Of the three roles, which do you use most? Which least? Why might
  that be?"
- "Have you ever noticed a problem in the barangay and told no one
  because you thought it wasn't your job? What happened?"
- "What happens to the household profile after you hand it in? Do you
  know where it goes?"
- "If there were one thing a brand-new BHW should know about the work
  before starting, what would it be?"

## Answer key to the retrieval checks

1. **"Why is a BHW's role wider under the UHC Act?"** → Because UHC's
   direction is primary care and health promotion, so the role now
   includes teaching and organising, not only delivering services. (Not
   because of patient numbers, and nothing to do with a BHW's required
   schooling.)
2. **"Which role is BHW Lito performing when he guides the mother to a
   prenatal check-up?"** → Health Service Provider — he is the first
   contact the need met, and he is guiding her where to go. Had he
   taught the whole purok about prenatal care, that would be Health
   Educator.
3. **"Why must the household profile be accurate and up to date?"** →
   Because these data are what guide health programmes on where to go
   and what to prioritise — not because of inspections or the
   honorarium.

## Where the skills are taught (not here)

This module explains **what** the three roles are. When a participant
asks *how* to do each one well, point them at the right part of the
course (facilitator-guide PDF 20 item 7):

- Health-promotion skills → Core Competencies
- Community-organising skills → *Community Mobilization*, Common
  Competencies
- Communication and recording skills → Module 6 (Effective
  Communication) and Chapter III
