## [purpose] Aim

The BHW explains the three roles needed in one situation, follows the midwife's instruction, and gives a short handover: observed, actual action taken, and action still needed.

## [time-materials] Time and materials

Allow about 60 minutes for discussion and role-play; self-study takes about 10–15 minutes. Prepare the six slides, a blank practice notebook, three role cards, and the midwife's instruction. The Filipino or English animated story is optional; watching it is not required for online completion.

## [prepare] Prepare

Read both languages and revisit 1.1.1–1.1.5. Confirm the local contact, record, and handover route with the health team. Use fictional details for this fictional scenario. Do not diagnose from an environmental observation.

## [opening] Start with the instruction

Read: “Record what you saw, ask whether anyone wants to speak with the health team, and report the actions still needed to me.” Ask learners to identify the three requested actions. What needs clarification before acting?

## [steps] Discuss the six screens

1. **Observation:** What did Riza see, and what does she not yet know? Separate standing water from an unverified cause of illness.
2. **Educator:** Say the opening question to the family. Listen first and use a health team approved message.
3. **Organizer:** Invite people to a discussion. If the suggested time does not work, listen and ask for another time; do not present the activity as already agreed.
4. **Provider and record:** Confirm the contact and local route, explain the step, and record only the actual action. Revisit record selection in 1.1.5.
5. **Handover:** Identify observed, done, and needed. Compare the notebook, instruction, and report; is the recipient appropriate?
6. **Own performance:** Answer the online question, then explain the roles and speak a personal handover.

## [expected-answers] Respond to common answers

- **The report only records:** Ask whether the instruction to ask was addressed and what remains needed.
- **A diagnosis is claimed:** Ask: “Was that observed, or is it an assumption?” Move the concern into a question for the health team.
- **The BHW decided alone:** Return to listening to residents' timing and suggestions.
- **The handover is long:** Use observed, done, and needed as prompts, then try without the card.

## [misconception] Common misconception

Completing just one role does not address the whole instruction. Explain all three roles and what remains unfinished.

## [practice] Role-play and short report

Work in groups of three: BHW, resident, and midwife/observer. There is standing water in three yards; the resident has a question and wants to speak with the health team. The midwife supplies the instruction and a fictional contact. Ask the BHW to listen, invite potential partners, explain the confirmed step, make a short practice note, and report back. Rotate roles. Ask: “Which role did you use at each step?”

## [answer-key] Answer guidance

The first option is the complete report: observed, done, and needed. The second is incomplete; the third adds an unverified conclusion. In role-play, check that the record and report agree and that the BHW explains all three roles.

## [observe] Observe practical competence

- **Kaya na:** without prompts, explains the three roles and how the instruction was followed, gives all three handover parts, agrees with the record, and uses the appropriate recipient; no invented conclusion.
- **Kailangan pa ng practice:** the report is sound but misses a part or role; completes it after a question.
- **Hindi pa:** adds an unverified cause or diagnosis, or cannot separate observation, action, and need even with support.

Record observed performance separately from online completion. A correct answer or watching the video is not a practical assessment.

## [support] Support and further learning

Demonstrate the example handover and ask learners to identify its three parts. Try again with the card, then without it. Revisit 1.1.2–1.1.5 as needed. Continue communication in 1.6, community mobilization in Common Competencies, and health-promotion teaching in Core Competencies. Confirm currently available lessons before supplying links.
