## [purpose] Purpose

By the end of this short lesson the BHW can name the roles a single barangay situation calls for, and can give a short spoken handover to the midwife — what they observed, what they did, what is still needed — that follows her instruction and does not add a conclusion they cannot support.

## [time-materials] Time and materials

- **Time:** about 60 minutes in a face-to-face session (the "Practice: which role is this + own examples" part of the 6-hour allocation in the subchapter guide). The module's separate 30-minute summary and retrieval block is not part of these 60 minutes. Self-study of the lesson alone takes about 10–15 minutes.
- **Materials:** manila paper and markers; the lesson's slides (the standing-water scenario and the three-line handover: *Observed · Done · Needed*); the midwife's instruction from the lesson written large on manila paper; the three role cards from earlier lessons (*Educate*, *Organize*, *Guide*) if you used them.

## [prepare] Prepare

- Read the lesson in both Read and Slides so you know exactly what the BHW has seen.
- This lesson brings together the Health Educator, Community Organizer and Health Service Provider lessons and the records lesson. Skim their facilitator guides so you can point back to them.
- Know how handovers to the midwife actually happen in your area (in person, by phone, in a logbook) so the practice matches real life.
- Scope limits: this is an introduction, not proof of competence in every task. **Do not diagnose or let the group diagnose from an environmental observation** — standing water is an observation, not a proven cause of illness. Do not teach health-promotion or mobilization skills in depth; the lesson points these to later chapters (communication in subchapter 1.6 (Module 6, Effective Communication); community mobilization under Common Competencies; health-promotion teaching under Core Competencies), and Chapters II and III are not yet available in this prototype.

## [opening] Opening

Read the midwife's instruction aloud from the manila paper: *"Record what you saw, ask whether anyone wants to speak with the health team, and report the actions still needed to me."* Then ask: *"How many different things is she asking Marites to do? Let's count them."* Underline each one as the group names it.

## [steps] Steps

1. **One observation (Read "One observation, several actions", first slide).** Marites sees standing water in three yards. Ask: *"What did she see? What does she not know yet?"* Stress: read the instruction, clarify what is uncertain, and keep observation and assumption apart. No diagnosis from an environmental observation.
2. **Education (Read "Choose the education needed", second slide).** Ask: *"How would you open the conversation with the family, in your own words?"* Take two or three openings. Point to the lesson's example: *"What have you noticed about the water collecting here?"* — listen before explaining. Ask which role this is (Health Educator).
3. **Organizing (Read "Identify potential partners", third slide).** Ask: *"Who else would you talk to for a shared activity?"* → residents, purok leader, council member, health staff. Ask: *"Why shouldn't the BHW just decide for everyone?"* Ask which role this is (Community Organizer).
4. **Handover (Read "Prepare a short handover", fourth slide).** Show the three lines: *Observed · Done · Needed.* Read the lesson's example handover aloud. Ask: *"Which part is the observation? Which is what she did? Which is what's still needed?"* Point out that it shares only relevant information with the right person and matches what was recorded.
5. **Own examples.** Ask two or three participants for a situation from their own barangay where one observation needed more than one role. Ask the group: *"Which roles were used?"*
6. **Next learning (Read "Continue developing your skills", fifth slide).** Say plainly that this subchapter introduces the roles and does not prove competence in every task; the skills are developed later, and practical competence is observed separately.
7. **Self-check.** Read the check question and let the group discuss before revealing the answer.

## [expected-answers] Expected answers and how to respond

- **The handover includes a cause** ("the water is making the children sick"). Acknowledge the concern for the children, then ask: *"Did you see that, or is it what you think? What did you actually see?"* Move the worry into the "Needed" line as a question for the midwife.
- **"That's only a Service Provider situation."** Accept it, then ask: *"Was there teaching in it? Was anyone else brought in?"* One situation can use several roles.
- **A very long report.** Thank them for being thorough, then ask them to say it again in three lines: observed, done, needed.
- **"Nothing more to do — I already told the family."** Ask: *"What did the midwife ask you to report back?"* The instruction included reporting actions still needed.

## [misconception] Common misconception

**"A good report tells the midwife what the problem is — the more I conclude, the more helpful I am."** BHWs want to be useful, and giving an answer can feel more useful than giving an observation. Correct it without embarrassing anyone by using the check's wrong option ("Everyone there is certainly ill"), not a participant's words: ask the group what the midwife could do with that sentence versus "Water in three yards; recorded; I need guidance on the next step." A useful handover separates what was seen, what was done and what is needed, and leaves the conclusion to the health team.

## [practice] Practice activity

In pairs, one BHW plays the midwife and one plays Marites. "Marites" gives a spoken handover of the standing-water situation in three lines — observed, done, still needed — then says which roles she used and how she followed the instruction. "The midwife" may ask one clarifying question, which "Marites" must answer without inventing anything. Swap roles, this time using a situation from the BHW's own barangay (no residents' names). Watch each BHW's spoken handover; do not rely on written notes.

## [answer-key] Self-check answer key

The lesson has one check. It appears at the end of Read ("Apply your learning") and again on the last slide; it is the same question, so it is covered once here.

- **"Which report follows the instruction without inventing a conclusion?"** → **Water in three yards; recorded; I need guidance on the next step.** It separates the observation, the action taken and the remaining need.
  - *Everyone there is certainly ill* is wrong: it invents a conclusion from an environmental observation. Standing water is not a diagnosis or a proven cause of illness.
  - *Nothing else needs to be done* is wrong: the midwife asked for the actions still needed, and the BHW cannot close the situation on their own.
- Remind the group of the feedback line: choosing an answer is not a practical assessment. The spoken handover in the practice activity is.

## [observe] What to observe

Use the lesson's observation indicator — *explain the roles needed in a situation and give a short handover following an instruction* — during the practice activity:

- **Kaya na:** without prompting, the BHW gives a handover with all three parts (observed, done, needed), adds no invented conclusion, names the roles used, and explains how they followed the midwife's instruction.
- **Kailangan pa ng practice:** the BHW gives a sound handover but misses one part or the roles, and completes it after you ask (for example, *"And what is still needed?"* or *"Which roles did you use?"*).
- **Hindi pa:** the BHW states a cause or diagnosis as fact, or reports that nothing more is needed, and cannot separate observation, action and need even with prompts.

Observing this in the room is separate from the BHW finishing the online self-check. A correct self-check answer is not evidence of the skill.

## [support] If a BHW needs more support

- Give them the lesson's example handover and ask them to point to the observed, done and needed parts before trying their own.
- Let them use the three words *Observed · Done · Needed* on a card for one more try, then without it.
- If they mix up the roles, send them back to the "One morning, many tasks" example in The BHW and HEPO lesson.
- Re-observe at the next session with the same standing-water situation, spoken aloud, not written.
