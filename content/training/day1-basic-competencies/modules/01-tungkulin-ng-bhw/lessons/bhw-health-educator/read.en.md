## [educator-scene] Start with your audience

During her discussion, Marites does more than read a poster. She first learns what residents are asking, then explains clearly. As a Health Educator, she helps people gain knowledge to care for their bodies and their environment.

## [body-environment] Body and environment

Health education includes the body and its environment. A discussion may address access to health services and cleanliness of the surroundings, using materials approved by the health team. This lesson concerns choosing a topic and audience; detailed health advice belongs in the relevant training.

## [life-stages] Needs change through life

Education continues through every life stage. An infant’s caregiver, a young person, a pregnant resident, and an older adult have different questions. Discussions of illness, accidents, and risks must fit their life stage. For example, a conversation with a young person has a different context from one with an infant’s caregiver.

## [all-sectors] Reach every community group

Education must reach beyond people who already attend a health talk. Consider who is missing: children, young people, older adults, and other groups in the barangay. Choose a time, place, language, and format that support participation. Offer information, knowledge, and opportunities to practise.
