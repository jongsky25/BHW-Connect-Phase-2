## [purpose] Purpose

By the end of this short lesson the BHW can pick a health-education topic and way of teaching that fits a specific audience — its community group and its life stage — and can say who in the barangay is usually left out.

## [time-materials] Time and materials

- **Time:** about 45 minutes in a face-to-face session (the Health Educator part of the 6-hour allocation in the subchapter guide). Self-study of the lesson alone takes about 10 minutes.
- **Materials:** manila paper and markers; the lesson's slides (the audience and life-stage visual); one approved IEC material your RHU already uses (a poster or leaflet) to show what "approved material" means.

## [prepare] Prepare

- Read the lesson in both Read and Slides so you know exactly what the BHW has seen.
- Know the barangay's groups: children, young people, pregnant residents, older adults, persons with disability, workers who are away during the day. Have one real example of a group your barangay's health talks rarely reach.
- This lesson is about **choosing the topic and the audience**, not about the health content itself. Do not teach clinical advice here; point to the relevant training instead.

## [opening] Opening

Ask the group: *"Think of the last health talk in your barangay. Who was in the room? Who was not?"* Write both lists on manila paper. Leave the "who was not" list up for the whole lesson.

## [steps] Steps

1. **Scene (Read "Start with your audience", first slide).** Point out that Marites first listens to what residents are asking, then explains. Ask: *"What did she do before she talked?"*
2. **Body and environment.** Explain that health education covers both the person and their surroundings — services, cleanliness, safety. Show the approved IEC material and say that BHWs teach with approved materials from the health team.
3. **Life stages.** Use the life-stage visual. For each stage (infant's caregiver, young person, pregnant resident, older adult) ask the group for one question that person might have. Show that the same topic is discussed differently for each.
4. **Every community group.** Return to the "who was not" list from the opening. Ask which of these the lesson calls sectors and life stages.
5. **Choose time, place, language, format.** For one missing group, ask the group to change one thing — the time, the place, the language or the format — that would help that group attend.
6. **Self-check.** Give the check scenario (only parents attend) and let the group discuss before revealing the answer.

## [expected-answers] Expected answers and how to respond

- **"Nobody comes to our talks anyway."** This is common and honest. Do not argue. Ask: *"When is that group free? Where are they already gathered?"* Move the conversation to changing the time or place.
- **They list only mothers and pregnant residents.** Accept it, then ask about young people, older adults and men. The point is who is missing, not who is wrong.
- **They answer with clinical advice** (for example, which medicine to give). Acknowledge the knowledge, then bring it back to the audience: *"Good — and how would you explain that to a 16-year-old compared to a grandmother?"*

## [misconception] Common misconception

**"Health education means giving a talk to whoever shows up."** A talk is only one format, and the people who show up are usually the same people. Correct it without embarrassing anyone: ask who the BHW has talked to one-on-one this week — a conversation at a house visit is health education too. Teaching is a purposeful conversation fitted to the listener.

## [practice] Practice activity

In pairs, each BHW chooses one group their barangay's health talks rarely reach. They prepare a 1-minute opening for that group: the time and place they would choose, the first question they would ask, and why it fits that group's life stage. Two or three pairs present; the group gives one strength and one suggestion.

## [answer-key] Self-check answer key

- **"Only parents attend. How can you better reach young people?"** → **Adapt the time and discussion to young people's questions.** Repeating the same talk to parents only does not reach the young people, and assuming they already know enough skips them entirely. Remind the group to keep the information accurate and approved.

## [observe] What to observe

Use the lesson's observation indicator during the practice activity:

- **Kaya na:** the BHW names a missing group, adapts time or place and the opening question to that group's life stage, and explains why without prompting.
- **Kailangan pa ng practice:** the BHW adapts the talk but only explains why after you ask a follow-up question.
- **Hindi pa:** the BHW repeats the same talk for every audience, or cannot say who is missing.

Observing this in the room is separate from the BHW finishing the online self-check. A correct self-check answer is not evidence of the skill.

## [support] If a BHW needs more support

- Pair them with a BHW who rated **Kaya na** for the practice activity and let them present second.
- Give them a narrower choice: pick one of two groups (young people or older adults) and change only the time or place.
- Re-observe at the next session using the same practice scenario, not a new one.
