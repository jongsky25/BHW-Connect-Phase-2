## [purpose] Purpose

By the end of this short lesson the BHW can sort examples of their own work into teaching, organizing and service support, and can explain in their own words how the three work together under the HEPO role.

## [time-materials] Time and materials

- **Time:** about 45 minutes in a face-to-face session (the "UHC shift and the HEPO umbrella" part of the 6-hour allocation in the subchapter guide). If this is the first lesson of the day, the module's 30-minute opening activity ("What is your work?") runs just before it and is not part of these 45 minutes. Self-study of the lesson alone takes about 10 minutes.
- **Materials:** manila paper and markers; the lesson's slides (the "One morning, many tasks" timeline and the HEPO visual with the three roles); three strips of paper or cards labelled *Educate*, *Organize*, *Guide*.

## [prepare] Prepare

- Read the lesson in both Read and Slides so you know exactly what the BHW has seen.
- Know your barangay's priority health programs and problems well enough to name one or two. The lesson says a BHW needs adequate knowledge of these before teaching; you should be able to model it.
- Know who in your RHU or health center the BHWs confirm uncertain information with, so you can name that person when the "Prepare before sharing" section comes up.
- Scope limits: this lesson introduces the three roles; the next lessons go into each one. Do not teach how to organize or how to teach here. HEPO is a role description in the curriculum — **do not present it as an official title, an appointment or a reporting line**, and do not show the visual as an organizational chart.

## [opening] Opening

If the module opening activity has already been done, point to that manila paper. If not, ask: *"What did you actually do in your barangay last week? Let's go round — one task each."* Write every answer down without correcting anything. Most answers will be services (weighing, referral, taking blood pressure). That is expected and not wrong. Say: *"We'll come back to this list."*

## [steps] Steps

1. **One morning (Read "One morning, many tasks", first slide).** Walk through Marites's 8:00, 10:00 and 11:00. Ask: *"Was that three jobs, or one job?"* Point out that one BHW did all three, and that the curriculum describes UHC as strengthening primary care and health promotion, so the role reaches beyond service support.
2. **Back to the list.** Return to the manila paper. Ask: *"How many of our tasks are teaching? How many are organizing?"* Usually few of either. Let the group notice this themselves.
3. **Act with the community (Read "Act with the community", second slide).** Explain that services matter when people need them, and alongside that the BHW teaches and invites people to take part. Ask: *"In Marites's clean-up drive, where do you see teaching, and where do you see residents taking part?"*
4. **HEPO (Read "HEPO: promoting health", HEPO slide).** Point to each of the three roles as you name it — do not read the screen aloud. Say plainly that the lines show connected tasks, not who reports to whom. Ask: *"Which of the three do you do most? Which least?"*
5. **Prepare before sharing (Read "Prepare before sharing", fourth slide).** Ask: *"What do you do when a resident asks something you are not sure about?"* Bring out that the BHW confirms with the appropriate health worker and uses approved materials — helping does not require guessing.
6. **Self-check.** Read the clean-up drive scenario and let the group discuss before revealing the answer. Stress the feedback line: more than one role can appear in the same activity.

## [expected-answers] Expected answers and how to respond

- **Only service tasks on the list.** This is what the Facilitator Guide predicts, and it is not wrong. Do not correct it. Ask: *"When you did that, did you also explain anything to the family?"* Often there was teaching inside the service.
- **"HEPO is a new position / will we get an appointment?"** Answer honestly: in this lesson HEPO is the term the Reference Manual uses to describe the BHW's role in sharing health information. It is not proof of an appointment. Refer questions about status or benefits to the module on the BHW Benefits and Incentives Act.
- **"A task can only be one role."** Accept the sorting, then ask: *"Could this same task also be another role?"* The lesson says the roles are not exclusive.
- **"If I don't know, I'll just say what I think."** Acknowledge the wish to help, then bring it back: *"Who could you check with first?"*

## [misconception] Common misconception

**"Health promotion and organizing are extra work they've given us — on top of our real job."** This is usually said wearily, and there is truth in where it comes from: what is expected of a BHW has grown. Do not tell anyone they are wrong. Go back instead to the three roles in RA 7883 (1995), which is older than the UHC Act. Teaching and organizing are not new work; UHC gave them a name (HEPO) and attention. Change the question from *"Why have they added to us?"* to *"Which of the three is least used in my barangay so far?"*

## [practice] Practice activity

In small groups, each BHW gives one real task from their own last month. The group places it under *Educate*, *Organize* or *Guide* (service support) and the BHW explains why. Then the group asks: *"Could this task also be one of the other roles? How?"* Each group presents one task that turned out to use two or three roles.

## [answer-key] Self-check answer key

The lesson has one check. It appears at the end of Read ("Apply your learning") and again on the last slide; it is the same question, so it is covered once here.

- **"Marites plans a clean-up drive with residents. Which role is most visible?"** → **Community Organizer.** She is connecting people and encouraging them to act together.
  - *Health Service Provider* is wrong: she is not meeting an individual's need or guiding someone to a health worker in this activity.
  - *Health Educator only* is wrong because of the word "only": there may be teaching inside the clean-up drive, but planning with residents is organizing. The roles are not exclusive.

## [observe] What to observe

Use the lesson's observation indicator — *explain how education, organizing and service support work together in the BHW role* — during the practice activity:

- **Kaya na:** the BHW sorts their own task correctly and, without prompting, explains how it links to at least one other role (for example, a referral visit that also included teaching).
- **Kailangan pa ng practice:** the BHW sorts the task correctly but only explains the link to another role after you ask a follow-up question.
- **Hindi pa:** the BHW cannot place their task in a role, or insists every task belongs to exactly one role and cannot explain the connection even with prompts.

Observing this in the room is separate from the BHW finishing the online self-check. A correct self-check answer is not evidence of the skill.

## [support] If a BHW needs more support

- Go back to Marites's morning and ask them to name the role at 8:00, 10:00 and 11:00 before trying their own task again.
- Give them one of their own service tasks from the opening list and ask only: *"Did you explain anything to the family while doing this?"* — one link at a time.
- Pair them with a BHW rated **Kaya na** for the next sorting round.
- Re-observe during the later "Which role is this?" practice in the module (the Apply the Roles lesson), using a task from their own barangay.
