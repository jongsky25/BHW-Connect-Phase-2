## [morning] One morning, many tasks

At eight, BHW Marites leads a health discussion in the purok. At ten, she talks with the council member about a clean-up drive. At eleven, she helps Aling Nena contact the midwife about her child’s needs. One BHW does all three. The curriculum describes UHC as strengthening primary care and health promotion, so the role extends beyond service support.

## [prevention] Act with the community

Services matter when people need them. Alongside this, the BHW teaches and encourages people to join health activities. In Marites’s clean-up drive, information is shared and residents help plan and act. Education, participation, and access to services are connected.

## [hepo] HEPO: promoting health

The Reference Manual uses the term barangay-level Health Education and Promotion Officer, or HEPO. It describes the BHW’s role in sharing health information. This role is expressed through being a Health Educator, Community Organizer, and Health Service Provider. The visual connects related tasks; it is not an official organizational chart or proof of an appointment.

## [knowledge] Prepare before sharing

Marites needs adequate knowledge of the barangay’s priority health programs and problems. Before teaching, she makes sure she understands the information. When a detail is uncertain, she confirms it with the appropriate health worker and uses approved materials. Helping does not require guessing an answer.
