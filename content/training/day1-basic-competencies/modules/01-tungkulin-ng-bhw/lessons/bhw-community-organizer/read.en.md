## [organizer-scene] From observation to conversation

BHW Marites notices standing water in three yards. She asks residents what they have noticed and who else should join the discussion. She also approaches the purok leader and health staff. As a Community Organizer, she connects people before they decide on a shared activity.

## [participation] Invite people to participate

Marites says, “I noticed standing water in three yards. Could we meet with the purok leader and health staff to discuss what we might do? When could you join us?” A resident suggests a different time. Marites listens before they choose a time together. Organizing gives residents a chance to shape the activity, rather than only attend it.

## [observation] Report what was seen

Marites writes, “There is standing water in three yards.” That is what she saw. She does not write that the water caused children to become ill; she has not established that. She shares the specific observation and residents’ concern with health staff so they can discuss an appropriate next step.

## [planning-team] Bring observations into planning

With residents and health staff, Marites identifies who will bring the observation to the barangay planning team for health. They record the proposed action and who will report back to residents. This brings what people saw into planning and lets them know what happened next. Confirm the actual process with your local health team.

## [liph] Contribute to wider planning

Barangay input can help identify health needs discussed for the city or provincial Local Investment Plan for Health, or LIPH. Share a proposal through the existing local process with the health team. Bringing a suggestion into planning does not mean it will automatically receive funding.
