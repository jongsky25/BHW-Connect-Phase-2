## [organizer-scene] From observation to conversation

Marites notices standing water in some yards. She talks with residents, the purok leader, the health council member, and health staff about a possible shared activity. A Community Organizer maintains good relationships and communication with the community and city or municipal health staff.

## [participation] Invite people to participate

Marites does more than announce a clean-up drive. She asks who can join, what they can contribute, and which schedule is possible. A clear invitation names an action, includes partners, and allows people to respond. Residents participate rather than simply watch.

## [planning-team] Bring observations into planning

The manual describes the BHW as part of the barangay planning team for health. The BHW brings relevant observations to help leaders understand and act on a problem. Separate what was seen from an assumption: “There is standing water in three yards” is an observation; it does not prove the cause of an illness.

## [liph] Contribute to wider planning

The Local Investment Plan for Health, or LIPH, is the city or provincial health plan described in the manual. It recognizes barangay and municipal contributions to planning. Observations shared by BHWs can inform discussion of local needs. Contribute through the existing local process with the health team; a proposal does not automatically receive funding.
