## [record-purpose] After the visit

After speaking with Aling Nena, BHW Marites returns to the health station. She has a fictional note about the conversation, but she will not put it into just any list. She asks the midwife, “What information is needed, and what will it be used for?” Accurate records help the health team understand community needs and plan programs. Every example in this lesson is fictional; it contains no real personal data or official form.

## [household-profile] What describes the household?

Marites first looks at the household profile. In the fictional example, code HH-014 stands for one household with four members. This helps describe the household. It does not answer who has already received a service. Use the approved local form and instructions for actual fields and safekeeping.

## [master-list] Who belongs to the group?

Next, Marites looks at a master list for a group, such as older residents in the neighborhood. The manual describes a master list as grouping people by category. It helps answer who belongs to that group. Being listed there does not establish that someone received health teaching or another service.

## [registry] Who received a service?

“Who has received health teaching?” asks the midwife. Marites checks the registry: the manual describes it as the record of people who received a service. She checks the recorded service before answering. If an entry is unclear, she clarifies it with the health team rather than inferring an answer from the master list or adding a guessed entry.

## [assigned-form] Clarify before handing it over

There is also an assigned form with the fictional label “recipient: midwife.” A nurse, midwife, or doctor may assign a government, DOH, or local-government form. Before handing it over, Marites checks what information is requested, whether it matches the verified record, and who should receive it. She does not guess missing details. She follows the approved local method for safeguarding and submitting it; this example is neither a real form nor a universal rule for where every record goes.
