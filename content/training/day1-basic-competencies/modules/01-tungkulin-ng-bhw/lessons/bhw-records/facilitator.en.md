## [purpose] Purpose

By the end of this short lesson the BHW can match four kinds of record — household profile, master list, registry and an assigned form — to the question each one answers, and can say who should receive the information on an assigned form.

## [time-materials] Time and materials

- **Time:** about 45 minutes in a face-to-face session (the "Records and documents" part of the 6-hour allocation in the subchapter guide). Self-study of the lesson alone takes about 10 minutes.
- **Materials:** the lesson's slides (the fictional record examples); real **blank** household profile and master list forms as used in your city or municipality; four cards per group showing the fictional examples from the lesson (household HH-014 with four members; master list "group: older adults"; registry "service provided: health teaching"; assigned form "recipient: midwife"); a card with the four record names for matching.

## [prepare] Prepare

- Read the lesson in both Read and Slides so you know exactly what the BHW has seen.
- Get blank copies of the forms your area actually uses. **Bring blank forms only. Never bring or show a filled-in form with real patient or household information,** and do not let participants use real names in the activity.
- Find out locally where each record goes after the BHW hands it in, and who assigns forms in your health center (nurse, midwife or doctor). BHWs will ask. If you don't know for a form, say so and name who to ask.
- Scope limits: this lesson is about **what each record is for and who receives it**, not how to fill in each field. Actual fields and storage follow the approved local form and instructions. Filling-in and recording skills come later (Module 6, Effective Communication, and Chapter III).

## [opening] Opening

Hold up a blank household profile form and ask: *"What happens to this after you hand it in? Do you know where it goes?"* Let several people answer. Write the answers on manila paper without correcting them. Say: *"Let's find out what each record is for."*

## [steps] Steps

1. **What records are for (Read "What is the record used for?", first slide).** BHWs help collect and safeguard information from households. Ask: *"Who uses this information after you?"* Bring out that accurate records inform program planning and help identify needs. Stress that every document in the lesson is a fictional learning example.
2. **Household profile (Read "Household profile: one household", second slide).** Show the HH-014 example next to your blank local form. Ask: *"What question does this record answer?"* → What describes this household? Point out that the actual fields follow the approved local form.
3. **Master list or registry (Read "Master list or registry?", third slide).** Put the two fictional examples side by side. Ask: *"One is 'group: older adults', the other is 'service provided: health teaching'. Which tells you who belongs to a group, and which tells you who received a service?"*
4. **Assigned forms (Read "Follow the form instructions", fourth slide).** Explain that a nurse, midwife or doctor may assign government, DOH or local-government forms. Point to "recipient: midwife". Ask: *"Before you fill in a form like this, what two things do you clarify?"* → the required information and the recipient. Then: *"What do you do if a detail is missing?"* → do not guess; check against the information collected.
5. **Back to the opening.** Return to the "where does it go" list. Add what you found out locally.
6. **Self-check.** Read the check question and let the group discuss before revealing the answer.

## [expected-answers] Expected answers and how to respond

- **"We submit it because it's required / for the honorarium / for inspection."** Accept it without judgment — these are real pressures. Then ask: *"And after that, what does the health team use it for?"* The lesson's answer is that these data guide programs on where to go and what to prioritise.
- **"I don't know where it goes."** Very common and honest. Thank them, then share what you found out locally, or name who to ask.
- **"The master list and the registry are the same."** See Common misconception.
- **"If I don't know a detail, I'll fill in what's most likely."** Acknowledge the wish to finish the form, then bring it back: *"If the record is wrong, the plan built on it will be wrong. What could you do instead?"* → leave it, check, and ask.

## [misconception] Common misconception

**"The master list and the registry are the same list of people."** Both are lists of names, so they easily blur together. Correct it without embarrassing anyone by using the lesson's fictional examples, not a participant's answer: ask *"If the midwife wants to know which older adults in the purok there are, which do you open? If she wants to know who already received health teaching, which do you open?"* A master list groups people; a registry records who received a service. They answer different questions.

## [practice] Practice activity

In groups of three, give each group the four fictional example cards and the card with the four record names. The group matches each example to its record name and to the question it answers (*What describes this household? Who belongs to the group? Who received a service? What must go to which recipient?*). Then each BHW picks one card and explains aloud to the group what the record is for and who should receive the information. Walk round and listen to each BHW's explanation.

## [answer-key] Self-check answer key

The lesson has one check. It appears at the end of Read ("Apply your learning") and again on the last slide; it is the same question, so it is covered once here.

- **"Which record answers 'who has received a service'?"** → **Registry.** The manual describes a registry as the record of people who received a service.
  - *Household profile* is wrong: it describes one household, not who was served.
  - *Group master list* is wrong: a master list groups people (for example, older adults); it does not show who received a service.
  - Remind the group to follow the names and purposes of their approved local forms.

## [observe] What to observe

Use the lesson's observation indicator — *match four record types to their purposes and explain who should receive relevant information* — during the practice activity:

- **Kaya na:** without prompting, the BHW matches the card to the right record and question, and explains who receives the information (for the assigned form: the recipient written on it, here the midwife).
- **Kailangan pa ng practice:** the BHW matches correctly but only explains the purpose or the recipient after you ask (for example, *"And who does this go to?"*).
- **Hindi pa:** the BHW mixes up the master list and the registry, or cannot say who receives the information, even with prompts.

Observing this in the room is separate from the BHW finishing the online self-check. A correct self-check answer is not evidence of the skill.

## [support] If a BHW needs more support

- Reduce to two cards first: master list and registry only. Ask the one question each answers. Then add the household profile and the assigned form.
- Put the blank local form next to the fictional card so they can see the real layout.
- Pair them with a BHW rated **Kaya na** and let them explain second.
- Re-observe at the next session with the same four cards, not new ones.
