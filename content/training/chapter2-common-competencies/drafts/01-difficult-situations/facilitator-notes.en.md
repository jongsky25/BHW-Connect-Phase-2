# Facilitator notes — Subchapter 2.1: Responding to difficult situations

## Timing

**10 hours** (source allocation, facilitator-guide PDF 33), used in full by the four lessons.

| Part | Lesson | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| 2.1.1 | Find out the concern | 120 |
| 2.1.2 | Listen and clarify calmly | 180 |
| 2.1.3 | Agree on the next step | 180 |
| 2.1.4 | Report and reflect | 120 |
| **Total** | | **600** |

Split across two days (2.1.1–2.1.2, then 2.1.3–2.1.4); breaks are extra. For 30 participants, form 10 triads rotating BHW, resident and observer over three rounds, with enough staff to observe everyone — add rounds rather than skip anyone. App reading time is not this face-to-face time. Completion is not NC II certification.

## Before the session

- **Confirm local facts one week before:** the midwife or assigned supervisor, the emergency contact, the authorized reporting channel, and a safe meeting place. Write them into the blank lines of the job aid.
- **Print, one set per triad:** participant cards for 2.1.1 *Find out the concern*, 2.1.2 *Listen and clarify calmly*, 2.1.3 *Agree on the next step* and 2.1.4 *Report and reflect*; the four matching observation sheets; and the job aid *A respectful conversation*.
- **Props:** manila paper, markers, blank action cards and handover sheets. No projector or internet needed.
- **Staffing:** one staff observer per three or four triads.
- **Scope limits:** fictional cases only. Resident players must not introduce a medical emergency. Do not teach forcing entry, guaranteeing a schedule, or promising services outside the BHW's control. For immediate danger, the answer is to get help safely through the local emergency service, then report — confirm the route with the midwife / RHU / local protocol.

## Delivery script

1. **Open the subchapter with their own experience** (F33). Ask: *"Tell us about a time a resident was angry, refused a visit, or did not take you seriously. What did you do?"* Write short answers on manila paper. Do not correct anyone yet. Say: *"Over these sessions we will plan, listen, agree and report. Let's see which of these stories each step would have helped."*
2. **2.1.1 Find out the concern.** Case: BHW Amina arrives and Rosa asks, "Why do you need our names again?" while neighbors listen. Ask: *"What do we know about Rosa's concern so far?"* Run the **plan card** practice: purpose, opening question, permission request, support contact. Link: *"Amina now has a plan. What happens when the person is already upset?"*
3. **2.1.2 Listen and clarify calmly.** Case: Liza raises her voice at BHW Jun in a queue: "You keep coming back. Nothing happens!" Ask: *"What would help Jun hear what happened, without arguing?"* Run the **listening role-play** in triads. Link: *"Once we understand the concern, how do we agree what happens next?"*
4. **2.1.3 Agree on the next step.** Case: Ben cannot go to the health center in the morning because of work; BHW Salma does not yet know if another schedule exists. Ask: *"What can Salma honestly offer?"* Run the **action card** practice: two realistic options, Ben chooses, confirm action, who acts and when. Link: *"Someone else now needs to know. What do we pass on, and what do we keep?"*
5. **2.1.4 Report and reflect.** Case: BHW Datu listened to Elena, who asked who could see her household form. Ask: *"What goes in the handover, and what stays out?"* Run the **handover and reflection** practice: a three-line handover, partner check for assumptions, one change for next time.
6. **Close with retrieval.** Go back to the manila paper from step 1. For each story, ask: *"Which step would you do differently now: plan, listen, agree or report?"* Each participant names one sentence from the job aid they will use.

For per-lesson detail, use the lesson guide.

## Named misconception

**"If the resident is difficult, the problem is the resident."**

People hold this honestly: meeting the same refusal again, sometimes in front of neighbors, is tiring, and a label feels like an explanation.

Do not say they are wrong. Point to the lessons instead: Rosa's "Why our names again?" turned out to be a privacy concern; Liza had gone twice and did not know whom to speak to; Ben had a work schedule, not a lack of interest. Ask: *"In each case, what did we learn once we asked?"* Then show that the handover for Elena does not call her "difficult." The shift is from *"what is wrong with her?"* to *"what is her concern, and what can I do about it?"*

## Discussion prompts

- *"When someone raises their voice at you, what is the first thing you usually feel like saying? What could you say instead?"*
- *"Where in your barangay could you move a conversation so it is more private and safe?"*
- *"Have you ever promised something to calm a resident down that you could not deliver? What happened next?"*
- *"What should never go into a group chat about a resident?"*

## Observing competence

The Competency tab has one indicator per lesson objective (four in all), each with *Kaya na / Kailangan pa ng practice / Hindi pa* (Ready / Needs practice / Not yet). Use it with the matching activity card and observation sheet.

1. **Who observes:** only staff give ratings. Peer feedback is practice, not evidence.
2. **Rounds:** each learner plays the BHW at least once per lesson; three rounds per triad rotates everyone.
3. **Record only what you saw and heard.** Write the words the learner used. Leave the rating blank if you did not observe it.
4. **Needs practice:** give one specific next action (for example, *"Ask permission before explaining the form"*) and have them repeat that part right away, with the resident changing the wording.
5. **Not yet:** repeat the model dialogue and give a new round the same day if time allows; otherwise re-observe at the follow-up practice with the midwife within 2–4 weeks.
6. A correct answer on the in-app checks or a quiz score is not evidence of the skill. Only observed performance is rated.

## Where this connects

- **Chapter 1, Effective Communication** and **Identifying Problems and Planning Solutions** — this subchapter applies open questions, summarizing and action planning when the conversation is hard.
- **Chapter 1, Policies at the Barangay Health Station** and **Subchapter 2.2, Quality service** — health-team rules and reviewing one's own work (F33 related topics; taught there, not here).
- **Job aid:** each BHW leaves with *A respectful conversation*, with local contacts filled in.
- **Transfer task:** within 2–4 weeks, each BHW practises a conversation and handover with the midwife using a fictional case. The midwife observes with the same indicators and gives feedback and a retry. Recall checks are specified for days 2, 14 and 60 (no reminders scheduled).
