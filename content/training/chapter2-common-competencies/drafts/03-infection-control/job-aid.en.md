# Infection prevention and control

## Recognize routes of transmission

Direct contact involves transfer through direct touch. Indirect contact involves a contaminated object or hands. Infectious respiratory particles may be inhaled or land directly on a nearby person’s eyes, nose or mouth. A touch or cough alone does not prove infection.

Other cards show fecal contamination of food or water: fecal-oral spread; an infected mosquito bite: vector-borne spread; a contaminated needle: possible blood exposure. ## Demonstrate hand hygiene

Wet hands and apply soap. Rub palm against palm, then each hand’s back with fingers interlaced. Clean between fingers and rub their backs against the opposite palm. Rotate around each thumb. Rub fingertips and nails against the opposite palm. Work on both hands.

Rinse and dry with a single-use towel. Use the towel to close a manual tap. The complete healthcare wash takes 40–60 seconds. When handrub is appropriate and hands are not visibly dirty, apply enough to cover all surfaces. Rub the same surfaces until dry: 20–30 seconds for the complete handrub. Do not rinse or wipe it off.

## Demonstrate cough etiquette

Cover nose and mouth with a tissue when coughing or sneezing. Without tissue, use the inside of a bent elbow rather than hands. Discard used tissue in the designated bin. Clean hands after contact with respiratory secretions. Report symptoms to staff and follow their instructions.

## Prevent diarrhea through safer surroundings

For food: keep hands, utensils and surfaces clean. Separate raw and cooked food. Cook thoroughly. Keep food at safe temperatures instead of leaving it exposed. Use safe water and ingredients. Wash produce with safe water. Protect food from insects and rodents.

Mila chooses to separate raw and cooked food. She will cover stored water and avoid dipping hands or cups into it. Clear appearance does not establish safety. They will confirm a safe source with health or water staff. The household will use a toilet and provide soap and water for washing.
