# Facilitator notes — Subchapter 2.3: Infection prevention and control

## Timing

**10 hours** (600 minutes), the source allocation for this common competency (Facilitator Guide PDF page 35).

| Part | Lesson | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| 2.3.1 | Recognize routes of transmission | 120 |
| 2.3.2 | Choose controls at several levels | 60 |
| 2.3.3 | Demonstrate hand hygiene | 120 |
| 2.3.4 | Demonstrate cough etiquette | 90 |
| 2.3.5 | Prevent diarrhea through safer surroundings | 90 |
| 2.3.6 | Find BHS IPC policies | 60 |
| 2.3.7 | Monitor and improve IPC practice | 60 |
| **Total** | | **600** |

The per-lesson split is an authoring proposal that keeps the ten-hour total. Run it over two days: 2.3.1–2.3.3 on day one, 2.3.4–2.3.7 on day two (300 minutes each). Breaks are extra. Work in rotating triads (learner, partner, observer); 30 participants means 10 triads, so plan enough staff or later rounds to observe everyone. App reading time is not this face-to-face time, and completion is not NC II certification.

## Before the session

- **Confirm local facts:** the IPC contact, the reporting channel for symptoms and gaps, the exposure contact and process, the water advisory or contact, and the sanitary inspector. Write them on the job aid's blank lines; if unknown, write "confirm with the midwife / RHU / local protocol".
- **Print, one set per triad:** the participant cards, worksheet and observation sheet for each lesson — *2.3.1 Possible exposure cards*, *2.3.2 Layered control map*, *2.3.3 Hand-hygiene return demonstration*, *2.3.4 Cough-etiquette demonstration*, *2.3.5 Food, water and sanitation review*, *2.3.6 Policy and training inventory*, *2.3.7 IPC improvement card* — and one copy of the job aid *Infection prevention and control* per participant.
- **Props:** running water, soap, single-use towels, alcohol-based handrub and a timer per hand-hygiene station; tissues and a bin; a mock household corner; a sample folder with old pages for 2.3.6.
- **Staffing:** at least one staff observer per two to three triads, more for the hand-hygiene demonstration.
- **Scope limits:** clean props only. No live sharps, body fluids, hot-water or chemical demonstrations. Do not teach chlorine amounts, boiling times, septic-tank distances, isolation arrangements, respirator fitting or any diagnosis. Reading a policy does not authorize a specialist task.

## Delivery script

1. **Open the subchapter (about 10 minutes, taken from 2.3.1's practice time).** Ask: *"Tell us about a time you worried you might catch or spread something while doing your BHW work. What did you do?"* Listen; do not correct yet. Say: *"Over two days we will practise what to do — and who to tell."*
2. **2.3.1 Recognize routes of transmission.** Case: BHW Jo holds three cards — dirty hands, a used object, a closed room with someone coughing. Ask: *"How could a germ travel here — without naming anyone's illness?"* Run the transmission card sort (eight cards). Link: *"Which controls block these routes?"*
3. **2.3.2 Choose controls at several levels.** Case: a coughing person in a crowded BHS queue, window closed, masks in a box, no clear process. Ask: *"If we only hand out masks, what problems remain?"* Run the layered control map (source, environment, work practice, PPE, each with an owner). Link: hand hygiene is a work practice everyone owns.
4. **2.3.3 Demonstrate hand hygiene.** Case: BHW Lea's hands are visibly dirty before simulated care. Ask: *"Which method first — and why?"* Model once, then run the return demonstration with both cards (visibly dirty, not visibly dirty).
5. **2.3.4 Demonstrate cough etiquette.** Case: Mang Lito is about to sneeze in the waiting area with no tissue. Ask: *"How does Rina help him without embarrassing him?"* Run the cough-etiquette demonstration — mime only, never cough toward a partner.
6. **2.3.5 Prevent diarrhea through safer surroundings.** Case: in Mila's home, raw chicken sits beside cooked food and a cup is dipped into uncovered water. Ask: *"'The water looks clear.' What do we say?"* Run the household review; the BHW asks permission first.
7. **2.3.6 Find BHS IPC policies.** Case: Nilo's IPC folder has old pages and no exposure contact. Ask: *"He found the sterilization policy. May he now sterilize equipment?"* Run the policy and training inventory. Link: a policy works only if someone checks it.
8. **2.3.7 Monitor and improve IPC practice.** Case: no soap at the station, but the attendance sheet is complete. Ask: *"'Everyone attended, so we are fine.' Is that true?"* Run the improvement card with both follow-up outcomes.
9. **Close and retrieve (last 10 minutes of 2.3.7).** Go round: each person names one route, one control and one person they will report to. Hand out the job aid and explain the transfer task.

For timing within each lesson, answer keys and scripts, use the lesson guide.

## Named misconception

**"If I wear a mask and use alcohol, I'm protected."**

People hold this for good reason: masks and alcohol were the loudest messages of recent years, and the reference manual opens the control topic with them. They do help. Correct it in 2.3.2: have the group fill all four rows of the control map for the crowded queue. When only the PPE row is full, ask *"What is still unsafe?"* Let them find the closed window, the missing reporting route and waiting area. In 2.3.3, add: handrub is not for visibly dirty hands. Correct the idea, never the person.

## Discussion prompts

- *"When someone coughs in the queue at your BHS, who do you tell now? Is that written anywhere?"*
- *"Where is the handwashing station in your BHS? What stops people from using it?"*
- *"How do you raise uncovered water or unwashed hands with a household without shaming them?"*
- *"Have you ever been asked to do a task you were not trained for? What did you say?"*
- *"What is the difference between 'we reported it' and 'it was fixed'? How would you know?"*

## Observing competence

- The Competency tab has **one indicator per lesson objective** (seven). Each activity card lists the objectives it serves.
- **Who observes:** staff only — the facilitator, midwife or supervisor. Peer observers in the triad give practice feedback; that is not a rating.
- **Rounds:** each triad rotates so every participant is learner once per activity. Rate **Ready**, **Needs practice** or **Not yet**, recording observed behaviour only (*"missed left thumb"*, not *"careless"*).
- **Needs practice:** give one specific retry action and re-observe in the same lesson's feedback/retry time.
- **Not yet:** name the one behaviour to change, give guided practice, and re-observe on day two or during the transfer task.
- Anyone not observed stays unrated; arrange another opportunity.
- A correct self-check, quiz score or attendance is **not** evidence of the skill. Only staff-observed performance is rated.

## Where this connects

- **Chapter 1, Modules 3 (Policies at the BHS) and 8 (Occupational Safety and Health)** — policies, controls and worker protection.
- **Chapter 1, Module 6 (Effective Communication)** — respectful reporting and asking a household's permission.
- **2.2 Quality service** — safe care is one of the quality dimensions, and tidy stations support hand hygiene.
- **2.4 First aid and referral** — ask participants to use the 2.3.3 hand-hygiene steps at every first-aid station.
- **Job aid:** *Infection prevention and control*, one section per lesson, local contacts filled in.
- **Transfer task:** within 2–4 weeks, the supervisor observes each BHW's hand hygiene once at the BHS using the job-aid checklist. The supervisor uses the same indicator: observe, feedback, retry. Recall checks are planned for days 2, 14 and 60 (no reminders are scheduled).
