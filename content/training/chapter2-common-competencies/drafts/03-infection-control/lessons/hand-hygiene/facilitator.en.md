## [purpose] Purpose

120 minutes: opening 5; model 10; guided practice 80; feedback/retry 10; debrief 5; next step 10. Rotate learner, partner and observer. Use every worksheet row and repeat missed actions.

In a return demonstration, choose the appropriate method and show complete cleaning of both hands, including correct drying.

## [time-materials] Time and materials

The seven IPC sessions total 600 minutes. This adapted split includes policy and improvement practice. Use clean props only. Hand hygiene needs running water, soap, single-use towels, suitable handrub and a timer. No live sharps, body fluids, hot-water or chemical demonstrations. Print cards, worksheet, observer sheet and job aid. Paper and spoken instructions can replace slides and internet.

## [prepare] Prepare

Confirm the local contact and reporting channel. Prepare one set per triad. Use fictional details and safe props. For 30 participants, use 10 triads; plan enough staff or later rounds to observe everyone.

## [opening] Opening

BHW Lea prepares for simulated care. Her hands are visibly dirty. Soap, water, single-use towels and alcohol-based handrub are available. A quick rinse is insufficient. First choose an appropriate method.

## [steps] Steps

Read the case. Take the first check before revealing feedback. Model the worked example. Run the task using the worksheet. Give one strength and one retry action, then take the second check. Ask: What evidence supports the choice? Who decides next?

## [expected-answers] Expected response

Rinse and dry with a single-use towel. Use the towel to close a manual tap. The complete healthcare wash takes 40–60 seconds. When handrub is appropriate and hands are not visibly dirty, apply enough to cover all surfaces. Rub the same surfaces until dry: 20–30 seconds for the complete handrub. Do not rinse or wipe it off.

## [misconception] Common error

Uses handrub on visibly dirty hands, misses essential surfaces or wipes off wet handrub.

## [practice] Activity you can run

Choose a method for two cards: visibly dirty and not visibly dirty. Demonstrate handwashing and handrub to the supervisor. Suggested activity, suitable for observation. Alone: complete a fictional worksheet and arrange staff observation later. Do not turn participation into an automatic competence rating.

## [answer-key] Check explanations

1: Visible dirt calls for soap and water. / 2: Soap, all-surface rubbing and drying are missing. / 3: Complete washing and appropriate drying.

1: Let hands dry while completing rubbing across all surfaces. / 2: Handrub should not be wiped off; hands may be recontaminated. / 3: Rinsing is not part of the handrub procedure.

## [observe] Observe

Appropriate method; all surfaces of both hands; complete timing and drying; no recontamination.

Ready: Completes both demonstrations and every checklist step without prompting.

Needs practice: Chooses the correct method but misses a surface or step; corrects it in a repeat demonstration.

Not yet: Uses handrub on visibly dirty hands, misses essential surfaces or wipes off wet handrub.

## [support] Support and debrief

Allow spoken or written work in the learner’s preferred language. Ask what helped and what to change. Record only observed behavior; peer feedback is practice support, not a staff rating. Leave unobserved work unrated and arrange another opportunity.

## [sources-review] Local resources

Use the job aid, blank worksheet and locally confirmed contact list. Ask the supervisor or organizer to confirm the reporting process. Keep personal resident details out of practice records.
