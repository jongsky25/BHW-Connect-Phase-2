## [scene] The situation

BHW Jo has three cards: contaminated hands, a used object and an enclosed room with someone coughing. Nobody’s illness is known. Infection involves germs entering and multiplying in the body. Symptoms may not be obvious. Identify a possible route, rather than naming a disease.

## [action] What to do and say

Direct contact involves transfer through direct touch. Indirect contact involves a contaminated object or hands. Infectious respiratory particles may be inhaled or land directly on a nearby person’s eyes, nose or mouth. A touch or cough alone does not prove infection.

## [example] Worked example

Other cards show fecal contamination of food or water: fecal-oral spread; an infected mosquito bite: vector-borne spread; a contaminated needle: possible blood exposure. Some infections pass to a baby during pregnancy, birth or breastfeeding. Ordinary social contact alone does not explain these routes. Match each card with an appropriate preventive action.

## [scope] Scope and support

A germ may spread by more than one route. Do not diagnose or label people from a card or appearance. Apply standard precautions to all patients. Designated clinical staff decide testing, treatment and additional precautions. Do not handle needles during practice.
