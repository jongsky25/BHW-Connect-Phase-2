## [purpose] Purpose

120 minutes: opening 5; model 10; guided practice 80; feedback/retry 10; debrief 5; next step 10. Rotate learner, partner and observer. Use every worksheet row and repeat missed actions.

Match eight fictional exposures to possible transmission routes and propose a control without diagnosing anyone.

## [time-materials] Time and materials

The seven IPC sessions total 600 minutes. This adapted split includes policy and improvement practice. Use clean props only. Hand hygiene needs running water, soap, single-use towels, suitable handrub and a timer. No live sharps, body fluids, hot-water or chemical demonstrations. Print cards, worksheet, observer sheet and job aid. Paper and spoken instructions can replace slides and internet.

## [prepare] Prepare

Confirm the local contact and reporting channel. Prepare one set per triad. Use fictional details and safe props. For 30 participants, use 10 triads; plan enough staff or later rounds to observe everyone.

## [opening] Opening

BHW Jo has three cards: contaminated hands, a used object and an enclosed room with someone coughing. Nobody’s illness is known. Infection involves germs entering and multiplying in the body. Symptoms may not be obvious. Identify a possible route, rather than naming a disease.

## [steps] Steps

Read the case. Take the first check before revealing feedback. Model the worked example. Run the task using the worksheet. Give one strength and one retry action, then take the second check. Ask: What evidence supports the choice? Who decides next?

## [expected-answers] Expected response

Other cards show fecal contamination of food or water: fecal-oral spread; an infected mosquito bite: vector-borne spread; a contaminated needle: possible blood exposure. Some infections pass to a baby during pregnancy, birth or breastfeeding. Ordinary social contact alone does not explain these routes. Match each card with an appropriate preventive action.

## [misconception] Common error

Diagnoses from appearance or assumes every germ spreads in the same way.

## [practice] Activity you can run

Sort eight exposure cards. For each, state a possible route and one control or person to approach. Suggested activity, suitable for observation. Alone: complete a fictional worksheet and arrange staff observation later. Do not turn participation into an automatic competence rating.

## [answer-key] Check explanations

1: Pregnancy or birth is not involved in this scene. / 2: The possible transfer is through an object and hands. / 3: This card cannot establish a diagnosis.

1: This does not address possible inhalation. / 2: This guesses a diagnosis and discloses information inappropriately. / 3: Use the agreed arrangements for space, reporting and protection.

## [observe] Observe

Explains all eight cards, distinguishes exposure from diagnosis and links prevention to the route.

Ready: Matches all eight cards appropriately, without labels or diagnosis, and suggests suitable controls without prompting.

Needs practice: Most matches are appropriate, but contact and inhalation need clarification; corrects them after feedback.

Not yet: Diagnoses from appearance or assumes every germ spreads in the same way.

## [support] Support and debrief

Allow spoken or written work in the learner’s preferred language. Ask what helped and what to change. Record only observed behavior; peer feedback is practice support, not a staff rating. Leave unobserved work unrated and arrange another opportunity.

## [sources-review] Local resources

Use the job aid, blank worksheet and locally confirmed contact list. Ask the supervisor or organizer to confirm the reporting process. Keep personal resident details out of practice records.
