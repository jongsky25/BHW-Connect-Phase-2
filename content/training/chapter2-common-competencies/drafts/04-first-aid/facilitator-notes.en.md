Run two sessions: 2.4.1–2.4.9 (235 min) and 2.4.10–2.4.18 (245 min); breaks are extra. Manikin stations (2.4.7, 2.4.15) may move to when the trainer is present; keep 2.4.1 first and 2.4.18 last. For 30 participants, use 10 triads and enough staff or later rounds to observe everyone. App reading time is not face-to-face time, and completion is not certification.

## Before the session

- **Confirm:** emergency contact, poison-advice route, referral facility and transport, reporting channel, supervisor, and local kit list. Write them on the job aid.
- **Staff and equipment:** a qualified trainer, adult and infant manikins, and an AED trainer if available.
- **Print, one set per triad:** the *First aid and referral* job aid, and each lesson's participant cards, worksheet and observation sheet.
- **Props:** clean cloths and gloves (2.4.10), a simple prop for support in place (2.4.11), an empty labelled ORS sachet (2.4.4, dry, no mixing), a fictional product label (2.4.9), an old inventory sheet and sample items (2.4.17).
- **Scope limits:** no compressions or thrusts on a live person; no water, chemicals or real medicines. Do not teach doses, a homemade ORS recipe, herbal wound care or a fixed spill distance. Beyond the lessons, say *"Confirm with the midwife, RHU or local protocol."*

## Delivery script

1. **Opening (first minutes of 2.4.1).** Write the F38 outcomes: *Assess the situation. Apply first aid techniques. Communicate details of the incident.* Ask: *"Have you ever been first at an emergency? What did you do first?"* Take two or three stories. Say: *"Every station ends the same way: call help, hand over."*
2. **Assess the situation and get help.** A rider has fallen beside moving traffic. Ask: *"What do you look for before stepping closer?"* Scene assessment station. Link: *"Same habit, now with a sick child."*
3. **Recognize childhood danger signs.** Niko cannot drink and is hard to wake. *"Which sign is enough to refer now?"* Childhood case cards.
4. **Respond to cough and breathing concerns.** Aya struggles to breathe; the family wants to wait. *"What will you describe, and who assesses her?"* Breathing case station.
5. **Respond to diarrhea and dehydration concerns.** Baby Mina is breastfeeding; there is an ORS sachet. *"Where do the mixing directions come from?"* ORS education station, dry.
6. **Respond to convulsions.** A neighbor wants to hold the child down and use a spoon. *"What do you tell the neighbor?"* Convulsion scenario.
7. **Recognize shock and loss of consciousness.** An adult collapses; a relative offers a sweet drink. *"What do you check, and what do you not give?"* Urgent response simulation.
8. **Prepare for supervised CPR/BLS practice.** Manikin, qualified trainer. *"Who goes for help and the AED?"* CPR training station.
9. **Respond safely to drowning.** Someone struggles in a flooded canal. *"Where do you stand?"* Drowning tabletop.
10. **Gather information in suspected poisoning.** A child may have swallowed a cleaner. *"What do you read from the label?"* Poisoning call rehearsal.
11. **Respond to wounds and bleeding.** A cut bleeds through the cloth. *"Do you remove the first cloth?"* Wound and bleeding station.
12. **Respond to suspected fractures.** She wants her deformed arm pulled straight. *"What do you support, and what do you not move?"* Injury support station.
13. **Respond to bites and stings.** Hives and breathing difficulty after a sting. *"Is this still a local sting?"* Bite and sting cards.
14. **Respond to snakebite.** Friends want a tight cord and a walk to the road. *"How does he reach the facility?"* Snakebite decision exercise.
15. **Respond to burns.** A stuck sleeve, and someone offers ice. *"What do you leave in place?"* Burn scenario station, dry.
16. **Respond to choking.** Adult and infant manikins. *"Can the person cough or speak?"* Choking skills station, qualified trainer.
17. **Arrange urgent chest-pain referral.** A neighbor offers tablets. *"Who decides about medicine?"* Emergency referral rehearsal.
18. **Check first-aid kit readiness.** Expired and unsealed items. *"Does having an item mean you may use it?"* Kit inspection.
19. **Hand over an incident.** A health worker arrives after a fall. *"What must they hear first?"* Integrated handover station.
20. **Closing (inside 2.4.18).** Each learner states, without notes, one case, the sign seen, who they called and one shortcut they refused.

For checks, answers and minute-by-minute timing, use each lesson guide.

## Named misconception

**"A good first aider fixes the problem on the spot."**

People hold this out of care: neighbors expect action, and old manuals and home remedies promised quick fixes (a spoon, ice, a tight cord, a sweet drink). Across all 18 lessons the safe response has one shape: keep the scene safe, call help early, do only what you are trained for, and hand over what you saw. Correct the case, not the person: *"That is what the neighbor in the case wanted too. What does the lesson say instead?"* Let the triad answer.

## Discussion prompts

- *"Which number or person do you actually call first? Has anyone checked it works?"*
- *"When a family asks to wait until tomorrow, what words help you insist on referral respectfully?"*
- *"Which shortcut is common where you live, and how do you decline it kindly?"*
- *"What is in the BHS kit today, and who replaces what runs out?"*
- *"In a handover, what do you usually forget: the time, what you saw, or what you already did?"*

## Observing competence

The Competency tab has one indicator per lesson; each activity card names the ones it covers.

1. Only staff rate (you, or the trainer at manikin stations). Peer feedback is practice, not a rating.
2. Record only observed behavior on the lesson's observation sheet: Ready, Needs practice or Not yet.
3. Run two rounds: try, one strength and one retry action, try again.
4. **Needs practice** (needed a prompt on referral or scope): give one retry action; re-observe this session or at the next supervised session.
5. **Not yet** (used the shortcut or did not call help): show the job aid step again; re-observe in a later round.
6. Leave unobserved indicators unrated. Self-checks and quiz scores are not evidence, and this station is not a CPR/BLS certificate.
