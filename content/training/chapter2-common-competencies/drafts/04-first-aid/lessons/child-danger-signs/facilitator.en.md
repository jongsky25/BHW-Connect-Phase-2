## [purpose] Purpose

In a sick-child case, the BHW names at least one danger sign, activates urgent referral and repeats the exact observation in a factual handover, without naming an illness.

## [time-materials] Time and materials

- **25 minutes** face-to-face (this lesson's share of the 480-minute subchapter plan): opening 3, model 4, practice 12, feedback 3, self-check 3. The Read takes about 5 minutes in the app.
- 2.4.2 participant cards, worksheet and observation sheet; job aid with the confirmed contact and referral facility.

## [prepare] Prepare

- Read the lesson and the job aid section *Recognize childhood danger signs*.
- Confirm with the midwife or RHU the local referral facility, transport and who receives a child urgently.
- Scope: only the lesson's signs — cannot drink, vomits everything, convulsions, hard to wake. No diagnosis, temperature cut-offs or medicines; nothing by mouth for a child who cannot safely swallow.

## [opening] Opening

*"A caregiver tells you: Niko, three, cannot drink, vomits everything and is very hard to wake. How many of these signs do you need before you refer?"*

## [steps] Steps

1. **The situation (Read and first slide).** *"What exactly did the caregiver say? Say it back word for word."*
2. **What to do and say (matching slide).** *"Which signs do you ask about, and which can you see yourself?"*
3. **Choose the first response.** Self-check 1 — let pairs answer before revealing.
4. **Worked example.** Model the handover: *"Niko, three years old. Cannot drink, vomits everything, hard to wake since this morning. We are bringing him now."* *"Did I name an illness?"*
5. **Scope and support.** *"The grandmother wants to give water first. What do you say?"*
6. **Apply it to the situation.** Self-check 2 — pairs first.
7. **On Monday, try this.** *"Who will you call first for a child like Niko?"*

## [expected-answers] Expected answers and how to respond

- **"Let's wait and see if he gets worse."** *"One danger sign is enough. How many does Niko have?"*
- **"It sounds like dehydration."** *"Maybe; the facility decides. What did you see?"*
- **"Give him ORS first."** *"He vomits everything and is hard to wake. What does the lesson say about giving by mouth?"*
- **"Refer now and say he is hard to wake."** Confirm; ask for the handover sentence.

## [misconception] Common misconception

**"Refer only when several signs appear."** Families fear a wasted trip, and BHWs do not want to alarm them. But waiting for every sign delays care. Correct the case: *"The lesson says any one danger sign. Which one would you report first?"*

## [practice] Practice activity

Childhood case cards (*Sick-child case stations*). Triads: learner as BHW, partner as caregiver, observer. Two short rounds; the caregiver asks to wait until tomorrow. One strength and one retry action between rounds, then rotate. The observer records the sign named, the referral step and the handover words. Fictional cases only. Solo: do the worksheet aloud; staff observe later.

## [answer-key] Self-check answer key

1. **"…What comes first?"** **"Arrange urgent facility assessment for Niko."** — one sign is enough. "Watch him at home…": waiting delays care. "Give him small sips…": nothing by mouth when swallowing is unsafe.
2. **"…What should the handover say?"** **"He cannot drink, vomits everything and is hard to wake."** — the exact signs. "…severe dehydration": an illness label. "Only that he vomited…": hides the urgency.

## [observe] What to observe

Indicator: Identify at least one danger sign, activate urgent referral and repeat the observation in a factual handover.

- **Kaya na:** Names Niko's danger signs, refers urgently even when the caregiver asks to wait, and hands over the exact signs without an illness label, without prompting.
- **Kailangan pa ng practice:** Refers urgently but needs a prompt to state the exact signs in the handover or to hold firm when asked to wait.
- **Hindi pa:** Agrees to wait for more signs, names an illness instead of the signs, or offers the child something by mouth.

Listen for the exact observation in the handover, not a label. A correct self-check is not evidence of the skill.

## [support] If a BHW needs more support

- **Kailangan pa:** replay only the caregiver's request to wait; retry with a new child.
- **Hindi pa:** read the job aid together, circle one danger sign, retry the handover with a Kaya na partner.
- Re-observe this session or next, new case. Peer feedback is practice, not a rating.
