## [scene] The situation

At a fictional BHS, Nena asks about the schedule. In the first encounter, BHW Mila reads an old message. A box blocks the walkway. Nobody asks when Nena can return. The conversation is fast, but is it good service?

## [action] What to do and say

Check three dimensions. Effective: confirm information against current guidance or with the designated staff. Safe: notice potential harm and seek appropriate help. People-centered: ask about needs and preferences. Speed or courtesy alone does not make service high quality.

## [check-start] Choose the first response

Choose an answer first. Then read the explanation for each option.

## [example] Worked example

In the second encounter, Mila confirms the schedule with staff. She asks for the box to be removed from the walkway. She asks what time Nena can manage and explains the confirmed options. Information is reliable, the path is safer and Nena participates in the decision.

## [scope] Scope and support

The BHW can confirm information and report hazards. Do not diagnose or change a clinician’s advice. If unsure, say so and ask the designated staff. Listen to the resident’s preferences while staying within safe service boundaries.

## [check-transfer] Apply it to the situation

Return to the story. Choose an appropriate next step.
