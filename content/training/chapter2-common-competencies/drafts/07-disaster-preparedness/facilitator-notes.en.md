# Facilitator notes — Subchapter 2.7: Disaster preparedness and response

## Timing

**Source allocation: 2 hours shared with Subchapter 2.6 (Community Mobilization), F38**, inside the 12-hour first-aid competency. F42–43 give no separate minutes. The 60/60 split between 2.6 and 2.7 is an **authoring proposal**, not source hours.

| Part | Lesson | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| 2.7.1 | Map hazards and capacities | 10 |
| 2.7.2 | Confirm evacuation and warning arrangements | 10 |
| 2.7.3 | Prepare a go-bag or e-balde | 10 |
| 2.7.4 | Coordinate the BHW/BHERT role | 10 |
| 2.7.5 | Connect people to essential services | 10 |
| 2.7.6 | Practise coordinated disaster response | 10 |
| **Total** | | **60** |

Each station: opening 1, model 1, triad practice 4, feedback and retry 2, debrief 1, next step 1. Run all six together, or split at a break (2.7.1–2.7.3 "before", 2.7.4–2.7.6 "during and after"). Breaks, the opening and the closing are extra; never take them from practice minutes. For 30 participants use 10 triads. In 60 minutes each person is the learner in only about two stations, so plan extra supervised rounds and enough staff to observe everyone. App reading time is not face-to-face time, and completion is not certification.

## Before the session

- **Confirm local facts** on one sheet (use them in debrief only): current official warning source and issue time; who issues evacuation orders; route and accessible shelter; barangay DRRM focal person and backup; current response team, lead and BHW role; approved reporting channel; referral contacts for medical/public health, nutrition, WASH and MHPSS; who handles medicine continuity and responder relief.
- **Print one set per triad** for 2.7.1–2.7.6: participant cards ("Practice: [lesson title]"), worksheet and observation sheet. Print the job aid *Disaster preparedness and response* for everyone.
- **Props:** a hand-drawn fictional map of Barangay Maligaya (river, riverside homes, school marked "not confirmed"); a mock advisory labelled PRACTICE with source and time; an old "directory"; a bag or bucket with item picture cards (no real medicines); an old fictional roster; four need cards.
- **Staffing:** one facilitator per 2–3 stations plus staff observers. F42 suggests inviting the city/municipal DRRM officer, local government operations officer and social welfare officer.
- **Do not teach here:** an official hazard map or evacuation order; fixed BHERT membership, contact tracing or isolation duties; prescribing or dispensing, setting rations, declaring water safe, or therapy; fixed 24-hour targets. Psychological first aid is calm, consent-based support and referral. First-aid techniques belong to 2.4.

## Delivery script

1. **Open (extra time).** F42 frames this as an orientation on the BHW's role in DRRM. Ask: *"Think of the last flood or typhoon. What did people ask you, and where did your answer come from?"* No resident names. Say: *"Every case today is fictional. The skill is knowing what is confirmed and who to hand it to."*
2. **2.7.1 Map hazards and capacities.** Riverside homes flood; one household needs help; the school is not confirmed as shelter. Ask: *"What do we know, and what must someone confirm?"* Run the **risk map exercise**. Link: *"The map shows who needs help. What do we tell them?"*
3. **2.7.2 Confirm evacuation and warning arrangements.** Nena's family asks when and where to go; the directory is old. Ask: *"Where did this advisory come from, and when?"* Run the **evacuation communication drill**. Link: *"They know when to go. What do they carry?"*
4. **2.7.3 Prepare a go-bag or e-balde.** Pio's family has an infant, regular medicine, a small budget. Ask: *"What would a copied list miss?"* Run the **go-bag demonstration** (F42 suggests BHWs demonstrate it). Link: *"When the water rises, which team do we work with?"*
5. **2.7.4 Coordinate the BHW/BHERT role.** An old BHERT roster may not be active. Ask: *"How do we know this is still the team in charge?"* Run the **response-team coordination** handover. Link: *"Where do the needs go next?"*
6. **2.7.5 Connect people to essential services.** At a shelter: food for a child, limited water, regular medicine, a distressed person. Ask: *"Which service takes each need, and how will we know it was received?"* Run the **EHSP sorting exercise** (F42's four packages).
7. **2.7.6 Practise coordinated disaster response.** Flood plus advisory; family needs help leaving, medicine low, water uncertain, volunteer exhausted. Ask: *"What comes first, and who takes each item?"* Run the **disaster tabletop**. Make sure the volunteer gets relief.
8. **Close (extra time).** Ask each person: *"What will you confirm on Monday, and with whom?"* Point to each lesson's "On Monday" step and the job aid.

For station steps, checks and expected answers, use each lesson guide.

## Named misconception

**"In an emergency any information is better than none — give them the hotline and the evacuation centre we used last time."**

This comes from care. BHWs are asked first and want to answer fast, and old source slides list hotlines and team members as if they never change.

Do not correct the person; return to Nena's case: *"Her directory is old. What if the number is wrong, or the route is flooded?"* Use the lesson feedback: old information may be wrong and can delay help. Then show the worked example: check the advisory date, call the official contact, say what is confirmed and what is still waiting. *"I will confirm and come back to you"* is part of the skill.

## Discussion prompts

- *"When a warning arrives by group chat, how do you check its source and time?"*
- *"Who in your barangay decides that people must evacuate?"*
- *"Which households might a standard go-bag list not fit?"* (Needs only, no names.)
- *"What is the least a handover must say so the next team can act?"*
- *"Who looks after the BHW when the BHW is exhausted or also affected?"*

## Observing competence

The Competency tab has one indicator per lesson objective (six). Each activity card lists the indicators it covers.

1. **Staff observe**: the facilitator, a trained staff observer or an invited DRRM/health officer. Peer feedback on the observer card is practice, not a rating.
2. **Rounds:** each participant is observed as learner in about two stations; schedule further supervised rounds for the rest.
3. **Record** only what you saw and heard, with a learner code. Leave anything unobserved unrated.
4. **Rate** Kaya na / Kailangan pa ng practice / Hindi pa (Ready / Needs practice / Not yet).
5. **Needs practice:** give one strength and one retry action; retry the station now if time allows, or at the next supervised practice.
6. **Not yet:** re-model the worked example, give a fresh fictional case, and re-observe next session or during the transfer task.

A correct app check or quiz score is not evidence of the skill.
