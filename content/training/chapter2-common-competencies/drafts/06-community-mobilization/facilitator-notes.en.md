# Facilitator notes — Subchapter 2.6: Community mobilization

## Timing

The source gives community mobilization **no hours of its own**. It shares **2 hours** with Subchapter 2.7 (Disaster preparedness) inside the 12-hour first-aid common competency (F38). The **60-minute** share below is an authoring proposal; the other 60 minutes stay with DRRM.

| Part | Lesson | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| 2.6.1 | Identify the issue with the community | 15 |
| 2.6.2 | Invite people and address barriers | 15 |
| 2.6.3 | Build trust and a core group | 15 |
| 2.6.4 | Agree actions and review results | 15 |
| | **Total** | **60** |

Every 15 minutes runs the same way: opening 1, model 2, triad practice 6, feedback and retry 3, debrief 2, next step 1. Run all four in one session, or as two 30-minute blocks. Breaks are extra. For 30 participants, use 10 triads and bring enough staff, or plan later rounds, to observe everyone. App reading time is not the face-to-face time. This hour is an introduction, not certification.

## Before the session

- **Confirm locally:** the organizer or supervisor who reviews community plans, the authorized reporting channel, an accessible meeting place, a confirmed resource contact, and what participation support (if any) local policy allows.
- **Print one set per triad, all fictional:** participant cards and worksheets *2.6.1 Community issue card*, *2.6.2 Invitation map*, *2.6.3 Short community meeting*, *2.6.4 Action plan and review*; the four observation sheets (staff keep these); and the *Community mobilization* job aid, one per participant.
- **Props:** manila paper, markers, name tags for Ina, the leader, Ruben, Sari and Eva. No slides or internet needed.
- **Staff:** one facilitator to model the worked examples, plus observers. Only staff rate.
- **Do not teach here:** real residents or reasons for non-attendance; promises of money, rewards, transport or services; attendance as a condition for services; any health or clinical advice. Do not present the issue card or action plan as official forms. They are training tools.

## Delivery script

1. **Open the subchapter (first opening minute).** F41 reminds us that organizing is one of the BHW's roles in health promotion. Ask: *"Think of a time you tried to get your purok to act together. What worked? What did not?"* Take two answers. Say: *"Today: name the issue with people, invite them, meet, then act and check."*
2. **Identify the issue with the community (2.6.1).** Case: three residents of fictional Purok Maligaya learned of a BHS schedule change too late; a leader says *"They just refuse to follow."* Ask: *"What do we actually know, and whose voice is missing?"* Model, then run the **Community issue card**. Link: *"Now we know who is missing. How do we reach them?"*
3. **Invite people and address barriers (2.6.2).** Case: Ruben works days, Sari has no phone, Eva is nervous in big groups. Ask: *"Sari has no phone. What should Ina do first?"* Run the **Invitation map**. Link: *"They came. Now the meeting must work for everyone."*
4. **Build trust and a core group (2.6.3).** Case: the existing group's leader wants to control every update; Eva is quiet; Sari has an idea. Ask: *"Eva has said nothing. Does that mean she agrees?"* Run the **Short community meeting** role-play. This is the demonstration F41 recommends: model Ina's line first. Link: *"Willing people have roles. What will they do, and how will we know it helped?"*
5. **Agree actions and review results (2.6.4).** Case: someone calls it a success because many attended. Ask: *"Does a full meeting prove the update reached people in time?"* Run the **Action plan and review**. Every plan needs a result measure and a respectful response to a missed task.
6. **Close (2.6.4 debrief and next-step minutes).** Ask each triad: *"Which step would you skip in a hurry, and what would go wrong?"* Hand out the job aid and explain the follow-up task.

For answer keys, expected responses and full steps, use the lesson guide for each lesson.

## Named misconception

**"If many people came to the meeting, the mobilization worked."**

This is an honest belief. Attendance is easy to count, it is often what gets reported, and a full room feels like success after a lot of inviting. The reference manual even notes that people come because of *who* calls the meeting, so turnout does show trust.

Do not tell anyone they are wrong. Return to the 2.6.4 case and ask: *"Did the people without phones get the update in time? How would we know?"* Let the group find that attendance cannot answer that. Then recall 2.6.2: attendance is only a start; meaningful participation matters. The real measure is the result residents experience, reviewed with them.

## Discussion prompts

- *"When you hear 'they just refuse to follow', what could you ask before you believe it?"*
- *"Who in your area usually misses announcements? What would help them hear sooner?"*
- *"Who do people in your purok trust to invite them? Is it always a barangay official?"*
- *"How can you give a quiet person a chance to speak without putting them on the spot?"*
- *"A volunteer did not finish what they promised. How do you follow up without shaming them?"*

## Observing competence

The Competency tab has four indicators, one per lesson objective (2.6.1–2.6.4). Each activity card names its indicator.

- **Staff observe and rate.** The triad observer's feedback is practice, not a rating.
- **Three rounds per lesson** so each triad member is the learner once. Rate only that round's learner.
- **Record only observed behaviour** as evidence on the observation sheet. If you did not see it, leave it unrated and arrange another chance.
- **Levels:** Ready / Needs practice / Not yet (Kaya na / Kailangan pa ng practice / Hindi pa), worded as on each sheet.
- **Needs practice:** give one next action (*"add the missing voices"*, *"confirm the resource first"*) and retry in the session if time allows.
- **Not yet:** name the behaviour calmly, model the step again, and re-observe during the 2–4-week follow-up.
- A correct self-check or quiz score shows recall, not skill. It is never evidence for a rating.
