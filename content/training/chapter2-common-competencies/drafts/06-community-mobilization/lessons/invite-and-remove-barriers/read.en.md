## [scene] The situation

Ina will convene a discussion about late schedule updates. A local leader is trusted by residents. Ruben works during the day. Sari has no phone. Eva feels nervous speaking in a large group. One chat invitation is not enough.

## [action] What to do and say

Identify affected residents, local leaders and others who can help. Ask who is trusted to invite them. Explore barriers involving time, location, language and speaking. Agree on options such as personal invitations, suitable timing or small conversations. Do not assume a leader represents everyone.

## [check-start] Choose the first response

Choose an answer first. Then read the explanation for each option.

## [example] Worked example

With the leader, Ina asks Ruben about timing. Sari chooses a personal invitation. Eva prefers a small conversation. They record who will invite each group and when to report back. Participation is voluntary, with another way to offer suggestions.

## [scope] Scope and support

Do not make attendance a condition for services. Do not promise payment or rewards to recruit participants. Clarify practical participation support with the organizer under local policy. Respect refusal and do not expose someone’s personal reason.

Return to the story. Choose an appropriate next step.
