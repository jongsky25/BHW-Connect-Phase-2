## [scene] The situation

In fictional Purok Maligaya, three residents say they learned about a BHS schedule change too late. A leader says, “They just refuse to follow.” BHW Ina has not heard from people without phones or those working during the day. What is the actual issue?

## [action] What to do and say

Hear affected people before choosing a solution. Separate reports from assumptions. Ask whose voice is missing. Together, state what is happening, who is affected and what evidence exists. Mark what remains unknown. Community members share decisions rather than simply receiving instructions.

## [check-start] Choose the first response

Choose an answer first. Then read the explanation for each option.

## [example] Worked example

The group writes: “Three residents report learning the new time late. We do not yet know how people without phones receive updates.” They will ask affected residents which channels they can use. They do not label people who missed the visit as lazy.

## [scope] Scope and support

Community mobilization involves identifying needs, acting and reviewing results together. People do not need to disclose personal circumstances publicly. Use combined, non-identifying information. For sensitive concerns, ask whether a small, private conversation is more appropriate.

## [check-transfer] Apply it to the situation

Return to the story. Choose an appropriate next step.
