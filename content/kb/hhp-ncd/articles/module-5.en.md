# Explaining results and getting the referral completed

A screening programme is judged by how many referred people actually reach the facility, not by how many were screened. That number is decided in the few minutes after you read out the result.

## Say the result plainly

Give the number, say in one sentence whether it is above or below the level that needs checking, and name the next step. "Ang presyon ninyo ngayon ay 158/96. Mas mataas ito sa dapat, kaya kailangan itong kumpirmahin sa health center."

Avoid systolic, diastolic, hyperglycemia, cardiovascular risk, and any word you would not use with a neighbour. Speak the language the household actually uses — in Iloilo and Guimaras that is usually Hiligaynon. Never say "may altapresyon kayo": the reading is high today and needs confirming, which is both true and less frightening.

When the result is normal, say so, praise what they already do well, and be honest that normal today is not normal forever. Avoid "wala kayong sakit", which people hear as permission to stop being checked.

Then check understanding by asking them to say back what they will do next — where, when, and what to bring. Do not ask "naintindihan ninyo ba?"; almost everyone says yes.

## Talking so people can change

Motivational interviewing is a way of talking that helps a person find their own reasons to act. In practice it means:

- **Ask permission before advising.** "Pwede ko po bang ibahagi ang isang bagay tungkol sa asin?" Advice that is invited is heard; advice that is announced is resisted.
- **Ask open questions.** "Ano ang alam ninyo tungkol sa altapresyon?" "Ano ang mahirap sa pag-inom ng gamot araw-araw?"
- **Reflect before you add.** Say back what you heard before offering anything of your own.
- **Agree one small step, chosen by them.** "Hindi na ako maglalagay ng patis sa kanin" beats "bawasan ang asin", and it gives you something specific to follow up.

When someone refuses, do not argue — arguing hardens the position. Acknowledge it, get curious about the real barrier, and leave the door open with a specific date.

## The four things a referral must include

Where to go, when to go, who to look for, and what to bring — the slip, current medicines, and their health record or PhilHealth details. Write the date on the slip and say it aloud. "Pumunta kayo sa health center" is not an instruction anyone acts on.

Then set the follow-up before you leave: a specific date, written on both copies, and say what you will check when you return.

## Common objections, honestly answered

- *"I feel fine."* Most people with raised blood pressure or blood sugar feel nothing — that is exactly why it is called silent, and why checking matters.
- *"Maintenance medicine damages the kidneys / is addictive."* These medicines are not addictive, and uncontrolled hypertension and diabetes are themselves a leading cause of kidney damage. Questions about their specific medicine belong to the doctor, and you will help them get that conversation.
- *"Herbal is enough."* Do not dismiss it and do not endorse it as a replacement. No herbal preparation tells you what the blood pressure or blood sugar is, and the doctor needs to know what they are taking.
- *"No money", "too far".* Find out what is actually available — free consultation and first-line medicines in many LGUs, outreach days, transport support — and never promise what you have not confirmed.

Never promise a cure, a permanent stock of medicines, a zero cost, or safety. Promising what you cannot control is the fastest way to lose the trust that makes every future visit possible.
