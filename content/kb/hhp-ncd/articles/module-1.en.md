# Where community screening fits in the HHP+ care pathway

Healthy Hearts Plus is the Department of Health programme, supported by WHO, that builds hypertension and diabetes care into routine primary health services. It began as Healthy Hearts Philippines in District 1 of Iloilo Province, working through seven rural health units, and has since scaled across Western Visayas while adding integrated diabetes care. Community screening by Barangay Health Workers is the entry point of that pathway — the step that finds the people the health system would otherwise never meet.

## The pathway, step by step

1. **Risk assessment in the community.** Using the PhilPEN risk assessment form and the NCD Flipchart, you collect the person's history, behavioural risk factors and symptoms.
2. **Screening measurement.** Blood pressure, and capillary blood glucose where your LGU authorises it and the assessment indicates it.
3. **Explaining the result.** In plain language, in the language the household actually speaks, with the next step named.
4. **Referral.** To the barangay health station or rural health unit, with the readings written on the slip.
5. **Confirmation and diagnosis.** At the facility, by a physician, using repeat measurements and laboratory tests.
6. **Treatment.** Started and adjusted at the facility.
7. **Adherence support and follow-up.** Back in the barangay, by you.

Documentation runs through every one of those steps, and the chain is only as strong as its records.

## Screening is not diagnosis

This is the line the whole module exists to draw. Screening identifies people who may be at risk and need further checking. Diagnosis is the decision that a person has the disease, and it belongs to the physician at the facility. Formal cardiovascular risk estimation and any decision about medicines are facility-based responsibilities under PhilPEN.

What that means in practice:

- Do not tell anyone they have hypertension or diabetes on the strength of a community reading.
- Do not start, stop, change or advise on any medicine.
- Do not reassure someone that a high reading is nothing, however well they look.
- Do record everything accurately, refer according to the protocol, and follow up.

## Why this work matters

Most people with raised blood pressure or blood sugar feel nothing at all. Left undetected, those conditions cause stroke, heart attack, kidney failure and blindness years later. Community screening is the only mechanism that reaches them early, and in Western Visayas it has already been shown to move blood pressure control rates well above the national baseline.

## Not a new task, a standardised one

Community screening — including blood glucose monitoring taught with demonstration and return demonstration — is already treated as a BHW competency by the regional office and appears in the BHW skills lab. What this training package adds is standardisation: one competency framework, one method, and an objective way to assess it, so that a person screened in one barangay gets the same quality of assessment as a person screened in the next.
